Read this page before you change the `color-correction` pass, its shader, or the matrices it builds on the CPU. What
the pass computes is specified under [Color correction](/docs/reference/rendering#color-correction) in the rendering
reference.

## How the pass is built

The pass is one tile pass, `color-correction/correct.besl`, and the CPU work in `render_passes/color_correction.rs`.

- **CPU matrices.** Exposure and white balance are linear, so `color_correction_shader_data` folds them, with the
  conversion to ACEScg, into one 3 × 3 matrix. The Planckian white, the CAT16 adaptation, and the grey luminance
  normalization run once in double precision at setup. The shader pays one matrix multiply for all three.
- **CPU split direction.** `split_direction` takes the Oklab color at mid grey's lightness with the split hue to
  ACEScct offsets once, with their luminance-weighted mean removed.
- **Shader.** The shader encodes ACEScct, moves the lightness (the L of hue, saturation, and lightness) through the
  shadows and highlights bumps and the contrast S-curve, adds the split tone's offsets, scales the channels' offsets
  around the saturation pivot, decodes, and converts back to linear sRGB with the second matrix.

## Why it works in ACEScct

In a logarithmic encoding, a channel's offset from the lightness is a ratio of linear light. Curving only the
lightness and keeping the offsets keeps every pixel's hue and saturation, so contrast doesn't make colors more
saturated, as a per-channel curve does. Saturation that scales those offsets acts the same at every exposure.

Moving the saturation pivot to the largest channel makes saturation subtractive: the channel that carries the color
stays put and the others drop, like dye adding density. Grey has no offsets, so it never moves, whatever the pivot.

## Why the split tone works like printer lights

The split tone adds a per-channel offset in ACEScct, which is a per-channel exposure change in linear light, the way a
film printer's lights change density. Colorists warn that teal and orange looks cheap when it tints every tone, turns
skin orange, or tints the deepest shadows. So the pass:

- Weights the offset by smooth bumps that are 0, with a slope of 0, at black, at mid grey, and at 16. Blacks,
  midtones, where faces usually sit, and the brightest highlights stay neutral, and no tone shows an edge.
- Uses one direction for both halves, negated for the shadows, so the two tints are exact opposites and cancel on
  average.
- Removes the direction's luminance-weighted mean, so tinting a grey doesn't brighten or darken it.
- Fades the tint down with the pixel's channel spread, so already saturated colors, such as skin and products, keep
  their hue while the neutral surroundings carry the look.
- Runs before saturation, so saturation acts on the finished split, as a colorist adjusts saturation after the split.

## Rules a change must keep

- The default settings must leave the picture unchanged, and a white card under light of the set temperature must
  show as D65. D65 lies a little green of the Planckian locus, so `white` shifts the locus by the offset that takes
  6,504 K to D65. Adapting to the 6,504 K Planckian white instead turns every balanced white about 5 % magenta.
- `SRGB_TO_ACESCG` must match `linear_srgb_to_acescg` in `layouts/acescct.besl`, and the shader's black, pivot, and
  white constants must be the ACEScct values of 0, 0.18, and 65,504.
- The tone curve must not clip and must keep a ramp in order at every setting: contrast fixes black and white, the
  shadows bump fixes black and mid grey, and the highlights bump fixes mid grey and 64. The shadows scale stops at a
  slope of 0 at black and the highlights scale keeps the slope at or above 0.42.
- The pass stays a pure per-pixel tile pass with no neighbors, so it can share a tile with other per-pixel work.

## Tests

The tests in `color_correction.rs` run the shader in the BESL virtual machine with the matrices the pass builds. They
check that the defaults change nothing, that a stop doubles the light, that contrast keeps mid grey and doesn't clip
shadows, that shadows and highlights move their tones by the specified stops and keep their fixed points, that no tone
setting reverses a ramp, that the split tone tints shadows and highlights opposite ways without changing a grey's
brightness, spares saturated colors, and follows its balance, that saturation and density never tint grey, that dense saturation darkens while additive saturation
brightens, and that a white card under the set temperature shows as neutral while grey keeps its luminance.
