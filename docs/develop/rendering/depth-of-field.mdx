Read this page before you change the `depth-of-field` or `depth-of-field-reference` pass, their shaders in
`assets/rendering/depth-of-field`, or the lens fields on `Camera` and `Sink`. What the passes render is specified under
[Lens](/docs/reference/rendering#lens) and [Depth of field](/docs/reference/rendering#depth-of-field) in the rendering
reference.

## Why one continuous gather

The pass gathers every pixel's blur from one lattice of taps into one weighted mean. It doesn't split the scene into
depth layers and composite them: layers show seams where a surface crosses from one layer to the next, and the seams
move as the focus changes. With one gather, a pixel's weight changes smoothly with its depth, so no depth draws a line.

The tap lattice is fixed in pixels. Tiles decide only how far the walk goes, never where a tap lands, so a tile's edge
never shows either.

## How the pass is built

The CPU work is in `render_passes/depth_of_field.rs`. It turns the sink's lens into the circle of confusion's bias and
scale over reverse-Z device depth, which is affine in it, and records three stages, each inside its own GPU counter:

- **Prepare** (`prepare.besl`, 16 × 16 compute). Each pixel computes its blur diameter b once and writes color and b
  to the full-resolution source. Each tile reduces its widest reach with lane shuffles over rows of 16 lanes, then one
  workgroup atomic per row, and writes it to `tile_reaches`. The debug view is written here.
- **Downsample** (`downsample.besl`, 8 × 8 compute, three dispatches). Each level averages 2 × 2 texels of the one
  before, weighted by min(|b| / w, 1), so sharp texels don't bleed into the blur that later rings read.
- **Gather** (`gather.besl`, 16 × 16 compute). Each workgroup stages its tile and a 3-pixel halo, 22 × 22 texels, into
  workgroup memory, with color already multiplied by density. 49 lanes read the 7 × 7 neighboring tiles' reaches to
  find the search reach, the farthest any blur nearby can reach into this tile. Each pixel then walks the 37 inner taps
  from workgroup memory, nearest first, and stops at the search reach. The 7 outer rings of 16 taps read the averaged
  levels directly; a ring beyond the search reach is skipped whole.

The tap lattice is compiled into `gather.besl` as constant arrays: each inner tap's texel in the shared tile and its
distance, each ring's bounds, radius, and area, and each outer tap's offset. Every offset, distance, and weight factor
is a constant, so the compiler can fold them, and the shader reads no lattice from memory.

On an Apple M4 rendering Sponza at 1024 × 1024 at f/1.4, the pass takes 1.11 ms: 0.88 ms for the gather, 0.15 ms for
the downsample, and a prepare stage too short for its counter. Three changes brought the gather down from 2.13 ms:

- Lane shuffles and one atomic per 16 lanes, instead of 256 lanes contending on one workgroup atomic, saved 0.7 ms.
- Constant tap tables, instead of a lattice read from the parameters buffer, together with inner taps sorted by
  distance so the walk can stop early, and rings skipped beyond the search reach, saved 0.5 ms more.

## The reference

The `depth-of-field-reference` pass renders the lens's exact blur, slowly, to judge the gather against. While it's
enabled, `RenderPass::samples_lens` asks the renderer to move each sink's view over the aperture every frame:
`Sink::with_lens_sample` picks a point on the R2 sequence mapped to the disc, and `View::with_lens_offset` moves the
eye there and shears the projection so the focus plane stays put. The pass keeps the running mean of those frames in
three R32F images. The aperture is scaled by S / (S − F), so the spread of each depth matches the thin-lens circle of
confusion that the gather uses.

To compare the two, capture the model viewer once with each pass enabled and the other bypassed:

```sh
--render.pass.depth-of-field=bypassed --render.pass.depth-of-field-reference=enabled
```

## Rules a change must keep

- Keep one continuous gather. Don't add depth layers or per-tile tap sets; both bring back seams.
- Keep the tap tables in `gather.besl` in step with the rule they follow: inner taps are every pixel within 3.5 pixels,
  sorted by distance; ring bounds grow by 1.39 times from 3.5 pixels; each tap sits at the radius that halves its
  ring's area; each ring reads the widest level no wider than its taps' spacing. When you change the rule, recompute
  every table from it; the tests check them.
- Keep the last ring's outer bound beyond the widest reach, 32.5 pixels, which follows from `MAX_BLUR_DIAMETER`, 64
  pixels, in `depth_of_field.rs`. `blur_limits` holds `DepthOfFieldPassSettings::max_blur_diameter` to it, and the
  shader rolls every blur off below the largest diameter it's given, so a smaller largest diameter also bounds the
  search reach and skips the outer rings.
- Keep `blur_diameter` in `layouts/parameters.besl` equal to the reference's blur diameter. The blur bound tests run
  it in the BESL VM against that formula.
- Keep `MAX_TILES` in `depth_of_field.rs` equal to the length of `TileReaches` in the layout.
- Every lane in a workgroup reaches every barrier and every shuffle. A lane past the image's edge returns only after
  the last barrier.
- Keep the pixel's own tap in the gather. It covers at least half of itself, so the sum of weights is never 0.

## Tests

- `inner_taps_are_the_pixels_within_their_radius_nearest_first` and
  `outer_rings_cover_every_reach_with_even_areas` check the shader's tap tables against the lattice's rule.
- `blur_diameters_stay_within_their_bounds` runs `blur_diameter` in the BESL VM and checks it against the
  reference's formula with no bounds, a smallest diameter, and both; `blur_bounds_are_held_to_their_ranges` checks
  how the settings are held to their ranges.
- `depth_of_field_shaders_link` compiles the three shaders.
- The circle-of-confusion tests check the thin-lens formula, its sign, the pinhole, and the held focus distance.
- `lens_samples_spread_each_depth_over_its_circle_of_confusion` checks that the reference's moved views spread each
  depth by the same circle of confusion.
- `reference_besl_vm_moves_the_mean_by_the_frames_share` runs the reference shader in the BESL VM.
