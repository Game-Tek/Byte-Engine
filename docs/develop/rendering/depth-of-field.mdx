Read this page before you change the `depth-of-field` or `depth-of-field-reference` pass, their shaders in
`assets/rendering/depth-of-field`, or the lens fields on `Camera` and `Sink`. What the passes render is specified under
[Lens](/docs/reference/rendering#lens) and [Depth of field](/docs/reference/rendering#depth-of-field) in the rendering
reference.

## Why one continuous gather

The pass gathers every pixel's blur from one lattice of taps into one weighted mean. It doesn't split the scene into
depth layers and composite them: layers show seams where a surface crosses from one layer to the next, and the seams
move as the focus changes. With one gather, a pixel's weight changes smoothly with its depth, so no depth draws a line.

The tap lattice is fixed in pixels. Tiles decide only how far the walk goes, never where a tap lands, so a tile's edge
never shows either.

## How the pass is built

The CPU work is in `render_passes/depth_of_field.rs`. It turns the sink's lens into the circle of confusion's bias and
scale over reverse-Z device depth, which is affine in it, and records three stages, each inside its own GPU counter:

- **Prepare** (`prepare.besl`, 16 × 16 compute). Each pixel computes its blur diameter b once and writes color and b
  to the full-resolution source. Each tile reduces its widest reach with lane shuffles over rows of 16 lanes, then one
  workgroup atomic per row, and writes it to `tile_reaches`. The workgroup then builds the three averaged levels from
  the half-float values it wrote, in workgroup memory: a 16 × 16 tile holds whole 2 × 2 blocks of every level, down to
  2 × 2 texels of the last. Each level averages 2 × 2 texels of the one before, weighted by min(|b| / w, 1), so sharp
  texels don't bleed into the blur that later rings read. The debug view is written here.
- **Gather** (`gather.besl`, 16 × 16 compute). Each workgroup stages its tile and a 3-pixel halo, 22 × 22 texels, into
  workgroup memory as the source holds them, in half floats. 49 lanes read the 7 × 7 neighboring tiles' reaches to
  find the search reach, the farthest any blur nearby can reach into this tile. Each pixel then reads the 37 inner taps
  from workgroup memory in four groups by distance, nearest first, and skips the groups beyond the search reach. The
  7 outer rings of 16 taps read the averaged levels directly, turned and placed by blue noise, as
  [Hiding the lattice](#hiding-the-lattice) explains; a ring whose inner bound lies beyond the search reach is skipped
  whole. It writes the blur to `Depth of Field Gathered`, with the rings' share of the weight in alpha.
- **Filter** (`filter.besl`, 8 × 8 compute). Each pixel moves toward a 3 × 3 tent of its neighbors by its ring share,
  each neighbor weighted by its own, and writes the pass's output.

The tap lattice is compiled into `gather.besl`: each inner tap's texel in the shared tile and its distance are written
into its own statements, and each ring's bounds, radius, and area and each outer tap's offset are constant arrays
indexed by constants. Every offset, distance, and weight factor is a constant, so the compiler folds them, and the
shader reads no lattice from memory. Every tap is written out, with a group's or a ring's reads issued before any is
weighed: the GPU waits for every read in flight at each branch, so a loop that branches once a tap leaves each read's
latency exposed.

Before the 4K changes below, on an Apple M4 rendering Sponza at 1024 × 1024 at f/1.4 focused at 8 m, the pass took
about 1.3 ms: 0.65 to 0.9 ms for the gather, about 0.3 ms for the filter, about 0.2 ms for the levels, then a
separate downsample, and a prepare stage too short for its counter. Focused at 1.5 m, where nearly every pixel blurs
widely, it took about 3.4 ms. These come from the model
viewer run with `--unattended=60`, each variant interleaved with the others, and vary by about 0.3 ms between runs.
Before the noise and filter, the gather took 0.88 ms at 8 m. Three changes brought it down from 2.13 ms:

- Lane shuffles and one atomic per 16 lanes, instead of 256 lanes contending on one workgroup atomic, saved 0.7 ms.
- Constant tap tables, instead of a lattice read from the parameters buffer, together with inner taps sorted by
  distance so the walk can stop early, and rings skipped beyond the search reach, saved 0.5 ms more.

In the model viewer at 3840 × 2036, unpaced with `--unattended=1000`, the hall view at f/1.4 focused at 8 m blurs the
near arch around most of the frame, and the pass took 7.5 ms: 0.64 ms to prepare, 1.02 ms to downsample in three
dispatches, 4.84 ms to gather, and 0.98 ms to filter. It now takes 4.96 ms, and the frame 2.5 ms less:

- Writing the inner taps out in four groups, where a loop stopped at the first tap beyond the search reach, took the
  gather from 4.8 to 3.6 ms, and writing out each ring's 16 taps took it to 3.4 ms.
- Staging half-float texels, 8 bytes instead of a 16-byte color times density and a 4-byte blur, and recomputing each
  inner tap's density instead of staging it, took it to 3.1 ms. The inner taps' workgroup reads, not their math,
  were the limit. Reading the inner taps from the texture cache instead of staging them was slower, 3.7 ms.
- Building the averaged levels in the prepare workgroup, instead of three dispatches that read the source back, took
  the prepare from 0.64 to 0.83 ms and removed the 1.02 ms downsample.

The blur is unchanged: the BESL VM gives the same gather as before to within float rounding, 2.3e-7 relative, and
`prepare_averages_each_level_from_the_one_before` checks the levels against the averaging rule.

## Hiding the lattice

With every pixel reading the same ring offsets, a small bright point blurs into rings of dots instead of a filled
disc, and a disc grows in steps as each ring starts to cover it. So each pixel turns all rings by its own angle, up
to one tap's spacing, and moves each ring's taps to its own radius between the ring's bounds, spread evenly by area.
Every tap still stands for a sixteenth of its ring's area, so the blur is the same on average; the pattern becomes
fine grain.

The angle and radius come from the 64 × 64 blue-noise texture in `lookup_textures.rs`, read at the pixel modulo 64.
Blue noise puts the grain in the finest detail, which the filter's 3 × 3 tent removes. The noise doesn't change from
frame to frame: nothing accumulates frames, so moving noise would shimmer. The filter weighs by ring share because
only the rings leave grain: the inner taps read exact pixels, and a pixel that the rings didn't reach is sharp and
must stay so.


The `depth-of-field-reference` pass renders the lens's exact blur, slowly, to judge the gather against. While it's
enabled, `RenderPass::samples_lens` asks the renderer to move each sink's view over the aperture every frame:
`Sink::with_lens_sample` picks a point on the R2 sequence mapped to the disc, and `View::with_lens_offset` moves the
eye there and shears the projection so the focus plane stays put. The pass keeps the running mean of those frames in
three R32F images. The aperture is scaled by S / (S − F), so the spread of each depth matches the thin-lens circle of
confusion that the gather uses.

To compare the two, capture the model viewer once with each pass enabled and the other bypassed:

```sh
--render.pass.depth-of-field=bypassed --render.pass.depth-of-field-reference=enabled
```

Against the same gather on a fixed lattice, the noise and filter add about 0.2 ms at 8 m and 0.9 ms at 1.5 m. About
0.3 ms of it goes to the filter. The gather slows by up to 0.4 ms because it skips a ring only past its inner bound, and
it turns each tap's offset. Staging the filter's 3 × 3 reads in workgroup memory measured no faster than reading them
through the texture cache.

## Rules a change must keep

- Keep one continuous gather. Don't add depth layers or per-tile tap sets; both bring back seams.
- Keep the noise fixed over frames while nothing accumulates frames, and keep each ring's taps within the ring's
  bounds, each standing for a sixteenth of its area.
- Skip a ring by its inner bound, not its middle radius: a jittered tap can sit anywhere between the bounds.
- Keep the taps in `gather.besl` in step with the rule they follow: inner taps are every pixel within 3.5 pixels,
  sorted by distance, each group skipped only when its nearest tap lies beyond the search reach; ring bounds grow by 1.39 times from 3.5 pixels; each tap sits at the radius that halves its
  ring's area; each ring reads the widest level no wider than its taps' spacing. When you change the rule, recompute
  every table and tap from it; the tests check them.
- Keep every tap written out, with a group's or ring's reads before its weights. Folding them back into a loop over a
  table brings back the exposed read latency.
- Build each averaged level from the half-float values the level before stores, as the gather reads them.
- Keep the last ring's outer bound beyond the widest reach, 32.5 pixels, which follows from `MAX_BLUR_DIAMETER`, 64
  pixels, in `depth_of_field.rs`. `blur_limits` holds `DepthOfFieldPassSettings::max_blur_diameter` to it, and the
  shader rolls every blur off below the largest diameter it's given, so a smaller largest diameter also bounds the
  search reach and skips the outer rings.
- Keep `blur_diameter` in `layouts/parameters.besl` equal to the reference's blur diameter. The blur bound tests run
  it in the BESL VM against that formula.
- Keep `MAX_TILES` in `depth_of_field.rs` equal to the length of `TileReaches` in the layout.
- Every lane in a workgroup reaches every barrier and every shuffle. A lane past the image's edge returns only after
  the last barrier.
- Keep the pixel's own tap in the gather. It covers at least half of itself, so the sum of weights is never 0.

## Tests

- `inner_taps_are_the_pixels_within_their_radius_nearest_first` and
  `outer_rings_cover_every_reach_with_even_areas` check the shader's taps against the lattice's rule.
- `prepare_averages_each_level_from_the_one_before` runs the prepare's workgroups in the BESL VM over an image whose
  tiles and levels end partway, and checks each averaged level against the averaging rule over the half-float level
  before.
- `blur_diameters_stay_within_their_bounds` runs `blur_diameter` in the BESL VM and checks it against the
  reference's formula with no bounds, a smallest diameter, and both; `blur_bounds_are_held_to_their_ranges` checks
  how the settings are held to their ranges.
- `filter_smooths_only_what_the_rings_carried` runs the filter in the BESL VM: a flat blur stays flat, sharp pixels
  neither blur nor tint their neighbors, and grain fades by the tent's weights.
- `depth_of_field_shaders_link` compiles the three shaders.
- The circle-of-confusion tests check the thin-lens formula, its sign, the pinhole, and the held focus distance.
- `lens_samples_spread_each_depth_over_its_circle_of_confusion` checks that the reference's moved views spread each
  depth by the same circle of confusion.
- `reference_besl_vm_moves_the_mean_by_the_frames_share` runs the reference shader in the BESL VM.
