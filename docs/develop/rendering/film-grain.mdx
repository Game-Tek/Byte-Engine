Read this page before you change the `film-grain` pass, its shader, or the constants it computes on the CPU. What the
pass renders is specified under [Film grain](/docs/reference/rendering#film-grain) in the rendering reference.

## Where the model comes from

The grain model is adapted from [cinegrain2](https://github.com/mr-berndt/cinegrain2) by mr-berndt, as published
under the MIT license up to commit `fd9e8f3`. Its notice is in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`. Later versions of cinegrain2
use a copyleft license that the engine's MIT license can't take in, so don't read or port them; take new ideas from
published papers, such as Newson et al.'s stochastic film grain model, instead.

The model fits ten scanned film plates: white noise blurred by a narrow Gaussian, minus 0.95 of a Gaussian 8.4 times as
wide, applied to the display-encoded picture as a change of density. Plain blurred noise keeps coarse structure that
shows as blotches three to eight times stronger than on the scans, which the wide part removes.

## How the pass is built

The pass is one tile pass, `film-grain/apply.besl`, and the CPU work in `render_passes/film_grain.rs`.

- **CPU constants.** `film_grain_shader_data` computes everything that's the same for every pixel: both blurs'
  standard deviations and radii, the scale that brings the difference of blurs to unit variance, the amplitude with
  its downscale retention, the zone weights divided by the weight at mid grey, and the frame's noise seed.
- **One tile per workgroup.** Each 16 × 16 tile draws the noise its pixels reach into workgroup memory, with a ring of
  the narrow radius, and the 6 × 6 cells its pixels filter between, with a ring of the wide radius. Each cell is the
  mean of a 4 × 4 block of the same noise. The tile blurs both regions' rows, then the wide region's columns, then
  each pixel blurs its own narrow column, filters the wide cells bilinearly, and applies the grain.
- **No intermediate images.** No grain image reaches memory.

The pass is one pass because any pass in front of it costs more than the grain. On an Apple M4 at 1024 × 1024, a
version that built the wide part in separate passes, two compute dispatches or two quarter-resolution tile passes,
added 0.5 to 0.6 ms to the frame. The single tile pass adds about 0.25 to 0.35 ms, and the grain math alone measured
about 0.13 ms over a copy. The separate passes slowed the color-grading pass in front of them, which a pass's own timer
doesn't show, so compare the sum of both passes' timers, or the frame time, when you measure a change.

The wide region's cost grows with the grain size: about 3.5 noise draws per pixel for 35 mm grain at 1024 pixels
tall, and about 25 for 16 mm grain at 4K. Each cell's 16 draws run on one lane, so most lanes wait while 36 lanes
average the cells. Spreading those draws across lanes is the next place to save time.

## Rules a change must keep

- Keep the pass a single tile pass. Measure before you split it, as [How the pass is built](#how-the-pass-is-built)
  explains.
- Keep `MAX_NARROW_RADIUS` and `MAX_WIDE_RADIUS` in `film_grain.rs` in step with the workgroup arrays and strides in
  `apply.besl`, and `GRAIN_NOISE_MARGIN` in `layouts/grain.besl` at least 4 × (1 + the largest wide radius), so noise
  positions stay unsigned.
- Keep `WIDE_SHARE` in `film_grain.rs` equal to `GRAIN_WIDE_SHARE` in the layout, and `unit_variance_scale` in step
  with the shader's kernels. The grey-card test catches a mismatch.
- Every lane in a tile reaches every barrier. A lane past the image's edge returns only after the last barrier.
- Draw the narrow and wide parts from one noise field. The wide part removes the narrow part's coarse structure, which
  only works when both blur the same noise.

## Tests

- `grey_card_grain_has_unit_variance_density` runs the shader in the BESL VM on a 96 × 96 grey card with 16 mm grain,
  so both blurs span several tiles, and checks that the density change has mean 0 and standard deviation 1 in units of
  the amplitude.
- `default_zones_leave_white_clean` checks that white takes no grain with the default zones and mid grey does.
- `shorter_pictures_show_less_fine_grain` checks that shorter sinks keep less fine grain than coarse grain.
