Read this page before you change the `film-grain` pass, its shader, or the constants it computes on the CPU. What the
pass renders is specified under [Film grain](/docs/reference/rendering#film-grain) in the rendering reference.

## Where the model comes from

The grain model is adapted from [cinegrain2](https://github.com/mr-berndt/cinegrain2) by mr-berndt, as published
under the MIT license up to commit `fd9e8f3`. Its notice is in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`. Later versions of cinegrain2
use a copyleft license that the engine's MIT license can't take in, so don't read or port them; take new ideas from
published papers, such as Newson et al.'s stochastic film grain model, instead.

The model fits ten scanned film plates: white noise blurred by a narrow Gaussian, minus 0.95 of a Gaussian 8.4 times as
wide, applied to the display-encoded picture as a change of density. Plain blurred noise keeps coarse structure that
shows as blotches three to eight times stronger than on the scans, which the wide part removes.

## How the pass is built

The pass is one tile pass, `film_grain/apply.besl`, and the CPU work in `render_passes/film_grain.rs`.

- **CPU constants.** `film_grain_shader_data` computes everything that's the same for every pixel: both blurs'
  radii and their weight one tap from the center, the two scales that bring the difference of blurs to unit variance,
  the amplitude with its downscale retention, the zone weights divided by the weight at mid grey, and the frame's
  noise seed.
- **One tile per workgroup.** Each 16 × 16 tile builds the noise of the cells its pixels reach into workgroup memory,
  the tile's own and a ring of whole cells that holds the narrow radius, and the 6 × 6 cells its pixels filter
  between, with a ring of the wide radius. The tile blurs both regions' rows, then the wide region's columns, then
  each pixel blurs its own narrow column, filters the wide cells bilinearly, and applies the grain.
- **One draw per cell.** The wide blur needs the mean of every cell in its ring, and a mean of 16 hashed draws for
  each made the ring most of the pass's work. So a cell's mean is one draw, `grain_cell_mean`, and the lane that
  builds a narrow cell shifts its 16 pixel draws to have that mean. See [The noise](#the-noise).
- **Stepped Gaussian weights.** Each blur steps its weight from one tap to the next with two multiplications instead
  of an exponential per tap, as in "Incremental Computation of the Gaussian" (Turkowski, GPU Gems 3, chapter 40). The
  blurs keep unnormalized sums, and the CPU folds both kernels' totals into `narrow_scale` and `wide_scale`.
- **No intermediate images.** No grain image reaches memory.

The pass is one pass because any pass in front of it costs more than the grain. On an Apple M4 at 1024 × 1024, a
version that built the wide part in separate passes, two compute dispatches or two quarter-resolution tile passes,
added 0.5 to 0.6 ms to the frame. The single tile pass adds about 0.25 to 0.35 ms, and the grain math alone measured
about 0.13 ms over a copy. The separate passes slowed the color-grading pass in front of them, which a pass's own timer
doesn't show, so compare the sum of both passes' timers, or the frame time, when you measure a change.

The noise is most of the pass's work, and its cost grows with the grain size. For 35 mm grain at 4K a tile makes
676 draws: 576 for the 36 cells its narrow blur reaches, and one for each of the 100 cells of the wide region. With a
mean of 16 draws for each wide cell it made 1,924. On an Apple M4 at 3840 × 2036 the pass's timer fell from about
2.36 ms to about 1.45 ms. Each narrow cell's 16 draws run on one lane, so most lanes wait while up to 64 lanes build
the cells. Spreading those draws across lanes is the next place to save time.

## The noise

The noise is the engine's own construction, not part of cinegrain2, which blurs plain white noise. A pixel's noise is
its draw, minus the mean of its cell's 16 draws, plus a quarter of the cell's own draw:

- **What it keeps.** The noise of any pair of pixels is uncorrelated, in one cell or across cells, and every pixel's
  variance is 1/3, exactly as for plain draws. The grain is a weighted sum of noise, so its variance, its power spectrum, and the scale
  `unit_variance_scale` computes are the same as with plain draws.
- **What it changes.** The distribution of one pixel's noise: it's a sum of more draws than four, so it's closer to a
  Gaussian than a plain draw is. The blurs already bring the grain close to a Gaussian, so the grain's distribution
  moves less than the noise's.
- **Why it's exact for the wide part.** A cell's mean is the cell's own draw, scaled to the variance a mean of 16 draws
  has. The wide part still blurs the means of the noise the narrow part blurs, which is what cancels the narrow part's
  coarse structure.

## Rules a change must keep

- Keep the pass a single tile pass. Measure before you split it, as [How the pass is built](#how-the-pass-is-built)
  explains.
- Keep `MAX_NARROW_RADIUS` and `MAX_WIDE_RADIUS` in `film_grain.rs` in step with the workgroup arrays and strides in
  `apply.besl`, and `GRAIN_NOISE_MARGIN` in `layouts/grain.besl` at least 4 × (1 + the largest wide radius), so noise
  positions stay unsigned.
- Keep `kernel_sums` and `unit_variance_scale` in `film_grain.rs` in step with the shader's kernels: the shader
  doesn't normalize its blurs, so a tap the CPU doesn't count changes the grain's strength. The grey-card test catches
  a mismatch.
- Keep the noise white and its cell mean one draw: a pixel's noise is its draw, minus the mean of its cell's draws,
  plus `grain_cell_mean`. Cells are 4 × 4 pixels from the image's first pixel, so keep the tile size and
  `GRAIN_NOISE_MARGIN` whole numbers of cells.
- Every lane in a tile reaches every barrier. A lane past the image's edge returns only after the last barrier.
- Draw the narrow and wide parts from one noise field. The wide part removes the narrow part's coarse structure, which
  only works when both blur the same noise. An independent draw per cell, without the shift, breaks this.

## Tests

- `grey_card_grain_has_unit_variance_density` runs the shader in the BESL VM on a 96 × 96 grey card with 16 mm grain,
  so both blurs span several tiles, and checks that the density change has mean 0 and standard deviation 1 in units of
  the amplitude.
- `default_zones_leave_white_clean` checks that white takes no grain with the default zones and mid grey does.
- `shorter_pictures_show_less_fine_grain` checks that shorter sinks keep less fine grain than coarse grain.
