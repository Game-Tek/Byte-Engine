Read this page before you change how frames place entities between simulation
steps, the transform contract of `PipelineManager`, or the loop's display time.
What an application can rely on is specified under
[shown transforms](/docs/reference/rendering#shown-transforms) in the
rendering reference, and the display time under
[loops](/docs/reference/time#loops).

## One timeline for the whole frame

`ShownTransforms` is the only display-side reader of the world's transform
route. The application owns it and lends it to the renderer, which places the
cameras with it and hands it to every pipeline manager in `update` and
`prepare`. A manager moves what it draws from `changed` and places what it
starts drawing with `get`; it keeps no transform listener and no transform map
of its own.

Before this, every consumer mirrored the route: the visibility pipeline blended
two samples per renderable, the isometric sample app copied that scheme, and
cameras, particles, and the simple pipeline took the latest transform as it
came. A camera that followed a unit stepped at the simulation rate while the
unit moved smoothly, and particle emitters ran up to a step ahead of the mesh
they belonged to. With one timeline, everything a frame shows comes from the
same display time.

Simulation systems such as physics and navigation keep their own listeners.
They need the latest sample, not the transform a frame shows a step behind.

## Samples carry stamps, not step numbers

A sample is a transform read in a simulation step, stamped with that step's
end time. A handle's segment starts at its stamp less one step, from wherever
the previous segment stands at that time, and lasts as long as the time since
the handle's previous stamp.

Keying samples by step number only worked for sources that publish every step.
A source that publishes every fourth step, such as a character's task at 15 Hz under a
60 Hz physics step, was blended over one step and then held still for three.
Measuring the span from stamps lets each handle move at its own cadence, at any
phase, without the source stating it. The span is capped at 250 ms, because
after a rest the gap since the previous stamp says nothing about the cadence: a
handle that starts moving after a minute would otherwise take a minute to get
there. The next sample's segment starts from wherever the capped one reached,
so the handle catches up within one sample.

Starting each segment from the transform shown at its start, not from the
previous sample, keeps motion continuous when a sample arrives early or late.

Stamps are differences, so they never need a shared clock. A replicated source
could stamp samples with its own simulation time and keep its spans exact under
network jitter. The transform route can't carry such a stamp yet: entity
creation publishes the same message through the factory.

## Placements end motion

A transform read outside a step is a placement, such as frame code that moves a
character by root motion or a camera rig. It shows at once and resets the
stamp, so the next sample spans one step.

## Adopt every tick, resolve every frame

The loop calls `step` after each simulation step, `adopt` once per tick after
the frame callback, and `resolve` only for a tick that renders. Adopting every
tick keeps the routes from holding back their publishers while an on-demand
application draws nothing. Resolving only for rendered frames keeps `changed`
from growing across ticks that no manager sees.

## Rules a change must keep

- Read the transform route for display only through `ShownTransforms`. A
  pipeline manager never keeps its own transform listener or transform map.
- Adopt every tick, whether or not the tick renders.
- Keep the segment rules in the pure `Segment` and `Timeline` code, apart from
  draining listeners.
- Start a new segment from the transform shown at its start, never from the
  previous sample.
- In a manager, apply `changed` on every prepared frame, including frames that
  draw nothing, and place new entities with `get`.

The spec tests in `rendering/shown_transforms.rs` guard the reference rules:
blending a source that publishes every step, sources that publish every fourth
step at every phase, placements, the span limit after a rest, a second sample in
one step, and first updates and deletions.
