Read this page before you change the `taa` pass, `render_passes/taa.rs`, the `taa/` shaders, the velocity stage in
`pipelines/visibility/render_pass/velocity.rs`, jitter in `sink.rs` and `renderer/core.rs`, or the engine's blue
noise. What these produce is specified under [Jitter](/docs/reference/rendering#jitter),
[Temporal antialiasing](/docs/reference/rendering#temporal-antialiasing),
[Blue noise](/docs/reference/rendering#blue-noise), and the `Velocity` and `Reactive` rows of
[Stages and targets](/docs/reference/rendering#stages-and-targets).

## How the pieces connect

Temporal antialiasing is an ordinary post-scene pass. Nothing selects an antialiasing technique: an application
installs SMAA, temporal antialiasing, or neither with its own setup call, and `render.pass.taa` bypasses the pass at
runtime. What a temporal
pass needs from the rest of the renderer comes through the sink:

- **Jitter.** `RenderPass::jitters_view` asks for it, like `samples_lens` asks for lens samples. While an enabled pass
  of a sink answers `true`, the renderer sets `Sink::with_jitter` from 8 Halton (2, 3) points. `Sink::view` stays the
  view without jitter, which every pass that culls, places, or reprojects reads. Scene pipelines rasterize through
  `Sink::jittered_view`: the visibility pipeline uploads it as the camera's `view_projection`, and particles and the
  simple pipeline take it from `Sink::view_projection`. Debug meshes draw after the pass, so they take `Sink::view`.
- **Velocity and the reactive mask.** The visibility pipeline writes `Velocity` and clears `Reactive` while its sink is
  jittered. Transparent material evaluation then writes `min(α, 0.9)` into `Reactive`.
- **Exposure and resets.** The pass keeps the extent and exposure of the last frame it resolved. A frame at another
  extent, or after a bypass, resets the history.

These are the inputs AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution (FSR), NVIDIA Deep Learning Super Sampling (DLSS), Intel XeSS, and the MetalFX temporal scaler
take, so another temporal pass reads the same
targets and asks for the same jitter.

## Velocity

The velocity stage runs after the depth pyramid, while the visibility buffer still holds the opaque layer; the
transparent layer overwrites it later. For each pixel it rebuilds the surface point from depth under the jittered view,
takes its barycentrics on the triangle the visibility buffer names, and places the same barycentrics on the triangle as
the previous frame posed and placed it. Rigid and skinned motion share that path, and the result is exact to depth
precision.

The previous frame's state stays on the GPU:

- **Model matrices.** A second dispatch copies each instance's model matrix into a GPU-only buffer after the velocity
  is written, by the instance's slot, which stays the same across frames. A zero matrix means the instance has no
  previous frame. The instance table's previous-frame copy can't serve instead: the CPU writes it for the next frame
  while the GPU may still read it.
- **Skinned positions.** Skinning copies each posed vertex's old position to `previous_vertex_positions` before writing
  the new one.

The background moves by the camera's turn alone, and takes the far plane as its previous depth, so the disocclusion
test catches background pixels that geometry covered in the previous frame.

## The resolve

`taa/resolve.besl` is one tile pass that writes `main` and the history. Its steps follow published methods, ordered by
how much ghosting each prevents:

1. **Nearest-depth velocity dilation** (FSR 2; Pedersen, *Temporal Reprojection Anti-Aliasing in INSIDE*, 2016). The
   edge of a moving object reprojects with the object's motion, not the background's, which is the largest single
   source of trails.
2. **Depth disocclusion** (FSR 2). The farthest of the 4 previous depth texels around the reprojected point must reach
   99 % of the surface's previous depth. Otherwise something stood in front of it, and its history is dropped. Both
   depths come from `Velocity`, which keeps a copy per frame, because the transparent layer overwrites `Depth` without
   moving the velocity: comparing against `Depth` dropped the history under every blended surface.
3. **Variance clipping toward the box center in YCoCg** (Salvi, *An Excursion in Temporal Supersampling*, Game Developers Conference 2016;
   Pedersen). The box is μ ± γσ of the 3 × 3 neighborhood, inside its minimum and maximum, with γ = 1.
4. **Feedback.** INSIDE's luma weight between feedback 0.88 and 0.97, lowered toward the minimum between 2 and 15
   pixels of motion per frame, held at `n / (n + 1)` for a history `n` frames old, and scaled by `1 − Reactive`.
5. **Sharp resampling.** A 5-tap Catmull-Rom history read (Jimenez, *Filmic SMAA*, SIGGRAPH 2016), so repeated
   resampling doesn't blur still images.
6. **Weighted blending** (Lottes): colors blend as `c / (1 + Y)`, so one bright sample can't dominate the history.

The history's frame count, kept in its alpha, is the one combination of the engine's own: FSR keeps a comparable
count in a separate image. The 1 % disocclusion margin is also the engine's own choice.

## Blue noise

Effects that take one random sample per pixel read the engine's spatiotemporal blue noise (Wolfe et al.,
*Spatiotemporal Blue Noise Masks*, Eurographics Symposium on Rendering 2022): 32 slices of 64 × 64, two channels, stacked in one 64 × 2048 texture.
Each slice is blue noise across the image, and each texel's values through the slices are blue noise in time, so the pass's
blend averages them faster than values that change at random. The tests in `lookup_textures.rs` generate it with the
paper's void-and-cluster energy: a Gaussian across a slice within one slice, a Gaussian through time within one texel,
σ = 1.9 on every axis, and a 10 % initial pattern. The camera jitter isn't blue noise: it moves the whole image by one
offset per frame, which a low-discrepancy sequence covers most evenly.

## Rules a change must keep

- **Reproject without jitter.** `Sink::view` never includes the jitter. Velocity uses the views without jitter on both
  frames, so a still scene has zero velocity whatever the jitter.
- **Rebuild positions with jitter.** A pass that turns a pixel and its depth back into a position uses the jittered
  view, because the depth was rasterized through it. `screen_view_data` builds the screen-space passes' constants from
  `Sink::jittered_view` and keeps the projection's off-center terms. Built without jitter, each frame's positions were
  off by that frame's jitter, and the shadows the sun visibility pass looked up from them shook.
- **Keep the velocity convention.** `Velocity` holds this frame's position less the previous frame's, in pixels, x
  right and y down, and the opaque surface's previous and current linear depth in blue and alpha.
- **Read the visibility buffer before the transparent layer.** The velocity stage records before the transparent draw.
- **Clear `Reactive` wherever velocity runs.** Only blended surfaces raise it.
- **Reset on frames whose history isn't this sink's.** A resize or a bypass resets the history.
- **Keep the noise still without jitter.** Passes without history read blue noise slice 0 unless the sink is jittered.

## Tests

- `renderer::core` tests the jitter sequence, and `view` tests the pixel offset.
- `tests/velocity.rs` runs `velocity.besl` in the BESL VM: a still scene under jitter, a moving wall, a moving camera,
  and an instance's first frame.
- The skinning VM test checks that skinning keeps the replaced position.
- `lookup_textures.rs` checks the blue noise's value counts, its spatial and temporal spectra, and, among the ignored
  tests, that the committed texture matches its generator. Regenerate it with
  `cargo nextest run -p byte-engine --cargo-profile release --run-ignored only write_blue_noise`.
- `render_passes/taa.rs` tests the resolve's stages in the BESL VM.

## Measure a change

The visibility pipeline times the velocity stage as `stage.velocity`, and the pass reports `pass.taa`. Read them as
[Measure performance](/docs/develop#measure-performance) describes, with `--render.ghi.features.counters=true`.
