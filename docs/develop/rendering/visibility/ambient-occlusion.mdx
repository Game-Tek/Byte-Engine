Read this page before you change GTAO's evaluate, blur, or upscale shaders, or
how material evaluation occludes reflections. What an application can rely on
is specified under
[ambient occlusion](/docs/reference/rendering#ambient-occlusion) and
[reflections](/docs/reference/rendering#reflections) in the rendering
reference.

## Three passes at half resolution

GTAO runs in three compute passes before material evaluation:

| Pass | Shader | Writes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evaluate | `gtao.besl` | `GTAO Raw` (visibility, R8) and `GTAO Bent Normal` (RG8), half size |
| Denoise | `gtao-blur-x.besl` | `GTAO Blur` and `GTAO Bent Normal Blur`, half size |
| Upscale | `gtao-upscale.besl` | `Ambient Occlusion`, full size |

Material evaluation reads the full-size visibility texel by texel, and the
half-size bent normal with bilinear filtering. The bent normal feeds only
specular occlusion, a smooth term, so it skips the depth-aware upscale.

## Two channels for the bent normal

Every slice's visible arc holds the view vector `V`, because the camera sees
the pixel, so the bent normal lies in the hemisphere around `V`. Its two
components across `V`, in the frame `t = normalize(up × V)` and `b = V × t`,
hold all of it: material evaluation rebuilds the same frame in world space from
the view's up axis and its own `V`, and the third component as
`√(1 − x² − y²)`. That map of the hemisphere has no folds, so the denoise's
averages and the bilinear read stay valid, which an octahedral map's seams
wouldn't allow. RG8 keeps it to about half a degree near `V`, coarser toward
the horizon, finer than the cone-cone fit can show. A pixel with no
reconstructed normal stores zero, the view vector.

## Horizons, not maximum cosines

The evaluation follows Jimenez and others' "Practical Realtime Strategies for
Accurate Indirect Occlusion" (2016), with the falloff, arc cosine, and bent
normal integral of Intel's XeGTAO, keeping their constants. Each slice is the
plane through the view vector and one screen direction:

1. Each side of the slice starts at the surface's tangent, `cos(n ± π/2)`,
   where `n` is the angle of the normal projected onto the slice.
2. Every sample raises its side's horizon cosine to the cosine between the
   view vector and the direction to the sample. Past 38.5 % of the radius, the
   sample's horizon fades back to the tangent, and past the radius it counts
   for nothing.
3. The two horizon angles, clamped to the hemisphere around the projected
   normal, bound the visible arc. Integrating the arc against the cosine around
   the normal gives the slice's visibility, and against the slice's direction
   its bent normal.

The slice's view-space direction is the direction a positive screen step takes
at constant depth, `(d.x · pixel_to_ray_mul.x, d.y · pixel_to_ray_mul.y, 0)`
times `view_z_sign`. Positive samples raise the horizon on that side, and the
integrals measure angles toward it, so a sign error there turns the bent
normal toward occluders instead of away from them.

Each horizon cosine is lowered by `GTAO_BIAS`, 0.05. That constant isn't part
of the published method: it predates the horizon integral, where it offset the
old estimator's elevation, and keeps flat surfaces open despite the depth
error of the pyramid's conservative coarse mips.

## Keep the tuned sample loop

The evaluation's sample loop is tuned for occupancy and memory latency. A
change to the occlusion math must keep:

- The rolled slice and sample loops. Unrolling the sample set raises the
  backend's register tier and lowers occupancy.
- The paired positive and negative reads, issued before either result is
  used.
- Mip selection by step length, which sends steps of 8 and 16 half-size pixels
  or more to pyramid mips 1 and 2.
- One fetch of the engine's static 64 × 64 blue noise per pixel. Its first
  channel turns the slices by up to one slice spacing; a turn of up to π gives
  the same slice sets but folds the noise over itself once per slice, which
  spoils its spectrum. Its second channel offsets the samples, shifted along
  the golden-ratio sequence for each side and each step. Nothing accumulates
  GTAO over frames, so the noise doesn't animate.
- Per-slice trigonometry only, and as little of it as the integrals allow. The
  sample loop does one inverse square root and no trigonometry. Each slice
  takes `sincos` of its two horizon angles and `sin n` from `cos n`, and
  expands every other angle the integrals need, multiples of `h` plus or minus
  `n`, with the double-angle, triple-angle, and angle-addition identities.

The denoise shares its occlusion and depth tiles in workgroup memory. The bent
normal takes the occlusion's weights but reads its taps from the image, since a
shared tile of two-channel texels would double the workgroup memory.

## Specular occlusion from the bent cone

Material evaluation dims the environment's reflection by Jimenez and others'
cone-cone approximation, as Unity's High Definition Render Pipeline (HDRP) writes it in
`GetSpecularOcclusionFromBentAO`. The open directions form a cone around the
bent normal whose cosine `√(1 − v)` holds the cosine-weighted visibility `v`,
and the reflection's lobe is a cone around the mirror direction. The share of
the lobe inside the visibility cone is the occlusion. Russell's horizon
occlusion still multiplies on top, because the bent normal comes from depth
and doesn't see the shading normal's normal map.

Without GTAO, the shading normal stands in for the bent normal, and the cone
still narrows with the indirect diffuse source's visibility.

## Tests

The BESL VM tests in `pipelines/visibility/tests.rs` run the production
shaders:

- `gtao_bends_the_normal_away_from_an_occluder`: an open plane keeps a
  visibility of 1 and its own normal, and a wall beside it occludes it and
  tilts the bent normal away.
- `gtao_writes_white_for_background_and_expected_recessed_foreground_ao`: the
  sky's visibility and bent normal, and a pit's near-total occlusion.
- `gtao_uses_depth_pyramid_for_distant_samples` and
  `gtao_floor_has_no_scale_dependent_normal_seam`: mip selection and normal
  reconstruction.
- `gtao_half_resolution_blur_preserves_uniform_ao_and_smooths_horizontally`:
  the denoise keeps uniform input in both images and mixes columns.

`cone_specular_occlusion_keeps_the_lobe_share_inside_the_visibility_cone_in_the_besl_vm`
in `shader_generator/tests.rs` checks the specular occlusion helper's limits
and its cap ratio.
