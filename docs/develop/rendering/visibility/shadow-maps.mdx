Read this page before you change which lights get shadow maps, how large those
maps are, or how often they're drawn. What an application can rely on is
specified under [shadow maps](/docs/reference/rendering#shadow-maps) in the
visibility pipeline reference.

## Shadow maps share a budget

Each selected point light renders six 90-degree views into a cube map of 16-bit
depth faces, and each selected cone light renders one perspective view into a
16-bit depth map. Every shadow map comes out of one budget,
`render.shadow-maps.budget-mb`, 128 MiB by default: the sun's four cascades
cost 32 MiB at 2,048 texels per side, and a cone or point light costs what its
size class costs. The [shadow map reference](/docs/reference/rendering#shadow-maps) lists the
classes and their costs.

Selection runs with the first sink each frame. The first directional light is
placed first, then cone and point lights ranked by their projected sink pixel
coverage, then the remaining directional lights. A light that doesn't fit stays
lit without shadows and warns once. The layout is kept across frames while it
still fits, so lights don't flicker between shadowed and unshadowed as the
ranking shifts. After an IES profile reaches the GPU, its dimmed calibrated
peak candela feeds the same ranking and the automatic shadow coverage.

## Local shadow maps come in size classes

A cone or point light's map is only as large as the light looks. Selection
projects each light's shadow range into every sink, keeps the largest diameter,
and picks the power-of-two size class from 128 to 2,048 texels per side that
gives about one texel per pixel. Powers of two keep a light's size from
changing every frame, and the selection keeps each light's previous class, so a
light shrinks only once it needs well under a smaller class. Without that
margin, a light whose span hovers at a power of two would reallocate its map
every frame.

Each class has its own cone image and its own point image. Material evaluation
binds them as the `cone_shadow_maps` and `point_shadow_maps` descriptor arrays
and indexes them with the light's `shadow_size_class`. A light's `shadow_layer`
is its layer within its class's image. Selection orders cone and point lights by
class and then layer, so consecutive layers of a class have consecutive views.

A change to the size classes must keep:

- The class count and the smallest class's 128 texels, which `material-evaluation.besl`
  and the point-shadow call in material evaluation spell out. A build guard in
  `shader_generator.rs` fails when they drift.
- Views in class and layer order, which the shadow pass relies on to draw a run
  of layers in one pass.
- The point-shadow filter's tap radius and face-edge margin scaled by the face
  resolution, so every class filters across the same number of texels. The
  shader generator tests check that face-coordinate and general taps agree at
  every class.

## Shadow maps update on a budget

A map isn't drawn again every frame. The
[shadow map updates](/docs/reference/rendering#shadow-map-updates) rules decide
which maps draw, and three pieces carry them out:

- **Slots.** `select_shadow_lights` takes last frame's `ShadowSlots` and keeps
  each light at its layer while it stays in its class; a new map takes the
  lowest free layer. Layers pack from zero only when the gaps would push the
  images past the memory budget. A map's contents survive only while its layer
  does, so anything that moves a light to another layer must start it over.
- **States.** `ShadowMapStates` in `shadow_updates.rs` keeps, for every map,
  the view it was drawn with and its age, and `schedule_updates` picks the maps
  that fit the texel budget. The manager updates a copy each frame and keeps it
  only once the first sink records the frame-wide work, so a frame that waits
  on a compiling pipeline doesn't mark maps drawn that never drew.
- **Views.** Every map that doesn't draw is sampled with the view it was drawn
  with. The CPU writes those views for cone and point maps, and for cascades
  under frustum fitting. Under receivers fitting the cascade fit pass does it on
  the GPU: it fits only the cascades in the update mask, stores their fitted
  views in its persistent `drawn_views` buffer, and copies the stored view back
  for every other cascade. A cascade with no drawn map gets a projection that
  puts every receiver outside it, so sun visibility falls back to a coarser
  cascade.

The shadow pass groups the frame's draws into runs of consecutive layers with
consecutive views and draws each run in one layered render pass with
`AttachmentInformation::layers(first_layer, layer_count)`. Only those layers
are cleared, which is what keeps the other maps, and a task workgroup still
culls each meshlet against up to six views of a run at once. Each backend
starts the range at `first_layer`: Metal through a 2D array texture view of the
range, Vulkan through a view that starts at that layer, and DX12 through a
render-target or depth-stencil view of the range.

A change to the updates must keep:

- Cascades 0 and 1 of every sun drawing every frame, ahead of the budget.
- A map's drawn view and its layer moving together: a map sampled with a view
  other than the one it was drawn with shows shadows in the wrong place.
- Only the drawn layers cleared in a run's render pass.
- The cascade fit's masks and `drawn_views` in step with the CPU's states. The
  VM test `cascades_that_do_not_draw_keep_their_drawn_view_in_the_besl_vm`
  checks the shader's side, and the `shadow_updates` tests the scheduler and
  states.

## Sun penumbrae read every texel under their filter

A sun's penumbra is as wide as its occluder is far above the receiver, times
the tangent of the sun's angular radius: about 55 millimeters on each side for
Sponza's roof edge, 12 meters above the courtyard floor. The
[penumbra rules](/docs/reference/rendering#the-visibility-pipeline) filter it
with a tent that reads every texel within 2 to 8 texels of the receiver. The
sun visibility resolve reads them in blocks of 4x4 texels, from one block at a
radius of 2 to 4x4 blocks at 8, and issues each block's four gathers before it
compares any texel, so they're in flight together. Each block's weights and
plane depths step from its first texel, and the weight sums come out of the
same loop. The material shader's copy, for transparent receivers, fetches the
texels one by one, because gathers in that kernel slowed every opaque material.

A penumbra outside the tent's 2 to 8 texels is clamped, so it comes out wider
or narrower in meters than it is. The penumbra cascade is the one where the
clamp changes it least, by the larger of 2 / w and w / 6, where w is the
penumbra's width in that cascade's texels: 2 / w is how far a 2-texel tent
widens a narrower penumbra, and w / 6 weighs the reads a wider one costs.
Between cascades whose texels differ by a factor of 2, the coarser one takes
over at 4.9 of the finer one's texels, and the fade mixes the two from 4 to 6
of them. Receivers fitting doesn't keep the cascades in order of texel size: on
Sponza, cascade 2 can have finer texels than cascade 1, and cascade 3 sixteen
times coarser ones than cascade 2. With receivers fitting, a coarser cascade
also often doesn't hold a nearby receiver, since it's fitted to farther
surfaces; then a finer one filters the penumbra, up to 8 texels wide, 64
gathers, a cost that falls only on the pixels of wide penumbrae.

Two earlier filters show why. The first kept 16 taps and spread them up to 4
texels apart, so it skipped up to 15 of every 16 texels and drew an occluder's
edge on a grid 4 texels wide: near Sponza's courtyard floor, a staircase of
soft blocks 20 to 30 pixels long that rearranged as the camera moved its
cascade, spacing, and blend. The second read every texel but moved to the next
cascade once the penumbra spanned 4 texels, wherever it landed there. Looking
down Sponza's courtyard from the upper gallery, the roof's penumbra kept its
55 millimeters in cascade 1, and past the boundary to cascade 2, which handed
it to cascade 3, where it spanned two-thirds of a texel, the 2-texel tent
blurred it to 167 millimeters. It now stays in cascade 2 at 41 millimeters,
clamped to 8 texels.

On Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, with the camera 2 to 3.2 meters
above the courtyard floor looking 35° down at the roof's shadow edge, moving
the camera up in 10-centimeter steps changed the edge's sun visibility,
compared on the floor plane, by 0.37 to 1.00 of 255 on average each step with
the spaced taps, and by 0.04 to 0.08 with every texel read, the comparison's
own noise. In that view, where most of the image is a wide penumbra, the
`screen-space` stage, which holds the sun visibility resolve, took 1.88 ms with
the spaced taps, 2.08 ms with every texel read in a loop that compared each
quad as it arrived, 1.94 ms with each block's four gathers issued first, and
1.99 ms with the cascade chosen by how little the clamp changes the penumbra,
in frames where the shadow raster matched within 2 %. From the upper gallery,
the cascade choice took the stage from 1.53 to 1.61 ms.

A change to the penumbra filter must keep:

- The penumbra cascade chosen by how much the 2-to-8-texel clamp changes the
  penumbra's width in meters, not by a texel count alone, so the blur stays the
  same width where the cascade changes. Cascades don't nest under receivers
  fitting, so neither their order nor a fixed ratio between them may be assumed.
- Every texel under the tent read. A filter that reaches further with the same
  taps undersamples the map and brings the staircase back.
- The filter's reach of 8 texels, plus the receiver's move off its surface,
  inside the 10-texel margin that `directional_shadow_cascade_holds` keeps from
  a cascade's edge, so the filter never reads past the map. The penumbra
  cascade is chosen only among cascades that hold the receiver, and the margin
  stays inside the 17 texels that pad every fitted cascade.
- The two copies, in `sun-visibility.besl` and `shader_generator/sources/shadow.rs`,
  in agreement. The VM tests
  `directional_shadow_penumbra_widens_with_its_radius_in_the_besl_vm` and
  `directional_shadow_penumbra_follows_a_shallow_edge_in_the_besl_vm` check the
  material shader's copy, and
  `sun_visibility_penumbra_filter_matches_the_material_shaders_in_the_besl_vm`
  holds the resolve's copy to it.
