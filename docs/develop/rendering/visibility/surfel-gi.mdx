Read this page before you change surfel global illumination (GI): its stages,
its buffers, the hit shaders materials bake for it, or how it shares the
indirect diffuse images with SSGI. What an application can rely on is specified
under [surfel GI](/docs/reference/rendering#surfel-gi) in the rendering
reference, and the GHI's side of ray tracing is in the
[ray tracing design note](/docs/develop/rendering/ray-tracing).

Surfel GI follows [webgiya](https://github.com/jure/webgiya) by Jure Triglav,
which builds on "Global Illumination Based on Surfels" by Henrik Halén, Andreas
Brinck, Kyle Hayward, and Xiangshun Bei. Its cascaded grid, the reconstruction
of each pixel's light from the surfels, and the surfels that rays' hits spawn
follow [kajiya](https://github.com/EmbarkStudios/kajiya) by Tomasz Stachowiak
and Embark Studios, which is also the reference surfel GI's quality and cost
are measured against. The shaders that adapt either carry its MIT notice, and
`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` lists them.

## The frame's stages

The first sink records the frame-wide stages after its light clusters and
before it draws the scene, so the GPU can run them alongside the raster passes,
which write nothing they read. It records them all or nothing: a frame either
records every one of them or none, because each stage leaves buffers in the
state the next one expects. Every sink that surfel GI lights then records its
own three passes inside `screen-space`.

| Stage | Pass | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `acceleration-structures` | Builds | Builds the bottom levels that fit this frame's budget, then the top level from this frame's instances. |
| `surfel-grid` | `surfel-count` | Ages each surfel, recycles the dead ones, asks for each live surfel's rays in its class, and counts the cells it reaches, nearest first, up to the listing limit. |
| | `surfel-scan-groups`, `-partials`, `-add` | One exclusive scan over every cell count and every surfel's wanted rays in both classes, which also grants the frame's rays. |
| | `surfel-scatter` | Lists each surfel in the cells the count pass picked, from the front of a cell's list when it lies near the cell's center and from the back otherwise, and writes a record for each granted ray. |
| `surfel-trace` | `surfel-trace` | Draws each granted ray's direction from its surfel's guide or its surfel's R2 sequence, and traces it with the inline intersector. A miss writes its result. A hit is kept, with its sort key. |
| | `ray-hit-sort-scan`, `-scatter` | A counting sort, which probe GI shares, that orders the hits by the material they hit. |
| | `surfel-shade` | A ray tracing pipeline: one invocation per sorted hit runs the hit's closest-hit shader with `invoke`, lights the surface it reports with the scene's lights and the surfels around it, votes for the surfel whose light reaches it most, and asks for a surfel where none covers it. |
| `surfel-validate` | `surfel-validate` | Traces a support ray for an eighth of the surfels, and votes out those whose surface is gone. |
| `surfel-integrate` | `surfel-integrate` | Folds each surfel's ray results into its estimate, its guide, and its radial depth, measures how far its radial depth is clear, votes out a surfel buried in geometry, and rewrites its record. |
| | `surfel-allocate-hits` | Turns the hits' spawn requests into surfels while the pool has room for them. |
| `screen-space` | `surfel-resolve` | Lights each half-resolution pixel from the first surfels its cell lists, blends that estimate into the pixel's reprojected history by their confidences, into `Surfel History`, and votes on spawns and despawns. |
| | `surfel-allocate` | Turns each tile's spawn request into a surfel. |
| | `surfel-spatial` | Filters the history over each pixel's surface and fills what it lacks, into `SSGI History`, which material evaluation reads. |

`render_pass/surfel_gi.rs` creates the buffers and the stages,
`render_pass/ray_hits.rs` the hit buffers and the sort that probe GI shares,
and `ray_tracing.rs` keeps the shade pass's pipeline and its shader binding
table current.

## Young and changing surfels get their rays first

Each surfel asks for its rays in `surfel-count`, in one of two classes. A
surfel the camera saw asks in the priority class while it's young, for 32 rays
a frame until it has received `SURFEL_WARM_UP_RAYS`, 512, and while its light
changes faster than its estimate follows, for 16 rays while its inconsistency
is above 0.3. Otherwise it asks for 4 in the regular class, and so does every
surfel a ray's hit spawned, young or not: it lights only what other rays hit,
so it never takes rays from the surfaces the camera sees.

The scan places every surfel's wanted rays after the cell counts, the priority
class's first, in slot order. Each class grants a window of its wanted rays
that starts where its last window ended and wraps around them. The priority
class takes up to `SURFEL_PRIORITY_SHARE`, three quarters, of
`render.surfel-gi.ray-budget`, or more while the regular class wants less than
the rest, and the regular class takes what remains. So a new surfel gets its
rays within a frame or two, and every settled surfel's rays still take their
turn. The scatter, the trace, and the integrate pass all find a ray's place
with `surfel_granted_ray`: the ray's index among its class's wanted rays, minus
the window's start, wrapped around the class's wanted rays.

It replaced one window over every surfel's wanted rays. A new surfel waited for
that window like any other, and it matured, and lookups trusted it, by its age
in frames, so a surfel the budget starved was trusted on a few rays.

A young surfel keeps asking in the priority class well past the
`SURFEL_MATURE_RAYS`, 128, at which lookups trust it fully. In a dim room, the
light comes from rare rays that find a sunlit opening, and a surfel that found
one in its first 128 rays shows as a bright spot until its regular four rays a
frame dilute it. When the camera turns to reveal Sponza's gallery, the warm-up
to 512 rays left 0.5% of the revealed pixels above four times their later light
over frames 10 to 45 after the turn, against 0.9% with the warm-up ending at
128, averaged over six turns each; pixels above twice their later light and the
average error stayed within the runs' spread. The trace spends the whole budget
either way, so its cost doesn't change, and the warm-up takes rays from settled
surfels only while young ones want them. The integrate pass, which walks each
surfel's wanted rays, rose from about 0.059 ms to 0.061 ms on Sponza at
1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, and the frame stayed within the runs' spread.

The trace, the hit sort's scatter, and the shade pass dispatch one thread per
ray of the budget, and threads past the frame's rays or hits return. A fixed
dispatch needs no indirect arguments, and once surfels fill the budget, which
they do in steady state, few threads idle.

## Each surfel takes its 32 nearest cell entries

The grid lists each surfel in every cell where a lookup can weigh it: out to
its lookup radius, 1.25 times its radius, which the resolve and the shade pass
weigh it out to. `surfel_cell_reach` bounds the surfel's disc distance to any
point of a cell from below, so every cell that holds a point the surfel can
light lists it.

The cell lists keep `SURFEL_LISTING_LIMIT` entries, 32, for each pool slot, so
a full pool's lists always fit. A surfel that reaches more cells than the
limit is listed in the nearest of them: `surfel-count` sorts the cells it reaches into
`SURFEL_LISTING_BUCKETS`, 8, shells of its lookup radius by how near their
nearest point lies, and lists the nearest shells whose cells fit the limit. The
first shell holds at most two cells a side in each of two cascades, so it
always fits, and the cell of the surfel's center is always listed. The count
pass stores the shells it picked in the surfel's state, and `surfel-scatter`
visits the same cells in the same order and lists the same shells, so every
count the scan reads matches the entries the scatter writes.

The limit used to stop the walk after the first 32 cells in grid order: the
finer cascade first, then by z, y, and x. A surfel near a cascade's corner,
which can reach 75 cells, then lost the cells along one edge of its disc,
however near, and the pixels there spawned surfels to cover what it no longer
reached. The grid also listed surfels only out to their own radius, so a pixel
near a disc's rim missed a surfel that lookups weigh. Listing out to the lookup
radius, the second walk the count pass takes to pick the shells, and the
surfels hits now spawn took `surfel-grid` from about 0.16 ms to 0.32 ms on
Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, and the cell entries from about 16,000 to
33,000.

## Four threads walk each surfel's cells

`surfel-count` and `surfel-scatter` dispatch `SURFEL_GRID_LANES`, 4, threads
for each pool slot, on consecutive lanes, and each thread takes every fourth
row of the surfel's cells. An Apple GPU waits for a thread's atomics at each
branch, so a surfel's walk takes about as long as one thread's share of its
atomics. In the count pass, the slot's first thread runs the surfel economy and
hands its verdict to the other three with two subgroup shuffles, and the four
add up their shells' cells with two more for each shell, so they all pick the
same listing. That took `surfel-grid` from about 0.31 ms back to 0.16 ms on
Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, in the GPU's faster clock state.

## Lists start with the surfels near each cell's center

A pixel reads at most `SURFEL_LOOKUP_LIMIT`, 64, surfels of its cell's list,
and a ray's hit at most 16, so a crowded cell's list must start with the
surfels that weigh most around it. The scatter lists a surfel from the front of
a cell's list when the cell's center lies within `SURFEL_NEAR_SHARE`, half, of
the surfel's lookup radius, and from the end otherwise. The scan's group pass
sets each cell's fill to its count and its front to zero as it reads the count,
so the two ends meet. Within each end, the order follows the atomics, which can
change from frame to frame.

Readers used to take the first entries in the order the atomics left them. A
sort of each list by distance, tried before the partition, cost about 0.56 ms
on Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4: it ran one thread per cell, so a
crowded cell's thread ran long while the threads beside it waited. Readers
only need the near surfels before the limit, which the partition gives.

## Hits sort by material before they're shaded

The shade pass runs each hit's closest-hit shader through a visible function
table, and rays that call different shaders side by side cost several times
more than rays that call one; the [ray tracing design note](/docs/develop/rendering/ray-tracing#why-hits-are-sorted-before-theyre-shaded)
has the measurements. So the trace keys each hit by the material slot it hit,
which the geometry table and the frame's mesh record name, and the counting sort
orders the hits by that key. Materials that share a closest-hit shader still
sort into separate runs, which keeps the key independent of the published
pipeline.

## Hit groups keep their numbers

Each material whose bake built a closest-hit shader gets a hit group: the
shader with the specialization constants of the material's variant. Materials
with the same shader and constants share the group. Groups keep the number they
first got, so a pipeline built again with more groups runs every older group as
before, and group 0 runs `default-hit.besl`, a black occluder, for every
record whose material's group the published pipeline doesn't have yet.

`HitShading` requests the pipeline again once the last request settled and
groups arrived since, so a burst of materials makes a few requests instead of
one each. The published pipeline keeps shading until the next one publishes,
and a request that fails leaves its new groups running the default shader. The
shader binding table maps each hit record, one per geometry of each bottom
level, to its material's group, and is written again when the pipeline, the
records, or a material's group changes. The frame admits the meshes it draws
for the first time, which gives their geometries records, before it writes the
table, so the table holds the records of every instance the frame places.

## Light is stored pre-exposed

Every surfel's estimate and record hold pre-exposed light, as the lit image
does: multiplied by the exposure of the frame that stored it. Half floats can't
hold physical daylight, and the multiscale mean estimator's constants, such as
its firefly margin of 0.1, are tuned for light around one. The integrate pass
rescales each estimate by the ratio of this frame's exposure to the exposure the
pool last stored its light at, which the pipeline manager keeps for the pool, and
the shade pass divides the light it reads from last frame's records by that
same previous exposure.

## Hits are lit by the scene's lights

The shade pass lights each hit with every light of the light table that reaches
it, through `surfel_direct_light`: each sun it faces, and each point and cone
light it faces within the light's reach, when a shadow ray finds nothing in
between. Point and cone lights shine at their peak intensity in every
direction, without their IES profiles, as `probe_direct_light` in
`probe-shade.besl` lights a probe's hit, so both techniques light a hit the
same way. A hit traces one shadow ray for each light that can reach it.

It replaced the first four suns, which the pipeline manager uploaded apart from
the light table. A lamp's light never reached a surfel, so a scene lit by lamps
alone got no bounce light.

## Surfels gather the sky

A ray that misses brings back the environment's light from its direction, as in
webgiya, so each surfel's light holds the sky it sees through every opening,
from the directions it sees it. The miss reads the environment's specular level
for roughness 2/7, whose blur keeps a small bright patch of sky from making the
estimate noisy, as webgiya's blurred level does. A hit reads the same light from
the surfels around it, so sky light that falls through an opening and off a wall
reaches surfels that see no sky. Under surfel GI, material evaluation adds no
environment light of its own, and each surfel's hit fraction only dims the
environment's reflections. The sky's light comes at the surfels' detail, without
normal maps, and follows a change to the environment as fast as the estimate
converges.

An earlier design left the sky to material evaluation: it added the
environment's irradiance around each pixel's normal times one minus the
surfels' hit fraction. That lost which part of the sky a surface sees, so a
wall that sees sky only high up through a roof got the light of a hemisphere
centered on the horizon. It also hung the whole sky on one number per surfel,
and in a dim gallery that number's error set neighbors visibly apart.

Surfels resolve occlusion only down to their own size, 24 centimeters across
near the camera, so the light in a crease, a joint between floor tiles, or the
foot of a column is the light of the open surface around it. GTAO fills that
gap: material evaluation multiplies the surfels' light by GTAO's visibility.
Keep GTAO's radius below the surfels' size, as the model viewer's 15
centimeters is. A radius that reaches as far as the surfels do counts the
occlusion their rays already found twice. Probe GI shares the same
push-constant value and gets the same product, since its cells are 20
centimeters across. SSGI instead takes the smaller of its own visibility and
GTAO's, because both estimate the same screen-space occlusion.

A pixel no surfel covers yet takes its light from the spatial pass: its
neighbors' light, or the nearest light the screen knows, or none, until a
surfel spawns there, as
[pixels nothing knows take the nearest known light](#pixels-nothing-knows-take-the-nearest-known-light)
explains. A hit where no surfel lies yet, such as a surface off the screen,
gets the scene's lights alone: the sky it would see is unknown, and counting all
of it would leak light into closed rooms. It asks for a surfel there instead,
whose rays then gather that sky.

## The estimate keeps converging

The multiscale mean estimator moves its long mean toward each update's average
only as fast as its catch-up allows, and while the long and short means agree
within a deviation, the catch-up falls to 1/256 of its variance-based blend:
at most 1/8192 an update. A young surfel averages its rays evenly, and with
four rays a frame they leave an error about as large as all the sky light a dim
room gets, which the estimator alone would keep for thousands of frames. So the
long mean also moves at least as fast as an even average of the rays the
estimate holds.

The estimate counts what it holds in rays, and what it held fades by
e<sup>−f/240</sup> over the f frames since its last update. So how fast it
follows a small change is set in frames, `SURFEL_MEMORY_FRAMES`, whatever
cadence the ray budget gives the surfel: a full pool, which updates each surfel
less often, still follows within seconds. A surfel updated every frame holds
about 960 rays, and an error too small for the catch-up to notice halves within
about 170 frames, while a change the estimator notices still catches up at
once. A surfel also matures, and lookups trust it, by the rays it received
rather than its age: trust grows from zero to one over its first
`SURFEL_MATURE_RAYS`, and a spawn that copies a parent's light starts with
`SURFEL_PARENT_PRIOR_RAYS`, 32, of them.

## Rays follow each surfel's guide

Each surfel keeps a guide: 8 × 8 cells over its hemisphere, each holding the
light its rays found in that direction lately, times the direction's cosine.
The cells come from the concentric map of the unit square onto the disk, lifted
onto the hemisphere so that equal areas of the square cover equal solid angles.
A cell's density is then a constant, so the trace draws a cell by its share of
the guide and a uniform point inside it, and knows the direction's density
without a table.

The trace draws a share of a surfel's rays from the guide that grows with the
rays the surfel received, to half from `SURFEL_GUIDE_RAYS`, 192, on, and the
other rays from the cosine-weighted distribution, so directions the guide holds
dark keep being sampled. Each ray's weight, the cosine-weighted density over
the mixture's density, turns the mixture back into the cosine-weighted average
the estimate expects. Webgiya steers 90% of the rays; here half stay with the
cosine-weighted distribution, which bounds every ray's weight at 2 and leaves
half the rays for the hit fraction, which counts only them.

The cosine-weighted rays walk the surfel's own R2 sequence ("The Unreasonable
Effectiveness of Quasirandom Sequences" by Martin Roberts), rotated by a hash
of its slot. A ray's point is the rays the surfel received before this update
plus the ray's index among this update's, so the sequence continues from update
to update, and a surfel that gets four rays at a time still covers its
hemisphere evenly instead of in random clumps.

A miss brings back the environment's light from its direction, so the guide
learns where the sky shines through, and guided rays keep finding those
openings. The integrate pass moves the four cells around each ray's direction
toward the light the ray found, and keeps the guide's sum, `guide_mass`, which
the trace draws against.

## The hit fraction counts cosine rays

The weights keep the light's average right, but they vary: about 0.07 in the
guide's strongest cells and 2 outside them. A hit fraction weighed by them
scatters around its true value even when every ray hits. Material evaluation
dims reflections by one minus the hit fraction, clamped at zero, so in an
enclosed room the scatter's low half let the environment's reflection through.
In Sponza's south gallery, the lit image's surfel-sized patches followed
max(0, 1 − A), with a correlation of 0.71 to 0.90 over five runs, and not the
diffuse light.

So each ray's result flags whether the guide drew it, and the hit fraction
averages only the rays drawn from the cosine-weighted distribution, each
counting once. In an enclosed room every one of them hits, and the fraction is
exactly one. The light still counts every ray with its weight.

The hit fraction stays unbiased. In Veach's multiple importance sampling, any
weights that sum to one wherever the integrand isn't zero, and that are zero
wherever their distribution can't draw, give an unbiased estimate. The hit
fraction's integrand is the visibility times cos θ / π, which is zero wherever
the cosine-weighted density is, so a weight of one on that distribution and zero
on the guide qualifies. The trace picks each ray's distribution independently of
where the ray goes, so given the picks, the cosine-weighted rays are plain
cosine-weighted samples: Veach's multi-sample model, whose variance is never
larger than the one-sample model's (Sbert, Havran, and Szirmay-Kalos, 2016).
Dividing by the cosine-weighted rays a frame traced, rather than by how many it
expected, keeps an enclosed room's fraction exactly one. A frame whose rays all
came from the guide, one frame in 16 with four rays, leaves the fraction as it
was; the picks don't depend on what the rays find, so skipping those frames
biases nothing.

Dividing the weighted hit fraction by the sum of the weights, Hesterberg's
weighted average (1995), would also read one when every ray hits. With four rays
a frame, though, its bias follows the guide, and the estimator's average over
frames doesn't remove it.

In the same gallery, the hit fraction now reads exactly one, and the lit image
follows the diffuse light. Its 32 × 32-pixel blocks changed by 73–88% of their
light between two captures 230 frames apart, and now change by 12%; the
gallery reads a sixth as bright, because the reflection it no longer gets was
most of its light.

## Radial depth hides surfels behind walls

A surfel's disc reaches every pixel near its center, including pixels on the
other side of a thin wall. So each surfel also keeps its radial depth: 4 × 4
cells over the same map, each holding the first four moments of how far its
rays went there, up to twice its radius. The resolve weighs each surfel's light
by the pixel's visibility from it, the Hamburger four-moment bound of moment
shadow mapping, as webgiya runs it. A cell no ray reached yet holds zero and
hides nothing. When every surfel around a pixel hides it, the pixel takes no
light, sky included, and a hit fraction of one, which also hides the
environment's reflections there.

Radial depth doesn't share the guide's map. The resolve maps a direction to its
cells for every pixel and surfel pair, so radial depth uses the hemisphere's
octahedral projection, which needs no trigonometry, and the integrate pass maps
each ray's direction to both. The resolve also skips the four-moment solve for a
pixel well nearer than a cell's mean distance, which Cantelli's inequality shows
the surfel sees, because the bound the solve finds can't exceed Cantelli's.

Most of the test's cost comes before the solve: turning each pixel's direction
into the surfel's frame, mapping it to cells, and reading them. So the integrate
pass also stores each surfel's clearance, the distance below which every cell a
ray reached clears a point alone, and the resolve sees a pixel nearer than that
without any of the test. A cell clears a point by Cantelli's inequality, or when
the point lies below the lower root of the cell's second orthogonal polynomial,
where some distribution with the cell's moments lies entirely beyond the point,
so the four-moment bound finds no shadow. Cantelli's bound alone clears little,
because one cell whose distances spread sinks it; the root clears up to about
the nearest surface a cell's rays hit. A blend of cells clears whatever all of
its cells clear, because its distribution mixes theirs.

Pixels on a surfel's own plane lie just below the point its rays leave from, so
they read the cells along its horizon. The shade pass weighs the surfels around
each hit by the same test, so light that reached one side of a wall doesn't
bounce off the other side; it reads at most 16 surfels a hit. On Sponza at
1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, the test at hits adds about 0.04 ms to
`surfel-trace`, and the gallery's light stays within the spread between
captures of one build.

## Hits keep the surfels that light them

Pixels vote only for the surfels the camera sees, so a surfel off the screen
would age out even while it lights what other surfels' rays hit, such as a
ceiling lit through a doorway. The shade pass votes for the surfel whose light
reaches each hit most, which keeps the surfels the bounce light depends on
alive.

## Hits spawn surfels off the screen

Pixels spawn surfels only on the surfaces the camera sees, so a ray that hit a
surface off the screen found no surfel there and brought back the suns alone.
As in kajiya's irradiance cache, which allocates entries where rays look them
up, a hit that no surfel covers asks for one, and `surfel-allocate-hits` spawns
it without light at the hit's position and normal. The next frame's grid lists
it, it fades in as its rays arrive, and the hits it lights keep it alive.

Three limits keep these surfels from crowding out the surfaces the camera sees:

- Only rays of surfels a pixel spawned ask, those whose `bounce` is zero, so a
  hit-spawned surfel's rays spawn nothing, as kajiya limits allocation by the
  lookup's bounce.
- A cell asks once a frame, the first hit to claim it, and a frame asks at most
  `SURFEL_MAX_HIT_SPAWNS`, 64, times. A claim holds the frame index plus one,
  so the zeros a new pool starts with claim nothing.
- The allocation spawns only while the surfels hits spawned make up less than
  `SURFEL_BOUNCED_SHARE`, a quarter, of the live surfels, as the frame's grid
  counted them, and the slots in use less than `SURFEL_HIT_SPAWN_SHARE`, three
  quarters, of the pool. It runs before the screen-space passes, so the rest of
  the pool stays free for the camera's own spawns.

Without the shares, hits spawned about ten surfels a frame on Sponza for as
long as the view stayed, the pool grew to 5,000–7,000 surfels, and the
resolve's cost grew with it. With them, the pool settles at about 1,500
surfels, about 400 of them spawned by hits.

## Surfels check that their surface is still there

A surfel keeps the world position it spawned at, while instances move with
their transforms, so a surfel can be left floating where a surface was, or end
up inside geometry that moved over it. Two checks vote such a surfel out with
the kill vote a crowded pixel casts, and the pixels it covered spawn surfels
where the surfaces are now:

- `surfel-validate` checks the slots whose index plus the frame index is a
  multiple of `SURFEL_VALIDATE_PERIOD`, 8, so each surfel every 8 frames. Its
  support ray crosses the surfel's plane, from a quarter of its radius above
  the point its rays leave from to as far below. A hit marks the surfel
  supported, and a supported surfel whose ray finds nothing dies. A surfel
  never supported, such as one on a masked or skinned surface the rays pass
  through, lives as before.
- `surfel-integrate` follows the share of a surfel's rays that hit back faces,
  which the shade pass flags, over about 16 rays. A surfel that received 32
  rays and whose share passes half lies inside geometry, and dies.

The pool counts both in `lost`, which the pipeline manager's debug log reports.
`surfel-validate` records after `surfel-trace`, which waits for the top level,
so its counter measures its own work: about 0.03 ms on Sponza at 1024 × 1024
on an Apple M4.

## Pixels keep the estimate and its confidence apart

`surfel-resolve` works out two things for each half-resolution pixel. Its estimate
blends the light of the surfels that see it by their weights and visibility,
normalized over how much lookups trust each one, so a new surfel's light shows
at once. Its confidence is how much of the pixel's coverage trusted surfels
make up, so that light counts only as much as the surfel's rays earned. A pixel
no surfel covers has neither.

The resolve used to blend each surfel by its weight times its trust and fill
the rest with the environment's irradiance. A new surfel then faded in from the
sky's light, so a fresh surfel in a dark room flashed bright before its rays
darkened it, and a pixel whose surfel died showed sky until another spawned.

The resolve then keeps a history of each pixel's light in `Surfel History`,
and in `Surfel History Weights` the confidence it accumulated, at most
`SURFEL_HISTORY_LIMIT`, 16 frames' worth, and its luminance's variance. It
reprojects the previous frame's history to where the previous camera saw the
pixel's surface, from the four texels around that point, each counting only
where the previous frame's depth and normal show the same surface, as SSGI's
temporal pass checks. It blends the estimate in by its confidence, so a surfel
that fades in or dies changes the light gradually. An estimate more than 3
deviations from the history's luminance cuts the history's confidence, down to
one frame's worth at 8 deviations, so a change, such as a lamp turning off,
shows within a frame or two. As in kajiya, the history counts its samples to a
limit and cuts the limit back when the light changes past its variance.
`surfel_temporal_accumulate`, in `layouts/surfel_temporal.besl`, does this at
the end of the resolve. It was a pass of its own, which read the estimate and
the confidence back from two images; inside the resolve, it saves that
dispatch, both images, and four reads a pixel: 0.64 ms of resolve and 0.19 ms
of history became 0.73 ms.

`surfel-spatial` filters the history for material evaluation, into
`SSGI History`. Each pixel averages its history with neighbors on a
golden-angle spiral, weighed by how surely they lie on its surface and by their
confidence, after a reversible tone map ("Optimized Reversible Tonemapper for
Resolve" by Timothy Lottes) that keeps a lone bright texel from spreading. As
in kajiya, the spiral shrinks from 16 texels to 2, and its taps from eight to
two, as the pixel's confidence grows. What the pixel's surface still lacks
comes from its neighbors at a similar depth on any surface, and where none
knows anything, from the environment's irradiance. The output feeds nothing but
material evaluation, never a surfel, a ray, or the history, so the filter's
error never compounds.

## Pixels nothing knows take the nearest known light

When the camera turns, no surfel covers the strip it turns toward yet. The
resolve spawns surfels there in the frame the strip appears, and their first
rays arrive in the next frame, so for that frame the strip's pixels have neither
an estimate nor a history. The spatial pass fills them: from the spiral's taps
where any of them knows the light, and otherwise from rings of 8 taps that
double in radius from 32 texels to 1,024, which reach across the image. The
nearest ring that holds known light fills the pixel, its taps at a similar depth
weighing most, but every tap with confidence counting a little, so a strip
beside a nearer surface still takes that surface's light. Where nothing on the
screen knows any light, as in the first frames, the pixel takes none, and a hit
fraction of one that also hides the environment's reflections, as a hit no
surfel covers gets no sky.

The fallback was the environment's irradiance around the pixel's normal, the
light material evaluation gives a surface without surfel GI: the light of a
surface nothing hides from the sky. In an enclosed gallery that's thousands of
times its bounce light. On Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, a 60° turn
from the gallery's arch left the two thirds of the image it revealed at about
5,300 times their settled light in the frame of the turn, almost white; with the
rings, about 1.6 times. The spiral reaches 16 texels, so a camera that turned
faster than about 2° a frame at a 75° field of view, which reveals a strip
wider than that, showed a white edge in every frame of the turn: in a pan at
150° a second, about 4° a frame, the 24 texel columns at the leading edge read
a median 440 times the light at the image's center, and with the rings 0.4
times. A frame that finds every pixel known never enters the rings, and
`screen-space` stayed within the runs' spread of its 1.9 ms.

On Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, in frames of one GPU clock state,
surfel GI adds about 1.77 ms to a frame without indirect diffuse light, which
takes 5.05 ms, against 1.33 ms before the kajiya comparison; `screen-space`
takes about 2.43 ms, against 2.05 ms with the old resolve. The resolve takes
0.97 ms of it with the history, and the spatial pass 0.13 ms. The indirect
light changes about 2.7 times less between captures 4 frames apart, at the same
mean. When the sun turns, 84% of the change remains after 15 frames, 18% after
60, and 2% after 120; before the reconstruction and the ray classes, 66%, 19%,
and 9%.

## Rules a change must keep

- **The frame-wide stages leave their counts at zero.** The scan resets each
  cell count and its front as it reads it, the scatter counts each cell's fill
  down to zero, the scan's partial pass resets the pool's frame counts and the
  hits' spawn requests, and the hit sort's scan resets the hit counts. A stage
  that skips this breaks the next frame.
- **Each surfel takes at most `SURFEL_LISTING_LIMIT` entries.** `surfel-count`
  and `surfel-scatter` visit a surfel's cells in the same order and list the
  shells the count pass stored in the surfel's state, and the cell entries hold
  the limit's entries for every pool slot. The resolve and the shade pass read
  their cell's range without a clamp, so a pass that lists a surfel in more
  cells, or entries sized below the capacity times the limit, reads and writes
  past the buffer. The limit in `render_pass/surfel_gi.rs` equals the one in
  `layouts/surfel_gi.besl`.
- **A slot's grid threads share a subgroup and reach every shuffle.**
  `surfel-count` and `surfel-scatter` take `SURFEL_GRID_LANES` threads a slot,
  in `render_pass/surfel_gi.rs` as in `layouts/surfel_gi.besl`, at consecutive
  indices that start at a multiple of their count, so any subgroup of four or
  more threads holds them. The count's threads run every shuffle, a dead or
  out-of-pool slot's included, so none returns before the last one; its walks
  then run zero times. A thread that left early, or a slot split across
  subgroups, would pick a listing the scatter doesn't follow.
- **A cell's two ends meet.** The scatter takes a near surfel's entry from the
  cell's front and any other's from its fill, which the scan's group pass sets
  to the cell's count; both drop an entry past the cell's range, which only
  floating-point rounding can cause. A pass that lists from one end only, or a
  fill set to anything but the count, overwrites entries or leaves entries of
  an earlier frame, which can name free slots.
- **The ray window formula is the same everywhere.** `surfel-scatter`,
  `surfel-trace`, and `surfel-integrate` find a ray's place with
  `surfel_granted_ray`, and a slot asks for rays in one class only.
- **A sort key fits 32 bits.** The material slot sits above 20 bits of rank, so
  the material limit stays at most 4,096 and the ray budget at most 2<sup>20</sup>.
  The material counts of `RayHitSort` in `layouts/ray_hits.besl` number
  `MAX_MATERIALS`.
- **Records reach the table before their instances.**
  `SceneAccelerationStructures::admit` gives the meshes a frame draws for the
  first time their records and returns the record list the shader binding
  table is written from; `prepare` places the instances after. The shade pass's
  `invoke` calls the table entry a hit's record names without a bounds check, so
  a table written before admission lets a new mesh's hits run past its end.
- **Closest hits list a prefix of the shade pass's layers.** Material hit
  shaders and the default hit list the scene records, the scene, and the
  ray-traced scene layers; the shade pass lists those, then the light table,
  the surfel GI, and the ray hits layers. `HitSurface`, the payload, stays
  declared once, in the ray-traced scene layer.
- **A new surfel's estimate starts from its prior.** `surfel_start` sets its
  rays and the rays its estimate holds to its prior, a parent's
  `SURFEL_PARENT_PRIOR_RAYS` or none, so its own rays outweigh what it copied as
  they arrive. It clears its guide and radial depth, so a recycled slot keeps
  nothing of the surfel it held before, and resets the state the grid, the
  validation, and the ray classes read: `listing`, `supported`, and `bounce`.
- **The guide's mass is the sum of its cells.** The integrate pass adds each
  cell's change as the cell stores it, after rounding to a half float, so the
  trace's draw never runs past the last cell.
- **The trace and the integrate pass agree on directions.** The trace writes the
  point of the square each ray left toward, and the integrate pass learns the
  guide and the radial depth at that point.
- **Each ray says which distribution drew it.** The trace sets
  `SURFEL_RAY_GUIDED` in the flags of every ray the guide drew, and the
  integrate pass counts only the rays without it toward the hit fraction, once
  each. Counting a guided ray, or any ray by its weight, scatters an enclosed
  room's hit fraction below one and lets the environment's reflections in. The
  shade pass only adds `SURFEL_RAY_BACK_FACE`.
- **The clearance stays within every cell's own tests.** `surfel_moment_clearance`
  reads a cell through `surfel_normalized_moments`, as `surfel_moment_visibility`
  does, and returns at most the distance where its Cantelli early out or its
  solve's zero-shadow branch stops holding. A clearance past either would light
  pixels behind a wall.
- **Votes only grow between the count pass's reads.** The resolve, the shade
  pass, the validation, and the integrate pass vote with `atomic_max`, and the
  resolve skips a vote it finds already cast as high. A pass that lowers a vote
  in between would make that skip drop votes.
- **Hit spawns stay bounded.** `surfel-shade` asks only for hits of rays whose
  surfel has a `bounce` of zero, once per cell a frame, and
  `surfel-allocate-hits` checks both shares before it takes a slot. The
  frame-wide stages run before the screen-space passes, so a hit spawn that
  could take every free slot would starve the surfaces the camera sees.
- **The sky reaches a pixel once, through a surfel.** Surfel light holds the
  environment's light, so under surfel GI material evaluation adds none, which
  the `indirect_diffuse` push constant tells it with the value
  `IndirectDiffuseSource::push_constant` gives surfel GI, and the shade pass
  adds none at hits. The spatial pass fills a pixel no surfel lights only with
  light other pixels know, or with none. A pass that adds the environment again
  counts the sky twice, and one that adds it where no surfel looked lights
  enclosed rooms with sky that walls hide.
- **The spatial pass feeds only material evaluation.** It reads the temporal
  history and writes `SSGI History`, which nothing in surfel GI reads. A pass
  that fed its output back into the history, a surfel, or a ray would compound
  its loss of energy every frame.
- **A bake without surfel GI carries none of its assets.**
  `VisibilityFeatures::excludes` leaves out the stems in `SURFEL_GI_PIPELINES`
  and `SURFEL_SHADING_SHADERS`, so a new `surfel-*` pipeline goes in the first
  and a new shader the pipeline manager assembles a pipeline from goes in the
  second. The hit sort and the default hit shader, which probe GI shares, stay
  while either technique is built in: their stems are in `RAY_HIT_PIPELINES`
  and `DEFAULT_HIT_SHADERS`. A stem left out of every list is baked for every
  project, and a platform whose shading language has no ray tracing rejects a
  ray tracing shader.
- **Host records match their layers.** Every struct the host sizes buffers
  with has a stride test.
