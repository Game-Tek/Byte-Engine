Read this page before you change surfel global illumination (GI): its stages,
its buffers, the hit shaders materials bake for it, or how it shares the
indirect diffuse images with SSGI. What an application can rely on is specified
under [surfel GI](/docs/reference/rendering#surfel-gi) in the rendering
reference, and the GHI's side of ray tracing is in the
[ray tracing design note](/docs/develop/rendering/ray-tracing).

Surfel GI follows [webgiya](https://github.com/jure/webgiya) by Jure Triglav,
which builds on "Global Illumination Based on Surfels" by Henrik Halén, Andreas
Brinck, Kyle Hayward, and Xiangshun Bei. The shaders that adapt webgiya's carry
its MIT notice, and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` lists them.

## The frame's stages

The first sink records the frame-wide stages after its light clusters and
before it draws the scene, so the GPU can run them alongside the raster passes,
which write nothing they read. It records them all or nothing: a frame either
records every one of them or none, because each stage leaves buffers in the
state the next one expects. Every sink that surfel GI
lights then records its own two stages inside `screen-space`.

| Stage | Pass | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `acceleration-structures` | Builds | Builds the bottom levels that fit this frame's budget, then the top level from this frame's instances. |
| `surfel-grid` | `surfel-count` | Ages each surfel, recycles the dead ones, counts the cells each live surfel overlaps, and asks for its rays. |
| | `surfel-scan-groups`, `-partials`, `-add` | One exclusive scan over every cell count and every surfel's wanted rays, which also grants the frame's rays. |
| | `surfel-scatter` | Lists each surfel in its cells, counting each cell's fill down to zero, and writes a record for each granted ray. |
| `surfel-trace` | `surfel-trace` | Draws each granted ray's direction from its surfel's guide or the cosine-weighted distribution, and traces it with the inline intersector. A miss writes its result. A hit is kept, with its sort key. |
| | `surfel-sort-scan`, `-scatter` | A counting sort that orders the hits by the material they hit. |
| | `surfel-shade` | A ray tracing pipeline: one invocation per sorted hit runs the hit's closest-hit shader with `invoke`, lights the surface it reports, and votes for the surfel whose light reaches it most. |
| `surfel-integrate` | `surfel-integrate` | Folds each surfel's ray results into its estimate, its guide, and its radial depth, measures how far its radial depth is clear, and rewrites its record. |
| `screen-space` | `surfel-resolve` | Lights each half-resolution pixel from the surfels of its cell that can see it, into the images SSGI writes, and votes on spawns and despawns. |
| | `surfel-allocate` | Turns each tile's spawn request into a surfel. |

`render_pass/surfel_gi.rs` creates the buffers and the stages, and
`ray_tracing.rs` keeps the shade pass's pipeline and its shader binding table
current.

## Rays take turns within a fixed budget

Each surfel asks for its rays in `surfel-count`, and the scan places every
surfel's wanted rays after the cell counts, in slot order. The budget grants a
window of `render.surfel-gi.ray-budget` wanted rays that starts where the last
frame's window ended and wraps around, so when surfels ask for more rays than
the budget, each surfel's rays still get their turn. The scatter, the trace, and
the integrate pass all find a ray's place in the window with the same formula:
the ray's index among the wanted rays, minus the window's start, wrapped around
the wanted rays.

The trace, the hit sort's scatter, and the shade pass dispatch one thread per
ray of the budget, and threads past the frame's rays or hits return. A fixed
dispatch needs no indirect arguments, and once surfels fill the budget, which
they do in steady state, few threads idle.

## Each surfel takes at most 32 cell entries

The cell lists keep `SURFEL_LISTING_LIMIT` entries, 32, for each pool slot.
`surfel-count` and `surfel-scatter` both stop listing a surfel once it has that
many, and both visit its cells in the same order: its finer cascade first, then
by z, y, and x. So a full pool's lists always fit, every count the scan reads
matches the entries the scatter writes, and the readers need no clamp. Each
pass keeps its own copy of the walk: one shared through a layout function, and
one that returns at the limit, both measured about 0.02 ms slower in
`surfel-grid` on Sponza at 1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4.

Run through the grid's own arithmetic, 4 million random surfels overlap 15
cells on average and 21 in cascade 0, and 0.8% of them overlap more than 32. A
search near the corners of cascade 0, where the grid lists surfels in cascade 1
too, found surfels that overlap 75. Those past the limit lose the cells the grid
visits last, along one edge of their disc, and pixels there see less coverage,
so they spawn surfels sooner. The limit costs 128 bytes a slot, 4 MiB at the
default capacity.

It replaced a shared budget of 8 entries a slot, which the lists outgrew once
the pool was about half full. The scatter dropped the entries past the budget
while the counts still promised them, so the cells the scan reaches last, those
of the outer cascades, lost their lists while their counts still held back
spawns and charged rent.

## Hits sort by material before they're shaded

The shade pass runs each hit's closest-hit shader through a visible function
table, and rays that call different shaders side by side cost several times
more than rays that call one; the [ray tracing design note](/docs/develop/rendering/ray-tracing#why-hits-are-sorted-before-theyre-shaded)
has the measurements. So the trace keys each hit by the material slot it hit,
which the geometry table and the frame's mesh record name, and the counting sort
orders the hits by that key. Materials that share a closest-hit shader still
sort into separate runs, which keeps the key independent of the published
pipeline.

## Hit groups keep their numbers

Each material whose bake built a closest-hit shader gets a hit group: the
shader with the specialization constants of the material's variant. Materials
with the same shader and constants share the group. Groups keep the number they
first got, so a pipeline built again with more groups runs every older group as
before, and group 0 runs `surfel-default-hit.besl`, a black occluder, for every
record whose material's group the published pipeline doesn't have yet.

`SurfelShading` requests the pipeline again once the last request settled and
groups arrived since, so a burst of materials makes a few requests instead of
one each. The published pipeline keeps shading until the next one publishes,
and a request that fails leaves its new groups running the default shader. The
shader binding table maps each hit record, one per geometry of each bottom
level, to its material's group, and is written again when the pipeline, the
records, or a material's group changes. The frame admits the meshes it draws
for the first time, which gives their geometries records, before it writes the
table, so the table holds the records of every instance the frame places.

## Light is stored pre-exposed

Every surfel's estimate and record hold pre-exposed light, as the lit image
does: multiplied by the exposure of the frame that stored it. Half floats can't
hold physical daylight, and the multiscale mean estimator's constants, such as
its firefly margin of 0.1, are tuned for light around one. The integrate pass
rescales each estimate by the ratio of this frame's exposure to the exposure the
pool last stored its light at, which the pipeline manager keeps for the pool, and
the shade pass divides the light it reads from last frame's records by that
same previous exposure.

## Surfels gather the sky

A ray that misses brings back the environment's light from its direction, as in
webgiya, so each surfel's light holds the sky it sees through every opening,
from the directions it sees it. The miss reads the environment's specular level
for roughness 2/7, whose blur keeps a small bright patch of sky from making the
estimate noisy, as webgiya's blurred level does. A hit reads the same light from
the surfels around it, so sky light that falls through an opening and off a wall
reaches surfels that see no sky. Under surfel GI, material evaluation adds no
environment light of its own, and each surfel's hit fraction only dims the
environment's reflections. The sky's light comes at the surfels' detail, without
normal maps, and follows a change to the environment as fast as the estimate
converges.

An earlier design left the sky to material evaluation: it added the
environment's irradiance around each pixel's normal times one minus the
surfels' hit fraction. That lost which part of the sky a surface sees, so a
wall that sees sky only high up through a roof got the light of a hemisphere
centered on the horizon. It also hung the whole sky on one number per surfel,
and in a dim gallery that number's error set neighbors visibly apart.

A pixel no surfel covers yet takes the environment's irradiance from the
resolve, as material evaluation lights it without surfel GI, until a surfel
spawns there. A hit where no surfel lies yet, such as a surface off the screen,
gets the suns alone: the sky it would see is unknown, and counting all of it
would leak light into closed rooms.

## The estimate keeps converging

The multiscale mean estimator moves its long mean toward each frame's average
only as fast as its catch-up allows, and while the long and short means agree
within a deviation, the catch-up falls to 1/256 of its variance-based blend:
at most 1/8192 a frame. A surfel's first 32 frames average evenly, and with
four rays a frame they leave an error about as large as all the sky light a dim
room gets, which the estimator alone would keep for thousands of frames. So the long
mean also moves at least as fast as an even average of the frames the estimate
holds, which stop growing at 200. An error that small then halves within about
a hundred frames, and a change the estimator notices still catches up at once.

## Rays follow each surfel's guide

Each surfel keeps a guide: 8 × 8 cells over its hemisphere, each holding the
light its rays found in that direction lately, times the direction's cosine.
The cells come from the concentric map of the unit square onto the disk, lifted
onto the hemisphere so that equal areas of the square cover equal solid angles.
A cell's density is then a constant, so the trace draws a cell by its share of
the guide and a uniform point inside it, and knows the direction's density
without a table.

Once a surfel is 16 frames old, the trace draws half of its rays from the guide
and the other half from the cosine-weighted distribution, so directions the
guide holds dark keep being sampled. Each ray's weight, the cosine-weighted
density over the mixture's density, turns the mixture back into the
cosine-weighted average the estimate expects. Webgiya steers 90% of the rays;
here half stay with the cosine-weighted distribution, which bounds every ray's
weight at 2 and leaves half the rays for the hit fraction, which counts only
them.

A miss brings back the environment's light from its direction, so the guide
learns where the sky shines through, and guided rays keep finding those
openings. The integrate pass moves the four cells around each ray's direction
toward the light the ray found, and keeps the guide's sum, `guide_mass`, which
the trace draws against.

## The hit fraction counts cosine rays

The weights keep the light's average right, but they vary: about 0.07 in the
guide's strongest cells and 2 outside them. A hit fraction weighed by them
scatters around its true value even when every ray hits. Material evaluation
dims reflections by one minus the hit fraction, clamped at zero, so in an
enclosed room the scatter's low half let the environment's reflection through.
In Sponza's south gallery, the lit image's surfel-sized patches followed
max(0, 1 − A), with a correlation of 0.71 to 0.90 over five runs, and not the
diffuse light.

So each ray's result says whether the guide drew it, and the hit fraction
averages only the rays drawn from the cosine-weighted distribution, each
counting once. In an enclosed room every one of them hits, and the fraction is
exactly one. The light still counts every ray with its weight.

The hit fraction stays unbiased. In Veach's multiple importance sampling, any
weights that sum to one wherever the integrand isn't zero, and that are zero
wherever their distribution can't draw, give an unbiased estimate. The hit
fraction's integrand is the visibility times cos θ / π, which is zero wherever
the cosine-weighted density is, so a weight of one on that distribution and zero
on the guide qualifies. The trace picks each ray's distribution independently of
where the ray goes, so given the picks, the cosine-weighted rays are plain
cosine-weighted samples: Veach's multi-sample model, whose variance is never
larger than the one-sample model's (Sbert, Havran, and Szirmay-Kalos, 2016).
Dividing by the cosine-weighted rays a frame traced, rather than by how many it
expected, keeps an enclosed room's fraction exactly one. A frame whose rays all
came from the guide, one frame in 16 with four rays, leaves the fraction as it
was; the picks don't depend on what the rays find, so skipping those frames
biases nothing.

Dividing the weighted hit fraction by the sum of the weights, Hesterberg's
weighted average (1995), would also read one when every ray hits. With four rays
a frame, though, its bias follows the guide, and the estimator's average over
frames doesn't remove it.

In the same gallery, the hit fraction now reads exactly one, and the lit image
follows the diffuse light. Its 32 × 32-pixel blocks changed by 73–88% of their
light between two captures 230 frames apart, and now change by 12%; the
gallery reads a sixth as bright, because the reflection it no longer gets was
most of its light.

## Radial depth hides surfels behind walls

A surfel's disc reaches every pixel near its center, including pixels on the
other side of a thin wall. So each surfel also keeps its radial depth: 4 × 4
cells over the same map, each holding the first four moments of how far its
rays went there, up to twice its radius. The resolve weighs each surfel's light
by the pixel's visibility from it, the Hamburger four-moment bound of moment
shadow mapping, as webgiya runs it. A cell no ray reached yet holds zero and
hides nothing. When every surfel around a pixel hides it, the pixel takes no
light, sky included, and a hit fraction of one, which also hides the
environment's reflections there.

Radial depth doesn't share the guide's map. The resolve maps a direction to its
cells for every pixel and surfel pair, so radial depth uses the hemisphere's
octahedral projection, which needs no trigonometry, and the integrate pass maps
each ray's direction to both. The resolve also skips the four-moment solve for a
pixel well nearer than a cell's mean distance, which Cantelli's inequality shows
the surfel sees, because the bound the solve finds can't exceed Cantelli's.

Most of the test's cost comes before the solve: turning each pixel's direction
into the surfel's frame, mapping it to cells, and reading them. So the integrate
pass also stores each surfel's clearance, the distance below which every cell a
ray reached clears a point alone, and the resolve sees a pixel nearer than that
without any of the test. A cell clears a point by Cantelli's inequality, or when
the point lies below the lower root of the cell's second orthogonal polynomial,
where some distribution with the cell's moments lies entirely beyond the point,
so the four-moment bound finds no shadow. Cantelli's bound alone clears little,
because one cell whose distances spread sinks it; the root clears up to about
the nearest surface a cell's rays hit. A blend of cells clears whatever all of
its cells clear, because its distribution mixes theirs.

Pixels on a surfel's own plane lie just below the point its rays leave from, so
they read the cells along its horizon. The shade pass weighs the surfels around
each hit by the same test, so light that reached one side of a wall doesn't
bounce off the other side; it reads at most 16 surfels a hit. On Sponza at
1024 × 1024 on an Apple M4, the test at hits adds about 0.04 ms to
`surfel-trace`, and the gallery's light stays within the spread between
captures of one build.

## Hits keep the surfels that light them

Pixels vote only for the surfels the camera sees, so a surfel off the screen
would age out even while it lights what other surfels' rays hit, such as a
ceiling lit through a doorway. The shade pass votes for the surfel whose light
reaches each hit most, which keeps the surfels the bounce light depends on
alive.

## Rules a change must keep

- **The frame-wide stages leave their counts at zero.** The scan resets each
  cell count as it reads it, the scatter counts each cell's fill down to zero,
  and the hit sort's scan resets the hit counts. A stage that skips this breaks
  the next frame.
- **Each surfel takes at most `SURFEL_LISTING_LIMIT` entries.** `surfel-count`
  and `surfel-scatter` visit a surfel's cells in the same order and stop at the
  same limit, and the cell entries hold the limit's entries for every pool slot.
  The resolve and the shade pass read their cell's range without a clamp, so a
  pass that lists a surfel in more cells, or entries sized below the capacity
  times the limit, reads and writes past the buffer. The limit in
  `render_pass/surfel_gi.rs` equals the one in `layouts/surfel-gi.besl`.
- **The ray window formula is the same everywhere.** `surfel-scatter`,
  `surfel-trace`, and `surfel-integrate` must agree on which ray is which.
- **A sort key fits 32 bits.** The material slot sits above 20 bits of rank, so
  the material limit stays at most 4,096 and the ray budget at most 2<sup>20</sup>.
  `SURFEL_HIT_KEYS` in `layouts/surfel-gi.besl` equals `MAX_MATERIALS`.
- **Records reach the table before their instances.**
  `SceneAccelerationStructures::admit` gives the meshes a frame draws for the
  first time their records and returns the record list the shader binding
  table is written from; `prepare` places the instances after. The shade pass's
  `invoke` calls the table entry a hit's record names without a bounds check, so
  a table written before admission lets a new mesh's hits run past its end.
- **Closest hits list a prefix of the shade pass's layers.** Material hit
  shaders and the default hit list the scene records, the scene, and the
  ray-traced scene layers; the shade pass lists those, then the surfel GI layer.
  `HitSurface`, the payload, stays declared once, in the ray-traced scene layer.
- **A new surfel's estimate starts over.** The allocation sets its count to zero,
  so its first frame of rays replaces the light it took from its parent, and
  clears its guide and radial depth, so a recycled slot keeps nothing of the
  surfel it held before.
- **The guide's mass is the sum of its cells.** The integrate pass adds each
  cell's change as the cell stores it, after rounding to a half float, so the
  trace's draw never runs past the last cell.
- **The trace and the integrate pass agree on directions.** The trace writes the
  point of the square each ray left toward, and the integrate pass learns the
  guide and the radial depth at that point.
- **Each ray says which distribution drew it.** The trace sets `guided` in every
  ray's result, and the integrate pass counts only the rays without it toward
  the hit fraction, once each. Counting a guided ray, or any ray by its weight,
  scatters an enclosed room's hit fraction below one and lets the environment's
  reflections in.
- **The clearance stays within every cell's own tests.** `surfel_moment_clearance`
  reads a cell through `surfel_normalized_moments`, as `surfel_moment_visibility`
  does, and returns at most the distance where its Cantelli early out or its
  solve's zero-shadow branch stops holding. A clearance past either would light
  pixels behind a wall.
- **Votes only grow between the count pass's reads.** The resolve and the shade
  pass vote with `atomic_max`, and the resolve skips a vote it finds already
  cast as high. A pass that lowers a vote in between would make that skip drop
  votes.
- **The sky reaches a pixel once.** Surfel light holds the environment's
  light, so under surfel GI material evaluation adds none, which the
  `indirect_diffuse` push constant tells it with the value
  `IndirectDiffuseSource::push_constant` gives surfel GI, and the shade pass
  adds none at hits. The resolve writes the environment's light for a pixel no
  surfel covers. A pass that adds the environment again counts the sky twice.
- **A bake without surfel GI carries none of its assets.**
  `VisibilityFeatures::excludes` leaves out the stems in `SURFEL_GI_PIPELINES`
  and `SURFEL_SHADING_SHADERS`, so a new `surfel-*` pipeline goes in the first
  and a new shader the pipeline manager assembles a pipeline from goes in the
  second. A stem left out of both is baked for every project, and a platform
  whose shading language has no ray tracing rejects a ray tracing shader.
- **Host records match their layers.** Every struct the host sizes buffers
  with has a stride test.
