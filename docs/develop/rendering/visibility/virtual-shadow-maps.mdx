Virtual shadow maps are the alternative to cascades for the first directional
light, chosen with `render.directional-shadows.technique`. Read this page before
you change them. What an application can rely on is specified under
[virtual shadow maps](/docs/reference/rendering#virtual-shadow-maps) in the
rendering reference. Cascades, and the cone and point maps, are described in
[Shadow maps](/docs/develop/rendering/visibility/shadow-maps); both techniques
share its selection of sun slots, its penumbra width and level choice, and its
small-caster cull.

## The design follows GPU Zen 3

Sun shadows implement *Virtual Shadow Maps* by Matej Sakmary, Jake Ryan, Justin
Hall, and Alessio Lustri, in *GPU Zen 3: Advanced Rendering Techniques*
(Black Cat Publishing, 2024), pages 319 to 336. The algorithm and its
constants come from the chapter's companion code, the Timberdoodle renderer at
commit `f5e9018826` (May 2024). Two later changes come from Sakmary's master's
thesis, *Virtual Shadow Maps* (Czech Technical University in Prague, 2025):

- Each page keeps the light-axis origin of the view it was drawn with, which
  replaced the chapter's per-level height offsets.
- Changed casters free their pages in a GPU pass instead of a CPU-built mask.

The engine departs from both sources in these places, each for the reason
given:

| Change | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| A receiver needs pages in its level and the next two coarser levels, within 16 texels of it. The chapter needs one page at one level. | The penumbra filter picks its level by the penumbra's width, which only the map can tell, and its blocker search and tent reach 16 texels. Without the coarser pages, a wide penumbra has nothing to read. |
| A receiver whose level lacks a page falls back to the next coarser level that holds it, and is lit when none does. The chapter's code reads such a page as lit. | A full pool leaves single pages out. Falling back keeps a shadow there, only blurrier. |
| The free-wrapped pass also clears each page's per-frame bits, and the cells pass clears each drawn page's dirty bit. The chapter's code clears them in passes of their own. | Fewer passes over the page table, with the same result. |
| A request the pool can't serve leaves its entry requested but unallocated. The chapter's code marks it with an allocation-failed bit. | Nothing reads that bit: an entry without `ALLOCATED` already tells the sampler to fall back. |
| Marking reads a page's entry before its atomic, and skips a page already visited this frame. The chapter elects one lane per page in each subgroup. | Most receivers then do no atomic at all, with no assumption about subgroup width. |
| Finding free pages appends to its two lists with plain atomics. The chapter's code counts each subgroup's pages with a ballot. | The lists' order doesn't matter, and the pass is small. |
| Invalidation compares each instance record's bounds and level word with the ones it stored, over the records the CPU rewrote. The thesis frees under a list of spheres the CPU builds. | The CPU already knows which records it rewrote, but not their bounds; the records hold those. |
| Each drawn page also gets min and max depth cells of 8 × 8 texels, and the blocker search and probes read them. **[own]** | The cascades read reduction-sampled pyramids, whose 2 × 2 footprint can cross a page edge. |
| Depth is stored as a fraction of 4,294,967,040 rather than the float's bits. | BESL has no bit cast. The code orders like the depth, so an atomic max still keeps the nearest. |

## Pages live in one pool

The sun's shadow is 16 levels, each a virtual map of 4,096 × 4,096 texels that
doubles the side of the one before it. Each level is cut into 32 × 32 pages of
128 × 128 texels. These GPU buffers hold the state, all in
`layouts/virtual_shadows.besl` and owned by `VirtualShadowMaps` in
`render_pass/virtual_shadows.rs`:

- **Page table:** 16,384 `u32` entries, 32 × 32 per level. An entry holds the
  physical page in bits 0 to 15, and the flags `ALLOCATED` (bit 31),
  `REQUESTED` (30), `DIRTY` (28), and `VISITED` (27).
- **Page owners:** one entry per physical page, holding `ALLOCATED` (bit 31),
  `VISITED` (29), and its page table slot.
- **Pool:** one `U32` storage image of physical pages, each texel a depth code.
- **Page origins:** one float per physical page, the near plane's position
  along the light when the page was drawn.
- **Page hierarchy:** per level, 1,365 entries in mips of 32 × 32 down to
  1 × 1, each the OR of the `DIRTY` flags below it, in the level's own page
  coordinates.
- **Page cells:** per physical page, 16 × 16 cells of the nearest and farthest
  depth of their 8 × 8 texels.
- **Caster bounds and levels:** per instance record, the world bounding sphere
  and level word the invalidation last saw.

Each level's view snaps to whole pages of its own world-fixed grid, so moving
the camera scrolls pages instead of moving texels. The table addresses pages
toroidally: grid page `p` sits in slot `p mod 32`. Grid coordinates carry a
bias of 2<sup>20</sup>, a multiple of 32, so they stay positive on the GPU.

## A frame keeps what it can

The first sink records the `virtual-shadow-maps` stage after its depth pyramid,
where cascades record their fit:

1. Free: frees the pages each level's scroll brought in, or every page when the
   sun turned, and clears every page's per-frame flags.
2. Invalidate: for each instance record the CPU rewrote since the stage last
   recorded, and each record that appeared or left, compares its world bounds
   and level word with the stored ones. When they differ, it frees the pages
   under the old and the new bounds at every level, and stores the new ones.
3. Mark: gives each receiver within the shadow distance its level and sets
   `REQUESTED` and `VISITED` on the pages it needs. The first requester of a
   page without memory appends an allocation request, and the first visitor of
   a page with memory marks its owner `VISITED`.
4. Find free: lists the free physical pages, then the ones nothing visited
   this frame, and writes the indirect dispatch arguments.
5. Allocate: gives each request a free page, then an unvisited page, taken
   from the slot that held it. A request past both waits for a later frame. It
   records the page's origin.
6. Clear: zeroes each new page and sets `DIRTY`.
7. Hierarchy: reduces the `DIRTY` flags into the page hierarchy.
8. Draw: draws the casters of every level into its dirty pages.
9. Cells: writes the cells of each new page and clears its `DIRTY`.

A page nothing changes draws once and stays, so a still camera over a still
scene draws nothing at all.

## Depth goes through atomics

The draw is one render pass with no attachments, 4,096 texels on a side. It
goes through `record_meshlet_dispatches` with `OcclusionPhase::Pages`, which
makes the shared task shader keep an instance or meshlet only where its sphere
covers a dirty page of the level: it reads the hierarchy mip where the
sphere's page rectangle spans at most two entries a side. A batch whose levels
have no dirty page at the hierarchy's root returns before it reads an
instance. The page hierarchy
sits in the occlusion layer for that reason. `virtual-shadow-mesh.besl` passes
each vertex's level and its position in the level, in texels and depth, and
`virtual-shadow-fragment.besl` looks the page up and, when it's allocated and
dirty, writes the depth code with an atomic max into the pool. Writing only
dirty pages is what lets clean pages keep their depth through the same draw.

With no depth attachment there is no early depth test, so every covered
fragment of a dirty page runs. Culling by the page hierarchy keeps that work to
the casters over new pages. Masked casters draw through the same pipelines,
solid.

## Pages keep their origin

Each level's view follows the camera along the light continuously, so a
cached page would read wrong depths once the camera climbed or descended. Each
page keeps the near plane it was drawn with, and a stored depth shifts by the
difference between that and the current near plane, over the 2,000 m depth
range, before it's compared. Under an orthographic view, that one number is
the whole of the thesis's stored translation: the other two axes snap to
pages.

## Sampling reads through the page table

`sample_virtual_shadow` in `sun-visibility.besl` resolves the opaque pixels,
with the helpers in `layouts/virtual_shadows.besl`. It keeps the cascades'
structure: a receiver's level, a search level, the blocker search and probes,
then the penumbra level and fade. The filter reads every texel under its tent
one at a time, through the entries of the 2 × 2 pages its reach can cross. The
cascades' block gathers are a later optimization.

Transparent receivers and the fog march don't sample the map yet: the
virtual sun's light record sets its `virtual_shadow` flag, and both skip the
sun's shadow where it's set.

## Levels share the cascades' cull and filter

Each level skips small casters as a cascade does, through the
`minimum_caster_extent` push constant at 2 × 32 / 4,096; see
[Cascades skip small casters](/docs/develop/rendering/visibility/shadow-maps#cascades-skip-small-casters).
The penumbra width, tent, and level fade follow the cascades' filter, see
[Sun penumbrae read every texel under their filter](/docs/develop/rendering/visibility/shadow-maps#sun-penumbrae-read-every-texel-under-their-filter).
Levels are exactly twice as coarse as the one before, so the choice between
neighbors needs none of the caveats that receivers fitting brings.

Each level also draws an instance at the coarsest detail level its texels
show, as a cascade does: the page draw pushes the level's 4,096 texels as
`view_texels`; see
[Shadow views pick their own level](/docs/develop/rendering/visibility/meshlet-dispatch#shadow-views-pick-their-own-level).

## A change must keep these rules

- Only allocated, dirty pages take depth writes. A write to a clean page
  corrupts its cached depth for every later frame.
- Every change to an instance record that moves or changes its caster lands in
  the range the invalidation compares. A missed change leaves pages drawn
  without it, which no later frame repairs.
- A page's origin is written with its allocation and read with every sample of
  it. A page sampled with another origin shows its shadow shifted along the
  light.
- Every page a receiver's blocker search and filter can read is needed, at the
  receiver's level and the two coarser ones. A filter that reaches further must
  widen the needed square with it.
- A level holds a receiver only when all its needed pages hold depth, so the
  filter never reads a page without depth.
- Freeing a page clears its page table entry and its owner together. An owner
  left pointing at a reused page frees the new page's entry.
- Cascades left as they are. The two techniques share sun slots, the
  penumbra rules, and the caster cull, and a change to one of those must keep
  both techniques' tests passing.

The CPU tests in `virtual_shadows.rs` guard the level placement and the
changed-record range, and the VM tests in `tests/virtual_shadows.rs` guard
scrolling, allocation, and the page hierarchy. `visibility_assets_lower_to_the_platform_shader_language`
compiles every pass, and `task_main_skips_casters_narrower_than_the_minimum_extent`
guards the caster cull.
