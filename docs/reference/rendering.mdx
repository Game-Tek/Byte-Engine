The renderer draws each window from a scene pipeline followed by post-scene
passes. This page specifies the objects an application creates, the camera,
how pipelines and passes compose, every pass the engine ships with, and the
contract of the visibility pipeline. The design notes behind it are under
[Rendering](/docs/develop/rendering) in Contribute, and the lights are in the
[lighting reference](/docs/reference/lighting).

## Objects

| Object | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Window | A named render surface with an extent, optional decorations, and optional [cursor capture](/docs/reference/input#cursor-capture), with one camera attached. Each window becomes one sink, numbered in creation order. |
| Camera | View settings created on the world with a transform, which the renderer follows. |
| Sink | The per-frame destination the renderer builds from a window's extent and camera: the view, the extent, the exposure scale, and the frame interval. |
| View | Projection and view matrices with near, far, vertical field of view, and aspect ratio, perspective or orthographic. |
| Renderable mesh | An entity's geometry: a baked mesh by ID, a generated mesh, a sphere, or a box. A generated mesh may name its material; otherwise it takes the pipeline's default. |
| Environment | A baked environment map, optionally scaled to deliver a given illuminance. Without one, the visibility pipeline uses an opaque black fallback. |
| Fog, lights, particle emitters | Specified with their own pages: [Light a scene](/docs/use/lighting), [lighting](/docs/reference/lighting), [particles](/docs/reference/particles). The newest fog wins. |
| Debug mesh | A retained wireframe sphere, box, capsule, segment, or circle with a color, tested against scene depth or drawn over it. |

Scene color is linear HDR in the packed `RGBu11u11u10` format. Tone-mapping
passes write 16-bit float display color. The window's swapchain is `BGRAsRGB`.

## Camera

| Field | Default | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field of view | 45° | Vertical, in degrees, above 0 and below 180. |
| Exposure | 0 stops | The exposure scale is 2 to the power of the stops, within ±64. |
| Near and far planes | 0.1 and 100 | Fixed. |
| Projection | Perspective | Sinks always use a perspective view. |
| F-number | ∞ | Above 0. ∞ is a pinhole, which keeps every depth in focus. |
| Focus distance | 10 m | Above 0. The depth that's in focus. |
| Sensor height | 24 mm | Above 0. |
| Aspect ratio | | Stored but not read; the aspect ratio comes from the window's extent. |

The camera's position and orientation come from its shown transform. Up is
+Y, unless the view direction is within 1 percent of vertical, when +Z is used.

### Lens

The camera is a thin lens. Depth is the distance from the camera along its
view direction.

| Quantity | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Focal length F | h / (2 tan(θ / 2)), for sensor height h and vertical field of view θ. |
| Aperture diameter A | F / N, for f-number N. |
| Focus distance S | The camera's focus distance, held to at least F + 1 mm. |
| Circle of confusion c | The signed diameter, in pixels, of the disc that a point at depth z spreads over: c = A · F / (S − F) · (1 − S / z) · H / h, for a sink H pixels tall. It's 0 at the focus distance, positive behind it, and negative in front of it. |
| Depth of the background | Where depth is 0, z is the far plane. |

### Lens sampling

While an enabled pass asks for lens samples, such as the depth-of-field
reference, the sink's view moves each frame to a point of the camera's
aperture. Scene pipelines and every post-scene pass of that sink see the moved
view.

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Aperture | A disc perpendicular to the view direction, centered on the camera, with a diameter of A · S / (S − F). |
| Sample on frame k | The point at √u of the disc's radius and at angle 2πv, for u = frac(0.5 + 0.7548776662 k) and v = frac(0.5 + 0.5698402910 k), where k counts the renderer's frames. |
| Projection | Sheared so that every point at the focus distance shows where the camera's own view shows it. Depth is unchanged. |
| Result | A point at depth z moves by c(z) pixels between opposite edges of the aperture. |

## Shown transforms

Every frame shows each entity's transform at the frame's
[display time](/docs/reference/time#loops). Scene pipelines, lights, cameras,
and particle emitters all follow the shown transform. Poses aren't blended;
animation steps once per frame.

| Term | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Sample | A transformation update read during a simulation step. Its stamp is the end time of that step. |
| Placement | A transformation update read outside a simulation step. It's shown at once and ends any motion in progress. |
| First update | A handle's first update is shown at once, sample or placement. |
| Span | The time from the handle's previous stamp to the sample's stamp, at most 250 ms. A sample after a placement spans one step. |
| Segment | Starts at the sample's stamp less one step, from the transform shown at that time, and reaches the sample one span later. Between the two, position and scale move linearly and orientation turns along the shorter arc. |
| Same step | A later sample in the same step replaces the earlier one's target and keeps its segment. |
| At rest | Past the end of its segment, a handle stays at its latest sample until its next update. |

| Source | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Publishes every step | Shown between its two latest samples by the blend factor, one step behind. |
| Publishes every _n_ steps | Moves without stopping between samples and is shown _n_ steps behind, as long as _n_ steps last at most 250 ms. |
| Starts moving after a rest | Its first segment lasts up to 250 ms; the next sample's segment starts from wherever that one reached. |

## Pipelines and passes

| Concept | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Scene pipeline | Owns scene state and per-sink resources and records one command per sink per frame. Several can be registered; the default setup registers the visibility pipeline and particles. |
| Render pass | Sink-local post-processing that reads `main` and usually replaces it. Registered for every sink. |
| Scene background | A pass the scene pipeline records after opaque and before transparent surfaces, such as the sky. A later registration replaces an earlier one. |
| Pass state | Enabled or bypassed, shared by name across sinks, set in code or by the `render.pass.<name>` parameter. A bypassed pass that replaced `main` has `main` forwarded in its place. |

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Registration | Post-scene passes and the scene background are registered before the first window. |
| Order per sink | Every scene pipeline in registration order, then every post-scene pass in registration order, then a copy to the swapchain only when no post-scene pass exists. |
| Terminal pass | The last post-scene pass must create the main render target, which is the swapchain. |
| Targets | A pass declares named, sink-scoped targets: full-size, scaled by a divisor, persistent, or history targets whose previous frame can be read. Reading a target that nothing creates fails at setup. |
| Clears | A node that records nothing has its first-use targets cleared; one that records has them discarded. |
| Frame-wide work | Detail levels, shadow views, and exposure follow the first sink. |
| Swapchain | FIFO presentation with 2 frames in flight. |

parameter use the name in this table. The default setup installs the visibility
pipeline, particles, and a 1920 by 1080 window, and no post-scene pass, so the
scene reaches the swapchain through the copy.

### Depth of field

The `depth-of-field` pass blurs each pixel p of `main` by the camera's lens.
The terms use the circle of confusion c of the [lens](#lens), r for the
distance in pixels from p to a point s of the image, and b<sub>p</sub> and
b<sub>s</sub> for the blur diameters at p and s.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum blur diameter M | 64 pixels, or the pass's maximum when it has one, held to 1 to 64 pixels. |
| Minimum blur diameter m | 0, or the pass's minimum when it has one, held to 0 to M. |
| Blur diameter b | The sign of c times max(d, m), where d is \|c\| when \|c\| is at most M / 2, and otherwise M / 2 + M / 2 × tanh((\|c\| − M / 2) / (M / 2)), which stays below M. A c of 0 counts as positive. |
| Reach of s | e = \|b<sub>s</sub>\| / 2. When b<sub>s</sub> > b<sub>p</sub>, s lies behind p, and e is at most \|b<sub>p</sub>\| / 2. |
| Coverage | clamp(e − r + 0.5, 0, 1). |
| Density | 1 / max(π b<sub>s</sub><sup>2</sup> / 4, 1). |
| Gathered | The mean of the colors around p, each weighted by coverage × density, in scene-linear light with each channel held to 65,504. |
| Ring share | The part of the gathered weight that points beyond 3.5 pixels of p carry. |
| Result | The gathered color moved toward the mean of the 3 × 3 pixels around p, by p's ring share. In that mean each pixel weighs 1, 2, or 1 along each axis, times its own ring share. |
| Sampling | Every pixel within 3.5 pixels of p is a point. Beyond, 16 points per ring stand for the ring's area, at rings whose bounds grow by 1.39 times from 3.5 pixels. Each pixel turns every ring by its own angle, up to a sixteenth of a turn, and places each ring's points at its own distance between the ring's bounds, spread evenly over the ring's area; both come from a fixed 64 × 64 blue-noise pattern tiled over the image, the same in every frame. Each ring is read from the image or an average of it whose texels are 2, 4, or 8 pixels wide, the widest no wider than the points' spacing. Each average takes 2 × 2 texels of the one before, weighing each by min(\|b\| / w, 1) for texels w pixels wide, or evenly where every weight is 0, and averages b the same way. Rings that no blur near p can reach are skipped. |
| Pinhole | A lens whose c is 0 everywhere, such as one with an f-number of ∞, leaves `main` unchanged when m is 0, and blurs every pixel by m otherwise. |

### Color correction

Color correction adjusts the scene-linear picture before tone mapping. It
reads only each pixel's own color. Its steps run in the order of the table.

| Step | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Input | Scene-linear sRGB with a D65 white. Alpha passes through. |
| Exposure | Every channel times 2<sup>exposure</sup>. |
| White balance | A von Kries adaptation in CAT16 cone space from the white W(temperature, tint) to D65, with both whites at luminance 1, then scaled so a neutral grey keeps its luminance. |
| White `W(T, t)` | The Planckian white of temperature T in CIE 1960 *uv*, from Krystek's 1985 approximation, moved by the *uv* offset that takes the 6,504 K Planckian white to D65, then 0.05 × t along the locus's unit normal toward the green side, which is higher *v*. W(6,504 K, 0) is D65. |
| Working space | ACEScg, through the D65-to-D60 adapted matrix of the ACES passes. Channels below 0 are held to 0, then encoded as ACEScct. |
| Lightness `L` | (max + min) / 2 of the three ACEScct channels. Each channel's offset is its value minus L. |
| Tone position | u = (L − black) / (white − black), where black = 0.0729055 (ACEScct of 0) and white = 1.4679963 (ACEScct of 65,504); p = 0.2442012, u at ACEScct 0.4135884 of mid grey 0.18; t = 0.5908968, u at ACEScct 0.8972603 of 64. |
| Shadows | For 0 < u < p, u becomes u + k u (1 − u / p)<sup>2</sup>, where k = 2 × shadows for shadows ≥ 0 and k = shadows below 0. |
| Highlights | For p < u < t, with y = (u − p) / (t − p), u becomes u + 3 × highlights × (t − p) × y<sup>2</sup> (1 − y)<sup>2</sup>. |
| Contrast | With u after shadows and highlights, for u ≤ p, f(u) = p (u / p)<sup>contrast</sup>; for p < u < 1, f(u) = 1 − (1 − p) ((1 − u) / (1 − p))<sup>contrast</sup>; at u ≥ 1, f(u) = u. L becomes black + (white − black) × f(u). Each channel keeps its offset from L. |
| Split tone | Each channel c becomes c + n × (m<sub>h</sub> × H − m<sub>s</sub> × S) × D. The masks are m<sub>s</sub> = 16 y<sup>2</sup> (1 − y)<sup>2</sup> with y = (L − 0.0729055) / (0.4135884 − 0.0729055) between black and mid grey, and m<sub>h</sub> = 16 y<sup>2</sup> (1 − y)<sup>2</sup> with y = (L − 0.4135884) / (0.7831050 − 0.4135884) between mid grey and 16 (ACEScct 0.7831050); each is 0 outside its range. H = amount × min(1, 1 + balance) and S = amount × min(1, 1 − balance). The protection n = 1 / (1 + (C / 0.1)<sup>2</sup>), where C is the largest minus the smallest ACEScct channel. |
| Split direction `D` | The ACEScct offsets log2(c<sub>i</sub> / 0.18) / 17.52 of the ACEScg color c that has mid grey's Oklab lightness, Oklab chroma 0.05, and Oklab hue h, less their mean weighted by ACEScg luminance (0.2722287, 0.6740818, 0.0536895). |
| Saturation | L and the offsets are taken again from the split-toned channels. Each channel c becomes q + saturation × (c − q), around the pivot q = L + density × the largest offset. |
| Output | ACEScct decoded to ACEScg, converted to linear sRGB with the inverse of the input matrix, and held to 0 and above. |

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Exposure | Stops, any value. +1 doubles the light, −1 halves it. It adds to the camera's exposure. |
| Temperature | The color temperature, in kelvins, of the light that shows as neutral. A lower value turns the picture bluer, a higher one warmer. |
| Tint | The white's distance from the Planckian locus, from −1 to 1. Positive values neutralize a greener light and turn the picture magenta; negative values turn it green. |
| Shadows | From −1 to 1. Positive values brighten the shadows, steepening the curve just above black up to 3 times: at 1, a tone 4.5 stops below mid grey rises 1.7 stops. Negative values deepen them, until at −1 the curve is flat at black and the same tone falls 1.2 stops. Black, mid grey, and everything brighter stay in place. |
| Highlights | From −1 to 1. Positive values brighten the tones from mid grey to 64, most at 4.25 stops above mid grey, which 1 raises by 1.6 stops; negative values pull them down by as much. The change fades out smoothly at both ends, so mid grey and everything past 64 stay in place. |
| Split hue | The Oklab hue h, in degrees, that the highlights move toward. The shadows move toward the opposite hue. About 55 gives orange highlights and teal shadows. |
| Split amount | From 0 to 1. At 1, a grey at the peak of either mask moves by about 0.05 in Oklab chroma, at the peaks 3 stops below and 3.25 stops above mid grey. Black, mid grey, and tones past 16 keep their color. The offsets sum to 0 under ACEScg luminance, so a tinted grey keeps its brightness to first order. |
| Split balance | From −1 to 1. Negative values keep the shadow tint and weaken the highlight tint, down to none at −1; positive values do the opposite. |
| Split protection | Built in. A grey takes the whole tint; a color whose ACEScct channels spread by 0.1 (about 1.75 stops) takes half, so skin and saturated colors keep their own hue. |
| Contrast | The curve's slope at mid grey. Above 1 it darkens the shadows and brightens the highlights, rolling off toward black and toward 65,504 so nothing clips; below 1 it flattens them. Mid grey never moves. |
| Saturation | 0 removes all color, 1 keeps it, and higher values strengthen it. A pixel whose channels are equal never changes. |
| Density | Where saturation takes its light from. At 0 channels move around L, so stronger color is brighter in its main channel. At 1 they move around the largest channel, so stronger color only lowers the others, as more dye does in film, and weaker color raises them toward it. Values between blend the two pivots. |

### Film grain

Film grain imitates the grain of scanned film stock on the display-encoded
picture. Each frame draws new grain. Grain changes the picture's density, so
every channel of a pixel scales by the same factor, and dark and bright areas
take the same relative grain.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Result | Each color channel times e<sup>a × z(L) × g</sup>, held to 0 and above. Alpha passes through. At a level of 0 or below, or while bypassed, the picture passes through unchanged. |
| Luma `L` | 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B of the display-encoded color, held between 0 and 1. |
| Noise | White noise per pixel and frame: the sum of four uniform draws minus 2, so mean 0 and variance 1/3. The noise continues past the image's edges, so edge pixels take grain like any other. |
| Narrow blur `N` | The noise blurred by a Gaussian of standard deviation σ = max(size × H / 2160, 0.05) pixels, where H is the sink's height, cut off at a radius of min(⌈2.5 σ⌉, 8) pixels, with weights that sum to 1. |
| Cells | The image divided into 4 × 4 pixel blocks from its top-left corner. A cell's value is the mean of its block's noise. |
| Wide blur `W` | The cells blurred by a Gaussian of standard deviation √(max((8.4 σ)² − 3.92, 0)) / 4 cells, cut off at a radius of min(⌈2.5 × that⌉, 16) cells, then filtered bilinearly between cell centers at each pixel. Below 0.3 cells the blur is left out, and the cells are only filtered. |
| Grain `g` | (N − 0.95 W) scaled to unit variance on a field of uniform color. |
| Amplitude `a` | level × 0.08505 × r. The factor r is 1 for a sink 2,160 pixels tall or taller. For a shorter sink, it's the share of the grain's standard deviation that's left when a 2,160-pixel picture is downscaled to H pixels with a Hermite filter widened by 2,160 / H, a widening held to at most 11. Fine grain loses more than coarse grain. |
| Zone weight `z` | Each zone's weight is max(0, 1 + gain), at luma 0 (Black), 0.13 (Shadow), 0.30 (Mid), 0.60 (High), and 1 (White). Between two zones the weight blends with a smooth step, 3t² − 2t³. Every weight is divided by the weight at luma 0.5, at least 0.05, so z is 1 at mid grey whatever the gains. |
| Level | 1 makes grain on a 50 % grey card as strong as on scans of real 35 mm, 16 mm, and 8 mm stock. |
| Size | The narrow blur's standard deviation in pixels of a 2,160-pixel-tall picture. About 0.4 matches 35 mm, 1.2 matches 16 mm, and 2.7 matches 8 mm. |
| Gain | Any value. −1 or below removes the zone's grain, 0 keeps it as strong as at mid grey, and 3 makes it four times as strong. |

### Bloom

Bloom moves a share of every pixel's light into a faint, wide tail around it,
and halation moves a share of each channel into a halo of a set radius. Neither
adds light: a field of one brightness leaves bloom unchanged, and a highlight
keeps the light it doesn't spread. There's no threshold, so every pixel spreads
light, and the tail and halo show only where they're brighter than the pixels
they fall on. Each channel's tail and halo come from that channel's light alone.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Result | For each channel c, (1 − S<sub>c</sub>) × scene + the sum over levels k of w<sub>k,c</sub> × level k, in scene-linear light after exposure, held to 65,504. S<sub>c</sub> = s + h<sub>c</sub> is the sum of the channel's weights. Where every S<sub>c</sub> is 0, the scene passes through unchanged. |
| Scatter `s` | The share of each pixel's light that moves into the tail, from 0 to 1, the same for every channel. |
| Halation `h` | The share of each channel's light that moves into its halo, per red, green, and blue channel, from 0 to 1 − s. |
| Levels | Level 0 is half the sink's resolution, and each later level is half the one before. Level k is the source blurred by a kernel of total weight 1 that reaches twice as far as level k − 1's, and one of its texels spans 2<sup>k + 1</sup> pixels. |
| Level weight w<sub>k,c</sub> | s × r<sup>k</sup> / the sum of r<sup>j</sup> over every level j, plus h<sub>c</sub> × g<sub>k,c</sub> / the sum of g<sub>j,c</sub> over every level j. The tail's and the halo's weights each sum to 1 before their share scales them. |
| Tail exponent `p` | Sets r = 2<sup>2 − p</sup>, so the tail falls off as about 1 / distance<sup>p</sup>. At 2, every level carries the same share; at 3, each level carries half the share of the one before. |
| Halation radius | The halo's radius, per channel, as a share of the sink's height. It sets g<sub>k,c</sub> = e<sup>−2 (k − m)²</sup>, where m = log<sub>2</sub>(radius in pixels / 2), held between 0 and the last level: the halo centers on the level whose texels span its radius and reaches about half a level to either side. |
| Source | The scene with every channel held between 0 and the maximum brightness. Level 0 averages 2 × 2 blocks of pixels in groups of four. Within a block, each pixel weighs 1 / (1 + M) against the others, and within a group each block weighs 1 / (1 + M) by its average, where M is the largest channel. A single bright pixel therefore can't flicker the tail, and where every pixel is alike the weights cancel. |

Rendering on demand, set by the `render-on-demand` parameter, renders a frame
only when a window, camera, or pass state changed, a pass asked for one, or a
screenshot is pending. Scene changes don't count, so an application that moves
cameras or objects requests a redraw itself.

| Materials, bindless textures | 1,024 each |
| Texture slots per material | 16 |
| Lights | 1,024; the rest are left out with one warning |
| Shadowed lights | 4 directional with 4 cascades each, 16 cone, 16 point |
| Light clusters | 16 by 8 by 24 |

A renderable is drawn only once its mesh's coarsest detail level, every
material, and every texture are resident.

A material texture is resident once its coarse mips are: the mips from the
first one whose larger side is at most 128 texels. Until its full mip chain
loads, it samples as though its finer mips were missing, so a surface that
needs them looks blurred. A texture with no mips larger than 128 texels, or no
mip that small, loads whole. Lightmaps and IES profiles always load whole.

A skinned primitive instance that receives a pose takes its own vertices out of
the geometry vertex capacity, as many as its primitive has, for as long as it
keeps receiving poses. It gives them back when it's removed. When the capacity
can't hold them, the instance draws in its bind pose and the pipeline logs one
error for it.

| Detail level rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Allowed error | One pixel times `render.lod.bias`, converted to world units from the first sink's projection. |
| Instance error | The level's error per distance times the straight-line distance from the camera to the nearest point of the bounding sphere, plus its constant error, divided by the largest model axis. Turning the camera never switches levels. |
| Wanted level | The coarsest level whose error is within the allowed error; the finest otherwise. An instance around the camera, with zero scale, or without a finite error wants full detail. |
| Drawn level | The wanted level when it's resident; otherwise the mesh's finest resident level. |
| Shadows | Draw the same level as the camera. |
| Ray tracing | Rays hit each primitive's coarsest level. |

| Detail level residency rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Base level | Each primitive's coarsest level loads with its mesh and stays resident while the mesh does. |
| Finer levels | Each primitive holds at most one finest resident level R, and every level from R down to its coarsest, with no gaps. Primitives of one mesh hold their levels independently. |
| Wanted level | A primitive's wanted level is the finest level any of its instances wants this frame. |
| Loading | While a primitive's wanted level is finer than R, its levels from R − 1 down load and replace the finer levels it held before, one level at a time. A mesh loads the primitives that want more together, at most one such load at a time. |
| Release | When a primitive's finer levels don't fit the geometry capacity, the primitive whose finer levels no instance has wanted for the longest releases them all and goes back to its base level, and the load tries again once that room frees. Levels an instance wanted in the last frame, and base levels, are never released. |

| Directional shadow rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Cascades | Four, splitting the range from the camera to `render.directional-shadows.distance` by the split blend between logarithmic and even. |
| Fitting | With receivers fitting, each cascade shrinks to the surfaces the camera sees, after two small depth passes. With frustum fitting, it covers the whole slice without them. |

### Shadow maps

Every shadow-casting light draws from one memory budget,
`render.shadow-maps.budget-mb`, 128 MiB by default:

| Light | Shadow map | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The first directional light | Four cascades of 2,048 texels per side by default, 16-bit depth | 32 MiB at the default resolution |
| A cone light | One map of its size class, 16-bit depth | 32 KiB to 8 MiB |
| A point light | A cube map of six faces of its size class, 16-bit depth | 192 KiB to 48 MiB |

A cone map or point cube face takes one of five sizes, its size class:

| Size class | Texels per side | Cone map | Point cube map |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 128 | 32 KiB | 192 KiB |
| 1 | 256 | 128 KiB | 768 KiB |
| 2 | 512 | 512 KiB | 3 MiB |
| 3 | 1,024 | 2 MiB | 12 MiB |
| 4 | 2,048 | 8 MiB | 48 MiB |

A light's span is its shadow range's projected diameter, in pixels, in the sink
where it looks largest. A light whose range contains a sink's camera spans that
sink's larger side. A cone light needs the span in texels per side, and a point
light half the span. The light takes the smallest class with at least that many texels, up to class 4. A light
moves to a larger class as soon as it needs one, and to a smaller class only once
it needs fewer than three quarters of that class's texels.

The first directional light is placed first, then cone and point lights in
order of the screen area they cover, each at the cost of its size class, then
any other directional lights. A light that doesn't fit stays lit without
shadows, with one warning in the log. At most 4 directional, 16 cone, and 16 point lights hold shadow maps at once.

### Shadow map updates

A shadow map isn't drawn again every frame. Each frame draws a set of updates
chosen within an update budget, `render.shadow-maps.update-texels`, 16,777,216
texels by default. One update draws one of these:

| Update | Texels |
| --- | --- |
| One cascade of a directional light | The cascade resolution squared, 4,194,304 at 2,048 texels per side |
| One cone map | Its size class's texels per side squared |
| One face of a point light's cube map | Its size class's texels per side squared |

Every frame draws cascades 0 and 1 of every shadowed directional light. Their
texels count against the budget first, even when they exceed it. Every other
update is a candidate: cascades 2 and 3, cone maps, and point cube faces.

Candidates are ordered in three tiers:

1. Maps that haven't been drawn yet.
2. Maps whose view changed since they were drawn: the light moved, turned, or
   changed its shadow range, or, for a cascade, the camera or the sun moved it.
3. Every other map.

Within a tier, candidates are ordered by age times importance, highest first.
Age is the number of frames since the map was drawn. Importance is the largest
number of sink pixels the light's shadow range covers in any sink, and for a
cascade, the pixel count of the largest sink. Candidates draw in that order
while their texels fit in what's left of the budget. A candidate that doesn't
fit is skipped, and later candidates can still draw. When no candidate fits,
the first one draws anyway.

Until a map has been drawn, its light is lit without that shadow. A map that
isn't drawn this frame keeps the view it was drawn with, and receivers sample
it with that view. A receiver that a cascade's drawn view doesn't hold uses the
next coarser cascade that holds it, and gets no shadow from that light when none
does. A new map, a map in a new size class, and every map of an image that
changed size count as not yet drawn.

A light keeps its map, at the same place in its class's image, for as long as it
stays selected in the same size class. A light that changes class gets a new
map. The image of each class grows when the class needs more maps than it holds,
and shrinks only when the budget needs its memory. An image that changes size
loses every map it holds.

### Materials

The pipeline accepts a material whose render model is `Visibility` with the
`MaterialEvaluation` pass, which is what every `.bema` file and imported model
bakes to. Its surface code is generated from the material's
[BEMX graph](/docs/reference/bemx): into the material's own evaluation shader
in `pipelines` mode, and into the shader of its phase in `uber` mode.

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Alpha mode | Opaque, masked with a cutoff, or blended. Blended materials draw in the transparent phase. |
| Double-sided | Draws without back-face culling. |
| Variables | Programs that differ only in textures or dynamic values share one pipeline and one dispatch; each distinct set of static scalar and vector values takes its own. Texture variables take slots in declaration order, up to 16. Dynamic values number at most 16384 in all, counting every loaded material's and every entity's own. |
| Layers | Every material evaluation shader takes the scene, visibility buffer, and material evaluation layers, and every closest-hit shader the scene and ray-traced scene layers. |

`render.material-evaluation` chooses how material evaluation runs. Both modes
shade every pixel with the same surface and lighting:

| Mode | Shaders | Work per phase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pipelines` | One evaluation shader per material program, specialized per set of static values | The material prepasses list each pipeline's pixels, then each pipeline shades its list. |
| `uber` | One shader per phase that holds every material variant in the bake as one arm | One pass over the sink in 8 × 8-pixel tiles, without material prepasses. |

| Uber rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Arms | One per distinct program, set of static values, and phase. Variants that differ only in textures or dynamic values share an arm. |
| Coverage | A material is drawn once the published shader of its phase holds its arm. A development build rebakes the shader when a material baked after it arrives; a release build logs one error for each material missing from the bake and doesn't draw it. |
| Background | The opaque pass writes zero to `Lit` and `Diffuse Radiance History` at every pixel no opaque surface covers. |
| Baked shaders | Material resources keep their graph instead of an evaluation shader. Every bake assembles both phases' shaders last, from every material variant in the store; they have no asset of their own. Changing the mode needs a forced rebake. |

The evaluation shader writes the graph's surface into these properties. A surface
input the graph leaves out takes its [BEMX default](/docs/reference/bemx#surface),
and a graph without a normal keeps the geometric one:

| Property | Type | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `albedo` | Four half floats: the base colour, then the opacity, which is alpha in the transparent phase | 0.8, 0.8, 0.8, 1 |
| `normal` | Three half floats in tangent space | 0, 0, 1, meaning no normal map |
| `metalness` | Half float | 0 |
| `roughness` | Half float | 0.5 |
| `occlusion` | Half float, multiplying indirect diffuse light | 1 |
| `emission` | Three half floats, added to the diffuse light | 0, 0, 0 |

The surface's world-space normal is converted to the tangent frame. A BEMX
`image` node samples its texture at the
vertex UV with the derivatives the pipeline computed. Writing a normal or
reading the tangent or bitangent makes the pipeline build the tangent frame for
that material, and sampling a texture or reading texture coordinates makes it
reconstruct the UV.

### Indirect diffuse light

One source lights the indirect diffuse light of a frame's opaque surfaces:

| Source | Lights the frame when |
| --- | --- |
| Surfel GI | `render.gi.enabled` is `true`, `render.gi.technique` is `surfels`, the device traces rays, and the surfel GI pipelines have compiled, the one that shades its rays included. |
| Probe GI | `render.gi.enabled` is `true`, `render.gi.technique` is `probes`, the device traces rays, and the probe GI pipelines have compiled, the one that shades its rays included. |
| Lightmaps | `render.gi.enabled` is `true`, `render.gi.technique` is `baked`, and `render.baked-gi.enabled` was `true` at startup. Each opaque pixel of an instance that a resident lightmap lights reads it; every other pixel is lit as under None. |
| SSGI | `render.gi.enabled` is `true`, `render.ssgi.enabled` was `true` at startup, and either `render.gi.technique` is `ssgi`, or it's `surfels` or `probes` and that technique doesn't light the frame. |
| None | None of them does. The environment alone lights indirect diffuse light, darkened by GTAO when it runs. |

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Images | The source writes `SSGI History` and `SSGI Normals`, which material evaluation reads. Material evaluation writes `Diffuse Radiance History`, which SSGI rays read in the next frame, only in a frame that SSGI lights. |
| SSGI history | SSGI reads its history only after a frame it lit at the same sink size. Its first frame after a frame it didn't light starts without history. |
| Environment light | Material evaluation adds the environment's irradiance around each opaque pixel's normal, times the environment's intensity and the pixel's environment visibility: the smaller of one minus the source's hit fraction and GTAO's visibility, each when it ran. Under surfel GI or probe GI, and at a pixel a lightmap lights, it adds none: their light holds the environment's light their rays reached. The environment visibility also dims the environment's reflections. |
| Small-scale occlusion | Under surfel GI or probe GI, and at a pixel a lightmap lights, material evaluation multiplies the source's light at each opaque pixel by GTAO's visibility, when GTAO runs. |
| Surfels | At most `render.surfel-gi.surfel-capacity` surfels live at once, and they trace at most `render.surfel-gi.ray-budget` rays a frame. |
| Probes | Probes trace at most `render.probe-gi.ray-budget` rays a frame, 128 for each probe they update. |
| Baked shaders | Material shaders keep the indirect diffuse read while any source is built in, the diffuse radiance write while SSGI is, and the lightmap read while lightmaps are. Materials have closest-hit shaders while surfel GI, probe GI, or lightmaps are built in. |

### Surfel GI

Surfel GI keeps surfels: small world-space discs on the surfaces the camera
sees. A grid around the first sink's camera lists the surfels that overlap each
of its cells, and every sink that surfel GI lights reads the surfels listed
around each of its pixels.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Surfel | A disc with a world-space position, a normal, and a radius, held in one slot of a pool of `render.surfel-gi.surfel-capacity` slots. |
| Radius | 0.24 meters, times the surfel's distance from the first sink's camera divided by 3.2 meters when that ratio is above one. |
| Grid | 8 cascades of 32 × 32 × 32 cells, centered on the first sink's camera and snapped to whole cells of cascade 0. A cell of cascade `c` measures 0.2 × 2<sup>c</sup> meters, and a position falls in the finest cascade that covers it. |
| Disc distance | A point's distance from a surfel's center, times one plus twice the point's distance from the surfel's plane in meters. |
| Cell list | The surfels whose disc distance to some point of the cell can be below 1.25 times their radius. A surfel near a cascade boundary is listed in both cascades. A surfel is listed in at most 32 cells: the cells it reaches fall into 8 shells by how near their nearest point lies, as a share of 1.25 times its radius, and it's listed in the nearest shells whose cells number at most 32 together. A list starts with the surfels whose disc distance to the cell's center is below 0.625 times their radius, then lists the rest; the order within each part can change from frame to frame. |
| Coverage | The sum, over the surfels listed in a pixel's cell, of a weight that falls smoothly from one at the surfel's center to zero at a disc distance of 1.25 times its radius, times the cosine between the surfel's and the pixel's normals. |
| Bounce | Zero for a surfel a pixel spawned, and one for a surfel a ray's hit spawned. |

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Spawn | Each 16 × 16-pixel tile of each sink spawns at most one surfel in each frame its pixels gather, at its least covered pixel. A pixel is a candidate when its coverage is below 0.1, its second-largest weight below 0.4, its cell lists fewer than 48 surfels, and a random draw passes with a probability that grows with its view depth. The new surfel takes the pixel's position and normal. When surfels whose trust is one and that face within 26 degrees of the pixel's normal light the pixel, the new surfel starts with the light of the one with the largest weight, worth 32 rays. |
| Hit spawns | A front-face hit on a surface whose albedo isn't black asks for a surfel when the sum of the weights times the visibilities of the surfels it reads is below 0.1, its cell lists fewer than 48 surfels, and the surfel whose ray found it has a bounce of zero: the first such hit in each cell a frame, and at most 64 hits a frame. Each request spawns a surfel with a bounce of one at the hit's position and normal, without light, while the surfels with a bounce of one make up less than a quarter of the live surfels, as the frame's grid counted them, and the slots in use less than three quarters of the pool. |
| Votes | In each frame it gathers, a pixel votes 5 for every surfel of its own cell that faces it within 37 degrees while the cell lists at most 48 surfels, and 2 for the surfel with the largest weight in its blend when that weight is above 0.05; when that weight is more than half of the blend's, the pixel votes 50 plus 50 times that share instead. A ray's hit votes for the surfel around it whose light reaches it most: 50 times that light's pre-exposed luminance, at most 50, when the luminance is above 0.01. A surfel keeps the strongest vote it got since the last frame. |
| Despawn | Each tile kills at most one surfel in each frame its pixels gather: the heaviest at its most covered pixel, when that pixel's coverage passes 3.5 and its second-largest weight passes 0.9. Both thresholds fall to 3.0 and 0.8 as the cell fills from 36 toward 48 surfels. |
| Aging | Each frame, a surfel ages by one frame plus its cell's rent, less its vote, and never gets younger. The rent is one frame for every 10 surfels the cell lists beyond 48, plus the square of those extra surfels over 32 once the cell lists more than 96. |
| Recycle | A surfel 500 frames old goes back to the pool. |
| Validation | Every 8 frames, each surfel traces a ray along its normal that crosses its plane, from 0.25 times its radius above the point its rays leave from to as far below its center. A hit marks the surfel supported, and a supported surfel whose ray finds no surface dies. |
| Burial | A surfel that received 32 rays and whose rays hit back faces more than half the time lately dies. Its share moves toward each update's share of back-face hits by the update's rays over the update's rays plus 16. |
| Rays | Each frame, a surfel with a bounce of zero asks for 32 rays until it has received 512, then 16 while its estimate's inconsistency is above 0.3, and 4 otherwise; a surfel with a bounce of one asks for 4. Requests for 32 or 16 rays form the priority class, the rest the regular class. Each class traces a window of its asked rays that starts where its last window ended, so the surfels in it take turns: the priority class up to three quarters of `render.surfel-gi.ray-budget`, or more while the regular class asks for less than the rest, and the regular class the rest of the budget. |
| Ray paths | A ray leaves from 0.01 times the surfel's radius above its center, but at least 0.5 millimeters and at most 1 centimeter, and reaches 10 kilometers. Its direction comes from the surfel's guide for a share of the rays that grows from 0 to 0.5 over the surfel's first 192 rays once the guide holds light, and from the cosine-weighted distribution otherwise. A guided direction is random. Cosine-weighted directions follow the R2 low-discrepancy sequence, rotated per surfel, at the point the surfel's received rays have reached. |
| Guide | Each surfel splits its hemisphere into 8 × 8 cells of equal solid angle, through the concentric map of the unit square onto the disk and the disk's equal-area lift onto the hemisphere. Each ray moves the four cells around its direction, by their bilinear shares, 2% of the way toward the pre-exposed luminance of the light it brought back times the cosine of its direction. A guided ray picks a cell in proportion to the cell's share of the guide, then a uniformly random direction inside the cell. |
| Radial depth | Each surfel splits its hemisphere into 4 × 4 cells of its octahedral projection, turned 45 degrees to fill the unit square, each holding the first four moments of the distance its rays went there. A ray counts its distance up to twice the surfel's radius, and a miss counts as twice the radius. It moves its cell 5% of the way toward its own moments when it ends nearer than the cell's mean, and 1% otherwise; a cell no ray reached yet takes the first ray's moments. |
| Hit surfaces | A ray hits the [ray-traced scene](#ray-traced-scene). The hit's material reports its diffuse albedo, the base color times one minus the metalness, and its emission, with texture levels that cover a cone widening by 0.31 radians along the ray. A material reports black until its closest-hit shader has compiled, and when its bake has none. The back of a single-sided surface reports black. |
| Ray light | A miss brings back the environment's light from its direction: the environment's specular level prefiltered for roughness 2/7, times the environment's intensity. A back-face hit brings back its emission. A front-face hit brings back its emission, plus its albedo times what reaches it: each sun that it faces and that no geometry hides; each point and cone light it faces, within the light's reach, that no geometry hides, lit as material evaluation lights a surface but without the light's IES profile; and the light of the first 16 surfels its cell lists, as they were the frame before, each weighted by its weight, its visibility from the hit, and its trust, over the sum of their weights times their visibilities, but at least 0.1. A hit that no surfel around it sees gets the scene's lights alone. |
| Estimate | Each surfel estimates the average light its rays bring back, as irradiance over π, and the fraction of its cosine-weighted rays that hit, with the multiscale mean estimator. Toward the light, each ray counts with the cosine-weighted density of its direction over the density it was drawn from. Toward the hit fraction, each ray drawn from the cosine-weighted distribution counts once, and a ray drawn from the guide doesn't count; a frame whose rays all came from the guide leaves the hit fraction as it was. The estimate holds rays: each update adds its rays, and what it held fades by e<sup>−f/240</sup> over the f frames since its last update. Until the surfel has received 128 rays, an update's rays weigh evenly with the rays the estimate holds. Afterward, an update's average counts at most 0.1 plus eight deviations above the recent mean, in pre-exposed units, and the estimate moves toward it at the estimator's rate, but at least by the update's rays over the rays the estimate then holds. |
| Trust | A surfel's weight in lookups is the rays it received, plus the 32 of a parent whose light it started with, over 128, up to one. |
| Visibility | A point's visibility from a surfel, for a pixel or a ray's hit, bounds the share of the surfel's rays toward the point that end before it: the Hamburger four-moment bound of moment shadow mapping on the radial depth around the direction toward the point, read bilinearly between cells, with its shadow strengthened 1.2 times. Visibilities up to a bleed then count as zero, and the rest stretch over zero to one. The bleed is 0.2, plus 0.15 times the deviation of the cell's distances over their root mean square, plus 0.25 times that ratio times one minus the cosine between the direction and the surfel's normal. A point is fully visible when Cantelli's inequality puts at most 0.1% of the blended distances before it. It's also fully visible when every cell a ray reached would leave it fully visible alone, by that rule or because the four-moment bound finds no shadow at its distance. A point below a surfel's plane takes the direction on its horizon. |
| Gathering | The half-resolution pixels of each 16 × 16-pixel tile gather an estimate in every other frame: the frames whose index has the parity of the tile's column plus its row, counted in tiles. A pixel also gathers in any frame where its history, or the history of a pixel in the same GPU subgroup, holds a confidence below 4. |
| Pixel estimate | In each frame it gathers, each half-resolution pixel reads the first 64 surfels its cell lists. Its estimate blends their light and hit fractions, each weighted by its weight, its visibility, and its trust, over the sum of those products. Its confidence is that sum over the sum of the weights times the visibilities, or over one when that sum is less. A pixel hidden from every surfel that would light it takes no light and a hit fraction of one, with the confidence of the surfels that hide it, counted the same way. A pixel no surfel lights has neither estimate nor confidence. |
| Pixel history | Each pixel keeps a history of its light, its confidence, and the variance of its luminance. The previous frame's history comes from where the previous camera saw the pixel's surface: the four texels around that point, each where the previous frame's depth lies within 5% of the point's, weighted by its bilinear share times its normal's agreement, as for SSGI's history. An estimate more than 3 deviations from the history's luminance cuts the history's confidence, down to one at 8 deviations, as far as the estimate's confidence goes; the deviation counts the history's own, plus 0.05 times its luminance, plus 0.001. The history's confidence is then at most 16, and the pixel blends its estimate into it by their confidences. A pixel that doesn't gather keeps its history. |
| Pixel light | Material evaluation reads each pixel's history averaged with neighbors on a golden-angle spiral, after toning each light down by one over one plus its largest channel and back up after. A pixel reads 2<sup>4(1 − c)²</sup> taps, its own included, but from 2 to 8, within sqrt(16² + (2² − 16²) c) texels, where c is its confidence up to one. Each neighbor weighs by its confidence, a falloff along the spiral, its normal's agreement, and its distance from the pixel's tangent plane. Where the summed weights fall short of one, the light of the neighbors at a similar depth on any surface fills the rest. Where no tap of the spiral has any, the nearest of six rings around the pixel, of 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, and 1,024 texels with 8 taps each, that holds a tap with confidence fills it, each tap weighted by its confidence times its depth similarity plus 0.001. Where no ring holds any, the pixel takes no light and a hit fraction of one. |
| Limits | The grid lists each surfel in at most 32 cells, a pixel reads at most 64 surfels of its cell, and a ray's hit at most 16. A full pool spawns nothing until a slot is recycled. A sink spawns in at most 129,600 tiles, the tiles of a 7,680 × 4,320 sink; in a larger sink, the tiles past them spawn nothing. Rays' hits ask for at most 64 surfels a frame. |

The first sink records the frame-wide surfel stages, `surfel-grid`,
`surfel-trace`, `surfel-validate`, and `surfel-integrate`, after its light
clusters and before it draws the scene; every sink that surfel GI lights
resolves, spawns, and reconstructs its surfels' light inside its `screen-space`
stage.

### Probe GI

Probe GI keeps a volume of irradiance probes around the first sink's camera,
in the manner of dynamic diffuse global illumination. Each probe records
the light that reaches it from every direction and how far its rays went, and
every sink that probe GI lights reads the probes around each of its pixels.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Cascade | One of 4 windows of 24 × 12 × 24 probes along x, y, and z. Cascade `c` places its probes `render.probe-gi.spacing` × 2<sup>c</sup> meters apart, on the grid points from the first sink's camera's grid point less 11, 5, and 11 probes. |
| Probe | A grid point of a cascade, plus an offset of at most 0.45 times the cascade's spacing on each axis, held in one of 27,648 slots. Each cascade's grid point keeps the same slot while the window moves, wrapping around the window. |
| Irradiance map | 6 × 6 texels of the probe's octahedral projection, inside a one-texel border that repeats the texels across the octahedron's edges. Each holds irradiance over π, in nits, in RGB and the hit fraction in alpha, in half floats. |
| Distance map | 14 × 14 texels in the same projection and border, each holding the mean and mean square of the distance rays went near its direction, in half floats. |
| State | Unset, until the probe's first update since its window reached it; buried, when more than 25% of its rays hit back faces; active, when a front face one of its rays hit lies within one spacing of its grid point on every axis, or a pixel read it in the last 8 frames; idle otherwise. |

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule | Each frame updates the `render.probe-gi.ray-budget` / 128 probes of highest priority. Unset probes come first, those a pixel read in the last 8 frames ahead of the rest, then finer cascades first. Every other probe ranks by the frames since its last update times its importance, in steps of a quarter of a doubling: the importance is 8 when it's active and a pixel read it in the last 8 frames, 1 when it's active otherwise, and 0.125 when it's idle or buried. Probes of the same rank take turns in an order that changes every frame. |
| Rays | Each update traces 128 rays from the probe, up to 10 kilometers, along a spherical Fibonacci pattern that a random rotation turns every frame. |
| Hit surfaces | As for [surfel GI](#surfel-gi): a ray hits the [ray-traced scene](#ray-traced-scene), and the hit's material reports its albedo and emission. |
| Ray light | A miss brings back the environment's light from its direction: the environment's specular level prefiltered for roughness 2/7, times the environment's intensity. A back-face hit brings back no light. A front-face hit brings back its emission, plus its albedo times what reaches it: each sun that it faces and that no geometry hides; each point and cone light it faces, within the light's reach, that no geometry hides, lit as material evaluation lights a surface but without the light's IES profile; and 0.95 times the probes' light at the hit as the last update left it. That light comes from the finest cascade whose window holds the hit, and the hit gets the scene's lights alone when no probe of it lights there. A ray's light is at most 30,000 nits in each channel. |
| Blend | Each irradiance texel averages the rays' light, weighed by the cosine to its direction, and the share of them that hit. Each distance texel averages the rays' distances and their squares, weighed by that cosine to the power 50; a back-face hit counts a fifth of its distance, and every distance at most twice the spacing. A texel keeps 0.97 of what it held, and n / (n + 1) while its probe has n of its first 32 updates. A probe whose rays' mean luminance and whose irradiance map's mean luminance differ by ten times or more, each counted as at least 0.01 nits, starts over: the update replaces both maps and counts as its first. |
| Relocation | A probe whose rays hit back faces more than 25% of the time moves through the nearest back face, past it by a tenth of the spacing. Otherwise, a probe nearer than a fifth of the spacing to a front face moves away from it to that distance. A move that would take the offset past 0.45 of the spacing on any axis doesn't happen, and a move longer than a tenth of the spacing starts the probe's averages over. |
| Pixel light | Each half-resolution pixel rebuilds its surface from the linear depth pyramid. Its point moves off the surface by 0.225 times the spacing along 0.2 times its normal plus 0.8 times the direction toward the camera. Each active probe of the eight around it weighs the product of its trilinear share; ((c + 1) / 2)² + 0.2, where c is the cosine between the normal and the direction toward the probe; and the cube of Chebyshev's bound on the probe's distances toward the point, but at least 0.05. A weight under 0.2 is crushed to its cube over 0.04. The finest cascade whose window holds the point with one probe to spare lights it; within two probes of that margin, and for the trilinear share of unset probes, the next cascade lights the rest. A pixel no cascade lights takes the environment's irradiance around its normal, times the environment's intensity, and a hit fraction of zero. |
| Marks | Every other pixel of every other row marks the eight probes around it in the cascade that lights it, and in the next one too where the two blend. |

The first sink records the frame-wide probe stages, `probe-schedule`,
`probe-trace`, and `probe-update`, after the surfel stages and before it draws
the scene; every sink that probe GI lights reads the probes and marks them
inside its `screen-space` stage.

### Lightmaps

A lightmap holds the indirect diffuse light of a level's static meshes, which
BELD bakes from the level; [lightmaps](/docs/reference/assets#lightmaps) define
what its texels hold. A spawned level's `lightmap` kind makes it resident.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Lit instance | An instance of a primitive that the resident lightmap gives a rectangle, on an entity of the spawn whose `lightmap` kind names it, once both the lightmap and its image are loaded. |
| Lightmap UV | The pixel's [lightmap UVs](/docs/reference/assets#lightmap-uvs), interpolated as its texture coordinates are, times the rectangle's scale, plus its offset. |

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Pixel light | Each opaque pixel of a lit instance reads the lightmap's full-size level at its lightmap UV, with bilinear filtering and clamped edges, at full resolution. Its light isn't pre-exposed. |
| Hit fraction | One minus the lightmap's alpha at the pixel's lightmap UV. The lightmap's light replaces the environment's diffuse light, and the hit fraction occludes the environment's reflections as it does for the other sources. |
| Movement | A lit instance keeps reading its rectangle when its entity moves. Bake the lightmap again after moving what it lights. |
| Skinned primitives | Are never lit instances. |
| Residency | One lightmap is resident at a time: a newer `lightmap` component replaces it, and deleting its entity removes it. |

Lightmaps record no stages: material evaluation reads them inside its
`material-evaluation` stage.

### Ray-traced scene

The rays surfel GI and probe GI trace hit the scene as the first sink's frame
places it, with these exceptions:

| Rule | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Geometry | Each mesh's opaque, unskinned primitives at full detail. Masked and blended primitives are left out, so rays pass through foliage and glass, and so are skinned primitives, so rays pass through the shapes skinning deforms. A mesh without such primitives has no geometry. |
| Instances | Every renderable the frame draws whose mesh has geometry, once that geometry is built. |
| Builds | A mesh's geometry is built once, from the first frame that draws one of its renderables, in the order meshes arrive: at most 262,144 triangles of builds a frame, and at least one mesh a frame. A frame that doesn't record the frame-wide work builds nothing, and its meshes wait for the next frame. A mesh's geometry lasts as long as the mesh stays resident. |
| Placement | Every frame that surfel GI or probe GI lights places the instances at their transforms in that frame. |

The first sink records the builds and the placement in the
`acceleration-structures` stage, before `surfel-grid`. The stage's time covers
the builds. The placement runs alongside the stages after it, so its time shows
in the first stage that traces rays.

### Stages and targets

Each sink records these stages in order, and each reports a `stage.<name>`
metric when it runs: `skinning`, `shadow-maps`, `shadow-depth-pyramid`,
`light-clusters`, `acceleration-structures`, `surfel-grid`, `surfel-trace`,
`surfel-validate`, `surfel-integrate`, `probe-schedule`, `probe-trace`, `probe-update`,
`visibility-early`, `occlusion-pyramid`, `visibility-late`,
`material-prepasses`, `depth-pyramid`, `cascade-fit`, `screen-space`,
`material-evaluation`, `background`, and `transparent`. Only the first sink
records the ray tracing, surfel, and probe stages: the surfel stages in a frame
that surfel GI lights, the probe stages in a frame that probe GI lights, and
`acceleration-structures` in either. `material-prepasses` runs only when `render.material-evaluation` is
`pipelines`.

| Target | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `Lit`, aliased `main` | Scene color, full size. Reflections read the previous frame's copy. |
| `Depth`, aliased `depth` | Reversed depth, full size. Reflections read the previous frame's copy. |
| `primitive index`, `instance_id` | 32-bit IDs per pixel. |
| `Sun Visibility`, `Contact Shadow Trace` | Sun visibility per shadow slot, full size, and its half-size contact trace. |
| `SSGI Raw`, `SSGI Normals`, `SSGI History`, `Diffuse Radiance History` | Half-size indirect lighting and its history. |
| `Ambient Occlusion`, `GTAO Raw`, `GTAO Blur` | Ambient occlusion, full size, and its half-size evaluation and denoised intermediate; no usable data on frames GTAO doesn't run. |
| `Surfel History`, `Surfel History Weights` | Half-size history of surfel GI's light, and its confidence and luminance variance; only when surfel GI is built in. |

These names, with a pass's outputs, select a screenshot through the inspector;
history targets can also be read from the previous frame.
