title: Smooth jagged edges
description: Pick temporal antialiasing or SMAA for your application, install it, and keep moving objects and effects free of trails.

Install an antialiasing pass to smooth the stair steps along edges. Byte Engine ships two, and you pick one per
application:

- **Temporal antialiasing** moves the camera by a fraction of a pixel every frame and blends the frames. It
  smooths edges, thin lines, and shimmering highlights, and it settles the grain of ambient occlusion, fog, and other
  effects that sample per pixel. It needs the visibility pipeline.
- **SMAA** finds edges in each frame on its own. It costs less and never trails, but it can't fix detail thinner than a
  pixel, and highlights still shimmer.

Leave both out to turn antialiasing off.

## Install temporal antialiasing

Call `setup_taa_render_pass` before you create the first window, after the scene pipeline, particles, and height fog,
and before depth of field, motion blur, bloom, and tone mapping:

```rust
setup_pbr_visibility_shading_render_pipeline(&mut app, SceneId::WORLD);
setup_particles(&mut app, SceneId::WORLD);
setup_exponential_height_fog_render_pass(&mut app);
setup_taa_render_pass(&mut app, TaaPassSettings::default());
setup_motion_blur_render_pass(&mut app, MotionBlurPassSettings::default());
setup_bloom_render_pass(&mut app, BloomPassSettings::default());
setup_aces_tonemap_render_pass(&mut app);
```

The defaults keep moving objects from leaving trails. To swap to SMAA, replace that one call with
`setup_smaa_render_pass(&mut app)`.

## Tune the blend

Set the fields of `TaaPassSettings`, or the `render.taa.` parameters while the application runs:

- `feedback_max` (`render.taa.feedback-max`) is how much a still pixel keeps from earlier frames. Raise it toward `1`
  for a smoother still image; lower it if a scene that changes, such as flickering lights, leaves a trail.
- `feedback_min` (`render.taa.feedback-min`) is how much a moving pixel keeps when its color changes, or when it moves
  fast. Lower it to cut trails behind fast motion, at the cost of more visible jagged edges in motion.
- `variance_gamma` (`render.taa.variance-gamma`) is how far earlier frames may differ from the current one before
  they're discarded. Lower it to reject stale color sooner; raise it if fine detail flickers.

The exact blend is in [Temporal antialiasing](/docs/reference/rendering#temporal-antialiasing) in the rendering
reference.

## Render fewer pixels with MetalFX

On a Mac, let MetalFX blend the frames instead, and render the scene at a fraction of the window's size to save GPU
time:

```rust
setup_taa_render_pass(
	&mut app,
	TaaPassSettings {
		technique: TemporalTechnique::MetalFx,
		scale: 0.67,
		..TaaPassSettings::default()
	},
);
```

Or set `render.taa.technique` to `metalfx` and `render.taa.scale` to a value such as `0.67` while the application runs.
A scale of `0.5` renders a quarter of the pixels; `1` keeps the window's resolution and only antialiases. Passes after
temporal antialiasing, such as bloom and the UI, still render at the window's resolution. Where the device lacks
MetalFX, the pass logs a warning and blends with its own shader at full resolution.

## Keep effects free of trails

Temporal antialiasing follows the motion of the meshes it draws, so moving and animated meshes need nothing from you.
Blended materials, such as glass and smoke, don't report motion, so the pass trusts earlier frames less where they
cover more of a pixel.

To see where a trail comes from, show the pass's debug views, as [Show debug views](/docs/use/debug-views) describes:

- `velocity` shows each pixel's motion. A trail where the view shows no motion means a mesh moves in a way the engine
  doesn't see.
- `disocclusion` shows in green where earlier frames were discarded because something stood in front.
- `reactive` shows how little each pixel trusts earlier frames.
- `taa-frames` shows how many frames each pixel has blended.

## Turn temporal antialiasing on and off

Set the `render.pass.taa` parameter to `bypassed` or `enabled` while the application runs. The camera stops moving by
a fraction of a pixel while the pass is bypassed.
