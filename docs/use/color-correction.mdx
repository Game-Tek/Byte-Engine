Correct the picture when a scene is too dark, lit by light that's too warm or too cool, crushed in the shadows, or too
flat or too colorful for the look you want, and give it a split tone, such as teal shadows and orange highlights. The correction works on scene light, so the tone mapper or a LUT still controls how highlights
roll off.

## Install the pass

Call `setup_color_correction_render_pass` before you create the first window. Install it after every pass that writes
scene light, such as bloom, and before a LUT or tone mapper:

```rust
setup_bloom_render_pass(&mut app, BloomPassSettings::default());
setup_color_correction_render_pass(&mut app, ColorCorrectionPassSettings {
	exposure: 0.5,
	temperature: 5000.0,
	contrast: 1.2,
	saturation: 1.1,
	..Default::default()
});
setup_agx_tonemap_render_pass(&mut app);
```

The default settings leave the picture unchanged, so set only the fields you need.

## Choose the correction

- `exposure` brightens or darkens the picture in stops: `1` doubles the light, `-1` halves it. It adds to the camera's
  exposure.
- `temperature` names the color temperature, in kelvins, of the light that should look white. Set it to the
  temperature of your scene's main light to neutralize it: `3200` for tungsten, `5600` for daylight. A lower value turns
  the picture bluer, a higher one warmer.
- `tint` corrects green or magenta light, from `-1` to `1`. Positive values turn the picture magenta, negative values
  green.
- `shadows` brightens the shadows from `0` to `1`, or deepens them from `0` to `-1`. Black and mid grey don't move,
  so use it to recover shadow detail that a LUT or the ACES output crushes without turning black grey.
- `highlights` brightens the highlights from `0` to `1`, or pulls them down from `0` to `-1`. Mid grey and the brightest
  light don't move.
- `contrast` sets the S-curve's slope at mid grey. Values above `1` deepen shadows and lift highlights, and mid grey
  stays where it is. The curve rolls off toward black, so shadows darken without clipping.
- `saturation` sets the strength of color: `0` is black and white, `1` keeps the color.
- `density` sets how saturation behaves. At `0`, stronger color gets brighter, which can look like neon. At `1`, it
  gets deeper and darker, the way more dye does on film, and weaker color gets lighter. The default, `0.5`, is between
  the two.

## Add a split tone

A split tone leans the highlights toward one hue and the shadows toward the opposite hue, such as the orange and teal of
many films:

```rust
setup_color_correction_render_pass(&mut app, ColorCorrectionPassSettings {
	split_hue: 55.0,
	split_amount: 0.4,
	..Default::default()
});
```

- `split_hue` is the highlights' hue in degrees around the Oklab color wheel. `55` gives orange highlights and teal
  shadows; `90` gives yellow and blue, and `0` pink and green.
- `split_amount` sets how strong the tint is, from `0` to `1`. Start low: around `0.3` is often enough.
- `split_balance` shifts the tint toward the shadows, down to `-1` for shadows only, or toward the highlights, up to
  `1` for highlights only.

The split tone keeps black, mid grey, and the brightest highlights neutral, doesn't change brightness, and leaves
saturated colors such as skin mostly alone, so faces keep their color while the scene around them takes the tint. It
runs before saturation, so raise `saturation` after you set the split, not before.

The exact formulas are in [Color correction](/docs/reference/rendering#color-correction) in the rendering reference.

## Turn the correction on and off

Set the `render.pass.color-correction` parameter to bypass the pass or turn it back on while the application runs, as
with any other pass.
