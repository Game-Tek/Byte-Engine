Use the editor when you want to arrange a level while you play it. It draws over your application's frame, so the game
keeps running and rendering under it, and every change you make reaches the game at once.

The editor picks what you click with a screen scene query, so it needs the visibility pipeline, which you install with
`setup_pbr_visibility_shading_render_pipeline`. It reads your keys through the default input, which you install with
`setup_default_input`.

## Install the editor

1. Set up your application's own render passes first. The editor's pass must come last, so it draws over everything
   else, your own UI included.

2. Call `install` before the first window opens, with the window's size. Render passes register before the first
   window.

   ```rust
   use byte_engine::ui::Size;

   let mut editor = byte_engine::editor::install(&mut app, Size::new(1920, 1080));
   ```

3. Call `frame` once in every tick, after your game's own tick:

   ```rust
   while app
   	.tick_with(|app, time| {
   		game.tick(app, time);
   		editor.frame(app);
   	})
   	.is_some()
   {}
   ```

The editor starts closed, and a closed editor costs nothing: its render pass forwards the frame unchanged.

## Edit the scene

1. Press F5 to open the editor. It frees the cursor and takes all of your application's input until you close it
   with F5 or Escape. Escape steps back one thing at a time: it cancels what you're carrying or typing, closes the
   palette, then a texture's preview or the material graph, and then the editor.

2. Click an entity in the game view, or its row in the scene list, to select it. The properties panel shows the
   entity's name, its transform while it has one, and its camera or light settings. A light shows its color and
   intensity in the form it was authored in, such as a temperature in kelvin and a flux in lumens. Its shadow range
   shows `auto` until you set it; type `auto` to hand it back to the renderer.

3. Pick Move, Rotate, or Scale in the toolbar, then drag a handle of the gizmo. To set a value exactly, drag a
   property field sideways, or click it and type the value.

4. Press Cmd+A to open the palette on what you can place: your meshes, your prefabs, and a point, cone, or
   directional light. Type part of a name and drag the result into the scene, or press Enter to carry it with the
   pointer until you click where it goes. A mesh or light follows the pointer; a prefab appears where you place it,
   with its parameters' defaults. A light starts a little above the surface you place it on, a cone or directional
   light facing down; select it to move or turn it.

   Before you type, the palette lists what you used most recently in it first.

   Press Cmd+K instead to search everything, material graphs and settings included. Press the shortcut again to close
   the palette, or another one to switch to it.

   Prefabs spawn through the spawner you give the inspector, so their components can use your own kinds. Give it
   the spawner you register your kinds with, after you register them:

   ```rust
   let mut spawner = Spawner::with_engine_kinds();
   spawner.register::<Patrol>("patrol")?;
   app.inspector().set_spawner(Arc::new(spawner));
   ```

   See [Spawn reusable objects with prefabs](/docs/use/prefabs) to write one.

5. To change a runtime setting, such as `render.lod.bias`, press Cmd+. and type part of its name. Each row shows the
   setting's value. Press Enter on one to put its value in the search field, type a new value, and press Enter again
   to set it. A setting that takes one of a few words, such as `true` or `false` or a technique's name, lists them
   instead: pick one with the arrow keys or a click, or type to narrow the list, and press Enter. Press Escape to go
   back to the search. The values a setting takes are the ones the console's `set` takes; when your application refuses one, a
   toast says why. See [runtime settings](/docs/reference/parameters#runtime-settings) for what each one takes.

6. To explore your resources, press Cmd+E and type part of a name. It finds your material graphs and textures. Open
   a texture to see it in a preview panel, with its size in pixels, and press Escape to close it. Textures show from
   their PNG or JPEG source files, so a build without sources finds none.

7. To start a new level, press Cmd+K, pick **New level**, and pick the folder it goes in; `/` is the asset folder
   itself. The editor writes an empty level there, named `untitled.level`, or a numbered name, such as
   `untitled 2.level`, when that name is taken. The level isn't loaded into the running game.

8. To change a material, find its graph with Cmd+E or Cmd+K and open it. Drag between sockets to connect nodes, press
   Cmd+A to add one, and press Cmd+S to save the graph; your application reloads the material.

## See warnings and errors

While the editor is open, each warning and error your application logs shows as a toast in the window's bottom-right
corner: what happened, over its likely cause. The newest toast is in front, with up to two older ones behind it. Move
the pointer over them to spread them out and keep them from leaving; otherwise each one leaves after four seconds, or
when you click its ×. A message that repeats counts up on its toast instead of adding another.

Toasts show what `log.level` lets through, which is only errors unless you change it. To see warnings too, run with
`RUST_LOG=warn` or set [`log.level`](/docs/reference/parameters) to `warn`.
