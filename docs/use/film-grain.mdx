title: Add film grain
description: Give your application's picture the grain of scanned 35 mm, 16 mm, or 8 mm film.

---

Add film grain when you want the picture to look shot on film. The grain changes every frame, and its strength
follows the picture's brightness, so you can keep highlights clean while shadows show grain.

## Install the pass

Call `setup_film_grain_render_pass` before you create the first window. Install it after tone mapping or color
grading and after antialiasing, so they don't smooth the grain away, and before the UI, so the UI stays clean:

```rust
setup_smaa_render_pass(&mut app);
setup_aces_tonemap_render_pass(&mut app);
setup_film_grain_render_pass(&mut app, FilmGrainPassSettings::default());
setup_ui_render_pass(&mut app, &ui, None);
```

The default settings give the look of 35 mm stock.

## Choose a look

Set the fields of `FilmGrainPassSettings` to choose a different look:

```rust
let settings = FilmGrainPassSettings {
	level: 0.6,
	size: 1.2,
	..Default::default()
};
setup_film_grain_render_pass(&mut app, settings);
```

- `level` sets the strength. At `1` the grain on mid grey is as strong as on scans of real film; `0` turns it off.
- `size` sets how coarse the grain is. Use about `0.4` for 35 mm, `1.2` for 16 mm, and `2.7` for 8 mm. Coarse grain
  costs more GPU time than fine grain.
- `zone_gains` sets how strong the grain is in the Black, Shadow, Mid, High, and White zones of the picture's
  brightness. `0` keeps a zone as strong as mid grey, `-1` removes its grain, and `3` makes it four times as strong.
  The defaults strengthen the shadows and fade the grain out toward white.

The exact grain model is in [Film grain](/docs/reference/rendering#film-grain) in the rendering reference.

## Turn the grain on and off

Set the `render.pass.film-grain` parameter to bypass the pass or turn it back on while the application runs, as
with any other pass.
