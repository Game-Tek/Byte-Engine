# Byte-Engine already owns half of motion matching

**Shipped games still animate locomotion with classic motion matching in October 2026, and Byte-Engine already has the runtime half of it.** Ubisoft first showed the technique in 2015. Every tenth of a second or so, it searches a database for the frame whose root-relative foot positions, foot and hip velocities, and path over the next second best match a spring-predicted plan. It then cuts to that frame and hides the cut with inertialization. Neural successors have shipped only as memory compression (Learned Motion Matching) or in narrow niches, and diffusion controllers still cost 5–21 ms of desktop GPU time per character. Unreal's Pose Search has been the only first-party, production-ready engine implementation since UE 5.4 in April 2024. Unity shelved Kinematica, the paid plugins went free and unsupported, and Ubisoft, EA, Naughty Dog, Remedy, and IO Interactive built their own. Byte-Engine already has packed clip sampling, a pooled clip arena, an inertializer that starts from the displayed pose, full root-motion extraction, lock-free crowd evaluation across worker lanes, and debug data streams, and its sandbox's hand-measured stop-frame tables amount to a manual, offline pose search. What it lacks is every piece specific to matching: per-frame feature baking, a resident database, trajectory prediction, the search itself, tags and mirroring, foot contacts and IK, and a way to reconcile root motion with gameplay. Three constraints shape the design. One pruned search costs as much as 17–50 complete updates of today's walking character (1.8 µs each), so searches must be throttled and staggered. The best public locomotion datasets are non-commercial or tied to Unreal, which leaves CMU, 100STYLE, CC0 libraries, and O3DE's sample clips as the only public data an MIT repository can hold. And no granted patent turned up that claims plain classic motion matching, although Unity's US 10,888,785 and EA's US 10,388,053 need a patent attorney's review before the feature ships; this report is not legal advice.

## A 2015 brute-force shortcut still defines the state of the art

Motion matching is the games industry's greedy simplification of two academic ideas. **Motion Graphs** (Kovar, Gleicher, and Pighin, SIGGRAPH 2002) built a graph from clips plus automatically found transitions, comparing frames by point-cloud distance over windows of about a third of a second; finding the transitions in 6,000 frames took about 25 minutes ([Kovar et al. 2002](https://graphics.cs.wisc.edu/Papers/2002/KGP02/mograph.pdf)). **Motion Fields** (Lee et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2010) let a character jump to any frame at any moment, with a reinforcement-learned value function choosing among nearest-neighbour states, and ran at about 200 frames per second unoptimized ([Lee et al. 2010](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~jovan/papers/lee-2010-mf.pdf)). Michael Büttner of Ubisoft Toronto presented motion matching at nucl.ai in Vienna in July 2015 ([talk video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z_wpgHFSWss); [event dates](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/events/711/nuclai/)), and Simon Clavet and Kristjan Zadziuk followed at GDC 2016. Clavet kept the Motion Fields idea that the character may jump anywhere, dropped the reinforcement learning, and called the result "a ridiculously brute-force approach to animation selection" ([Clavet GDC 2016 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Clavet_Simon_MotionMatching.pdf)). *For Honor* (February 2017) was the first game to use it for player control ([Game Anim](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/05/03/motion-matching-ubisofts-honor/)). The lineage explains the central design choice: the cost's future-trajectory term does the job the value function did, so it serves as a cheap stand-in for planning.

The algorithm was written down precisely in Section 3 of **Learned Motion Matching** (Holden et al., SIGGRAPH 2020), which describes Ubisoft's in-house system from several AAA productions, and in Holden's MIT-licensed reference code ([LMM paper](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf); [database.h](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/blob/main/database.h)). Each frame gets a **27-float feature vector**, expressed relative to the character's root. It holds both foot positions, both foot velocities, the hip velocity, and ground-plane positions and facing directions 20, 40, and 60 frames ahead at 60 Hz (about 0.33, 0.67, and 1.0 s). Each feature group is normalized by its standard deviation across the dataset, with one deviation shared by the whole group so a 3D position keeps its geometry. A user weight then scales the group; the code defaults are 0.75 for foot position, 1.5 for trajectory direction, and 1.0 for the rest. At query time the pose features are copied from the database row now playing rather than measured from the blended output, and the trajectory comes from a spring the stick drives. The search minimizes squared Euclidean distance, switches only when another frame beats the current frame's cost, and hides the switch with an inertialized transition.

The reference controller builds an anti-shredding contract into the search itself ([database.h](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/blob/main/database.h); [controller.cpp](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/blob/main/controller.cpp)). It computes the current frame's cost first and makes it the bar to beat. It skips frames within 20 of the current one, and it skips the last 20 frames of every clip range, so a frame without a full future can never be picked. It searches on a **0.1 s timer**, again whenever a clip runs out, and again the moment a rapid change in desired velocity or rotation settles, at most once per 0.1 s. Unreal exposes the same ideas as Continuing Pose Cost Bias, Pose Jump Threshold Time, Pose Reselect History, Search Throttle Time, and notify states that block entry into a frame range or exclude it entirely ([Epic: Motion Matching in Unreal Engine](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine)). Gait and context tags enter either as one-hot features or as precomputed frame ranges that restrict the search ([LMM paper](https://theorangeduck.com/media/uploads/other_stuff/Learned_Motion_Matching.pdf)).

### The predicted trajectory runs on a separate simulated body

Classic motion matching splits the character into two bodies. A code-driven simulation point moves by a critically damped spring on velocity and facing and predicts the future path, while the chosen clip's root motion moves the visible character. The search keeps the two together by preferring clips that follow the simulation, and Clavet clamped the character to within 15 cm of it ([Clavet slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Clavet_Simon_MotionMatching.pdf)). Holden's controller evaluates the spring in closed form at each future sample, with damping of 4·ln2 divided by the halflife and a demo halflife of 0.27 s for both velocity and rotation. Its target speeds were measured from the data: about 4, 3, and 2.5 m/s running forward, sideways, and backward ([Spring-It-On](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)). Fitting the spring to a captured stand-to-run gives a halflife of about 0.63 s ([Fitting Code Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/fitting-code-driven-displacement)), so the demo's gameplay spring is roughly 2.3 times stiffer than the human data. Naughty Dog made the same trade on purpose: its player starts run about twice as fast as an actor's, and its stops take 15–20 cm instead of metres ([Naughty Dog GDC 2021 deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)).

Holden reconciles the two bodies in three layers ([controller.cpp](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/blob/main/controller.cpp)). Damped corrections pull the character toward the simulation with halflives of 0.1 s for position and 0.2 s for rotation. Each correction is capped at half the character's own speed times the frame time, so a standing character never slides. A hard clamp then keeps the character within 0.15 m and 90° of the simulation. Because the query is expressed relative to the animated character, any drift between the bodies shows up in the query and the search corrects it at no extra cost. The clamp radius is the main trade between responsiveness and quality. Naughty Dog allowed the player essentially no offset and its NPCs 0.3–0.6 m, although one slide's speaker notes say 0.1–0.4 m. For NPCs on navigation paths, the team shortened the predicted trajectory to 0.5–1.2 s so it holds a single path shape at a time, and slowed characters before corners using the deceleration spring ([Naughty Dog deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)).

## Production turned motion matching into a data-curation discipline

Talks or official statements confirm a short list of shipped uses. *For Honor* (Ubisoft Montreal, 2017) matched about ten factors and ran on more than 100 on-screen characters through aggressive LOD, and four or five Ubisoft projects were already using the technique in 2016 ([Game Anim](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/05/03/motion-matching-ubisofts-honor/)). EA's *UFC 3* (2018) describes its Real Player Motion as a database query with timing constraints, without using the name motion matching ([GDC 2018 abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025228/Real-Player-Motion-Tech-in)), and EA applied matching to player interactions in *Madden* and *FIFA* ([GDC 2021 abstract](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027465/Animation-Summit-Environmental-and-Motion)). *The Last of Us Part II* followed in 2020, Fortnite Chapter 5 in December 2023 on every platform ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/enjoy-improved-animations-in-fortnite-chapter-5)), and *Alan Wake 2*, whose NPCs use distance-based motion matching ([Remedy](https://www.remedygames.com/article/how-northlight-makes-alan-wake-2-shine)). *Black Myth: Wukong* uses it on Unreal, according to a Game Science talk whose year is unconfirmed and whose write-up was readable only as a search excerpt ([ElectroDealPro](https://electrodealpro.com/game-science-this-technology-allows-black-myth-wukong-to-solve-many-action-problems/)). IO Interactive's *007 First Light* (May 2026) added a new motion matching system to the Glacier engine ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/007-first-light-io-interactive-hitman-interview-sgf-2025)). No primary source confirms motion matching in Assassin's Creed, Ghost Recon, Spider-Man 2, or Call of Duty. Only Naughty Dog published its scale: **2,627 player clips (2 h 17 min) and 6,050 NPC clips (6 h 34 min)**, an index search of about 0.1 ms, and a complete update of 0.5–1.0 ms against 2–3 ms for the locomotion system it replaced ([Naughty Dog deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)).

### Naughty Dog's account is the most detailed and the most sobering

The talk's own summary traces a path from excitement through frustration to worry ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027118/Motion-Matching-in-The-Last)). The central failure was *shredding*: near-duplicate clips replaced one another so often that the performance looked washed out, even though the joint transforms stayed continuous. The team set itself a rule that pressing the stick forward, holding it, and releasing it should play three animations, four at worst. To meet it, the team gave every clip's head and tail a controlled length, so transitions drop out when their future samples run out. A compulsory past-trajectory sample let the search tell a corner from a start and a stop from a quick plant. For the player, pose weighed 1.3–1.5 times as much as trajectory, current-velocity weight stayed low, and a continuation bias favoured the clip already playing. Soft cost groups did the rest: a *Moving* group switched on by a smoothed speed signal, and penalty layers such as the 1,000-cost penalty that kept wade loops out until the character entered water. Every move came in left- and right-foot versions with the wrong foot penalized, and some sets were mirrored ([deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)). The team's list of drawbacks is candid: the system needs technically minded animators, uses a lot of memory, costs a lot up front, turns the Motion Model into a bottleneck, and depends on mocap stage access. Against that it lists very high connectivity, free transitions from canned animations into locomotion, and no stuck transition that can block progression.

### EA's lesson is about how to organize databases

EA's 2023 retrospective addresses database organization ([EA GDC 2023 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2023/Slides/Motion+Matching+at+EA_Delannoy_JC.pdf)). One database for everything makes similar assets hard to separate, such as a start and an acceleration out of a turn. EA answered with *bucketing* (one database per category), tags such as DoNotChoose and NextNaturalOnly, priority databases that win when their best cost clears a threshold, and a root-motion weight that blends between model-driven and animation-driven displacement at runtime. It also embedded matching inside ordinary graphs: a Match Node queries once on entry, a Match Gate Node takes a transition only when the best cost is under a threshold, and a Context Database filters frames by game state.

### Two philosophies now compete

Naughty Dog aimed for a **stateless** system, with one set per locomotion style, soft biases, and clip cut lengths as the handover control. Epic built a **stateful hybrid**. Gameplay predicates (starting, pivoting, turning in place) and Chooser tables pick which databases to search, such as walk databases only while walking and no pivot databases during a start, and motion matching picks the frame within that choice ([Epic GASP docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/game-animation-sample-project-in-unreal-engine)). Ubisoft's 2016 prototype already planned the hybrid, and *For Honor*'s game logic stayed a state machine ([Zadziuk GDC 2016 notes](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Zadziuk_Kristjan_MotionMatchingFutureOfGamesAnimation.pdf); [Game Anim](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/05/03/motion-matching-ubisofts-honor/)). The 2016 pitch of dropping raw mocap straight into a database did not survive production. Zadziuk's team found that well-chosen data beat sheer volume, split capture into *dance-card* phases per gait (starts and stops, plant-and-turns, circles, accelerations, snakes), and reported reaching about 70% quality quickly with the last 30% the hard part ([Zadziuk notes](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Zadziuk_Kristjan_MotionMatchingFutureOfGamesAnimation.pdf)). Naughty Dog put the plateau at 60% and replaced long dance cards with directed medium and short takes ([Naughty Dog deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)).

### Not everyone bought in

Bobby Anguelov argued in 2021 that animation-driven locomotion is a mistake and that animation must give way to gameplay ([X post](https://x.com/Bobby_Anguelov/status/1395375951639453700)); his MIT-licensed engine Esoterica contains no motion matching ([Esoterica](https://github.com/BobbyAnguelov/Esoterica)). Unity suspended Kinematica in March 2021, later citing integration problems ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/unite-now-kinematica-democratizing-motion-matching-for-character-animation/788112?page=9)), and said in May 2025 that motion matching would not be in the first release of its new animation system ([Unity Discussions, 2025](https://discussions.unity.com/t/animation-status-update-q1-2025-gdc-roadmap/1614718?page=3)). Fortnite's switch took about a year and changed movement feel enough that players objected and Epic sped crouching and running up again ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/enjoy-improved-animations-in-fortnite-chapter-5); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/yes-fortnite-feels-different-with-chapter-5-and-epic-is-listening-to-feedback/1100-6519678/)). For Byte-Engine, which has no animation team and no capture stage, the lesson is that data and tuning, not the search, will set the schedule. Epic's hybrid, where code chooses the context and matching runs inside it, demands less content discipline than Naughty Dog's stateless model.

## What runs after the search decides whether feet slide

### Inertialization makes frequent switching affordable

Gears of War 4 cut straight to the target animation and let a post-process decay the difference between the old and new poses. On Xbox One the transition cost **12 µs instead of 30 µs** for a crossfade, 60% less, and 1D and 2D blend spaces cost 150% and 300% more than a single sequence ([Bollo, SIGGRAPH 2017](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/2017-Talks-Bollo_High-Performance-Animation-in-Gears-of-War-4.pdf)). Bollo's GDC 2018 curve is a quintic matched to the initial offset and velocity. It chooses the initial acceleration to prevent overshoot, shortens the duration when velocity is high, and decays vectors by magnitude and quaternions by angle so the axes stay in step ([Bollo GDC 2018 slides](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2018/presentations/bollo_david_inertialization_high_performance.pdf)). Holden's variant decays the offset with an exact critically damped spring instead. It has no fixed duration and can overshoot, but it is the cheapest form when every joint shares one halflife; it adds the existing offset when one transition interrupts another and inertializes the root in world space, with a 0.1 s halflife in the demo ([Spring Roll Call](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call); [controller.cpp](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/blob/main/controller.cpp)). Dead blending (Holden 2023, also an experimental Unreal 5.3 node) extrapolates the outgoing pose with decaying velocity and crossfades into the target. It copes better with very different poses and frequent transitions, at the price of damped detail ([Dead Blending](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending); [Unreal node](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending-node-unreal-engine)). Unreal's Motion Matching node can instead run a blend stack that evaluates several animations at once, bounded by Max Active Blends ([Epic docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine)).

Byte-Engine's `PoseInertializer` is a fixed-duration member of Bollo's family. Each node's offset decays by (1−p)³(1+3p+6p²), which by my algebra equals one minus the smootherstep polynomial. A separate term, v·t·e^(−6.9077·t/d), carries the velocity mismatch and leaves one thousandth of it at the end ([inertialization.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/inertialization.rs); [reference](../docs/reference/animation.mdx)). The inertializer can already start from the pose and velocity last displayed (`begin_from_display`), and measuring offsets at the same moment cut a sprint stop's worst per-bone step from 15 cm to about 7 cm ([CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md)). The open question is its behaviour at motion matching's rate of several transitions per second, the regime where Holden favours dead blending for large pose gaps.

### Root reconciliation hides the gap between simulation and animation

Unreal packages Holden's adjust-and-clamp as two nodes. **Offset Root Bone** has halflives, maximum translation and rotation errors, and a clamp to the incoming motion's speed so a near-stationary character does not slide ([FAnimNode_OffsetRootBone](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/AnimationWarpingRuntime/FAnimNode_OffsetRootBone)). **Steering** scales the clip's rotational root motion toward the target facing. Whatever mismatch remains is hidden in the pose. Orientation Warping turns the legs toward the movement direction and counter-twists the spine, Stride Warping scales foot spacing by the ratio of capsule speed to root-motion speed and lowers the pelvis, and Slope Warping handles inclines, although Epic warns against relying on it yet ([Epic: Pose Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/pose-warping-in-unreal-engine)). Playback rate is a bounded correction. *For Honor* capped time scaling at 10% faster and 20% slower and preferred a slight slide that foot IK cleaned up ([Game Anim](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/05/03/motion-matching-ubisofts-honor/)); Naughty Dog allowed ±10–15%, smoothed by a spring ([Naughty Dog deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)). Distance matching and Motion Warping solve a different problem, hitting a known stop point or ledge, and matching systems use them for montage-style actions ([Epic: Distance Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/distance-matching-in-unreal-engine); [Epic: Motion Warping](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine)).

### Foot locking is the last line of defence

Holden labels toe contacts offline: a toe is in contact when its speed is below 0.15 m/s, a six-frame majority filter cleans the labels, and labels are computed after mirroring. At runtime the contact point is locked through its own inertializer, so locking and release blend smoothly; the lock releases beyond 0.2 m, and closed-form two-joint IK pins the leg ([generate_database.py in Motion-Matching](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching); [Simple Two Joint IK](https://theorangeduck.com/page/simple-two-joint)). He treats foot locking as a last resort, because a small slide looks better than a distorted pose ([Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)). The sources agree on an order of operations: search and transition first, then inertialization (which Unreal says must precede additives), root reconciliation, pose warping, layered blends and additives, look-at, foot locking and IK, and finally any physics layer ([Epic: blend nodes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprint-blend-nodes-in-unreal-engine); [controller.cpp](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/blob/main/controller.cpp)). Byte-Engine already has the inertialization step and a look chain with yaw and pitch limits ([look.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/graph/runtime/player/look.rs)). It has no contact data, two-bone IK, warping, or foot locking.

## Neural successors compress motion matching rather than replace it

Neural kinematic controllers have passed through three generations. The first was small CPU networks: PFNN (2017), Mode-Adaptive Neural Networks (2018), Neural State Machine (2019), and Local Motion Phases (2020), with PFNN's authors claiming a few megabytes of memory and a few milliseconds per frame ([PFNN page](https://theorangeduck.com/page/phase-functioned-neural-networks-character-control)). The second learned phase manifolds and codebooks: DeepPhase (2022) and Categorical Codebook Matching (2024). The third is diffusion. A-MDM (2024) costs about 21 ms per frame at 40 steps on an RTX 4090 ([A-MDM](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.00416v3)), CAMDM (2024) costs 13 ms at 8 steps ([CAMDM](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15121v1)), and DART (ICLR 2025) generates 334 frames per second for a single sequence on a 4090 ([DART](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05260v3)). CAMDM's quality lead over a motion matching baseline on 100STYLE (FID 0.913 against 3.455) comes with a caveat: the baseline covered only 10 of the 100 styles because of its memory use ([CAMDM](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15121v1)). The fair reading is that motion matching's memory scales badly across many styles, not that it looks worse on a curated database. NVIDIA's MotionBricks (SIGGRAPH 2026) claims 2 ms latency on unspecified hardware, and its own authors concede that traditional methods still dominate real-time interactive control ([MotionBricks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24833)). Licensing limits reuse further: Starke's AI4Animation code, which covers PFNN through Codebook Matching, is restricted to research and education ([AI4Animation](https://github.com/sebastianstarke/AI4Animation)).

**Learned Motion Matching** is the one neural method designed to keep classic behaviour. A decompressor turns features into a pose, a stepper advances the features each frame, and a projector replaces the search. On Ubisoft's large test controller (47 extra hand joints and about 30 styles), memory fell from **590 MB to 17 MB, or 8.5 MB with 16-bit weights**, and animators kept their workflow ([Ubisoft La Forge](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/laforge/news/6xXL85Q3bF2vEj76xmnmIu/introducing-learned-motion-matching)). The saving moves cost onto the CPU. A 2023 MIG follow-up states that LMM raised runtime cost, and claims an 8.5-fold CPU saving over it for large character counts ([MIG 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3623264.3624442)). A hobbyist test on two minutes of mocap measured the exact search at 0.027 ms against 0.26 ms for the networks ([monjar study](https://github.com/monjar/Advanced-Procedural-Animations-Study/pull/5)). From the layer sizes in Holden's training scripts I estimate about 0.83 million parameters for the projector, 0.32 million for the stepper, and 0.2 million for the decompressor ([train_projector.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/main/resources/train_projector.py)), which is small enough to hand-write in Rust without an ML framework. For an engine whose reference benchmark is a 1,024-character crowd, though, LMM trades in the wrong direction until memory, not CPU time, becomes the limit.

Neural methods have shipped only in narrow forms. Tencent's MotorNerve combines motion matching with LMM ([GDC 2024](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/motornervea-character-animation-system-using-machine-learning-presented-by-tencent-games/903481)). EA's HyperMotion is a proprietary model trained on 8.7 million frames of match capture ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/ea-uses-machine-learning-for-animations-in-fifa-22)). *ARC Raiders* uses reinforcement-learned physics locomotion for its robot enemies, not its humans ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20259/nexon-reveals-arc-raiders-reboot-story-at-gdc-2026-after-massive-aaa-reset)). LIGHTSPEED STUDIOS simulates secondary skeletal physics with a network that costs 0.02–0.13 ms on one CPU core ([GDC 2026](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/softmax-neural-simulation-of-skeletal-physics/915222)). Physics controllers build on motion matching rather than replacing it: DReCon and SuperTrack train a physics character to track a kinematic motion matching controller, so building motion matching first is also the prerequisite for any later physics layer ([DReCon](https://montreal.ubisoft.com/en/drecon-data-driven-responsive-control-of-physics-based-characters/); [SuperTrack](https://theorangeduck.com/page/supertrack-motion-tracking-physically-simulated-characters-using-supervised-learning)). Training such controllers is expensive; ASE's low-level policy needed more than 10 billion samples, about 10 days on one V100 ([ASE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.01906)). Academic work since 2021 mostly adds channels or costs to the classic search: predicted body orientation for VR avatars ([MMVR](https://diglib.eg.org/items/217984e0-450a-4d1d-8dba-c9b701ea88a8/full)), learned phase features that work even across species ([DeepPhase](https://i.cs.hku.hk/~taku/deepphase.pdf); [WalkTheDog](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18946)), and **Environment-aware Motion Matching** (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025), which penalizes collisions with other agents and obstacles inside the search so pose and trajectory adapt together in crowds ([arXiv 2510.22632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22632)). That last paper is the academic work closest to Byte-Engine's crowd-steering effort, and its Unity code is MIT-licensed ([repository](https://github.com/UPC-ViRVIG/Environment-aware-Motion-Matching)).

## Unreal alone productized it, and no Rust crate exists

**Unreal Engine is the only general-purpose engine with first-party, production-ready motion matching.** The PoseSearch plugin existed by May 2023 ([jiahaoli.org](https://jiahaoli.org/blog/2023/05/16/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine-53-the-plugin-pose-search-in-unreal-engine/)). Fortnite adopted it in December 2023, and it became production-ready in UE 5.4 in April 2024 ([CG Press](https://cgpress.org/archives/unreal-engine-5-4-released.html)). Epic released the Game Animation Sample (GASP), with more than 500 animations, in June 2024 ([GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/epic-games-release-500-free-aaa-quality-game-animations/)). CD Projekt Red's June 2025 Witcher 4 tech demo showed multi-character motion matching between Ciri and her horse ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2025/06/03/get-a-new-look-at-the-witcher-iv-in-unreal-engine-technical-showcase)). UE 5.7 (November 2025) moved the sample onto the experimental Mover plugin and added a Pose Search column to Choosers. UE 5.8 (June 2026) made databases created for Chooser columns default to brute-force search, because the KD-tree usually fails under external filtering, and added an experimental multi-character search, `MotionMatchMulti` ([UE 5.8 release notes](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/unreal-engine-5-8-release-notes?lang=en-US)). Epic's tooling sets the bar: the Rewind Debugger shows every candidate's cost broken down by channel as a heat map, with the chosen pose and trajectory drawn in the viewport ([Epic debugging docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-debugging-in-unreal-engine)).

The rest of the market thinned out once Epic's version arrived free. Unity has nothing built in. Motion Symphony's documentation now says the plugin is free and supported no longer ([Motion Symphony FAQ](https://www.wikiful.com/@AnimationUprising/motion-symphony/f-a-q)), and MxM's author left the industry and published the package at no cost ([MxM](https://github.com/Animation-Uprising/Motion-Matching-for-Unity)). Commercial activity moved to data: Motorica sells generated datasets of more than 40 minutes organized for Unreal's Pose Search ([Motorica](https://docs.motorica.com/documentation/integrations/unreal-engine)). O3DE, an open-source engine, shipped motion matching as an experimental gem in release 22.05 (May 2022) ([O3DE blog](https://docs.o3de.org/blog/posts/blog-motionmatching/)).

### Bounding boxes are the proven exact accelerator

Holden stores per-dimension minima and maxima for blocks of 16 and 64 consecutive frames, about 16% extra storage. The search skips any block whose lower bound already exceeds the best cost, and stops accumulating a frame's cost once the partial sum passes the best ([database.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching/main/database.h)). Because animation is smooth, short time windows have tight bounds without any tree build, and in the thesis measurements the boxes cut search time 7–18 times below the best linear scan. Tree-based engines treat their trees as approximate broad phases. Unreal projects poses onto a few principal components, queries a KD-tree, and re-ranks the nearest candidates at full cost. O3DE's KD-tree returns a single leaf of at least 1,000 frames without backtracking, so it can miss the true nearest frame ([O3DE KdTree.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/o3de/o3de/development/Gems/MotionMatching/Code/Source/KdTree.cpp)). No source documents HNSW, product quantization, or LSH in production motion matching beyond EraEngine's HNSW module. Unreal's 5.8 switch to brute force for filtered Chooser databases is the production lesson: tag and context filters break spatial indexes, so a design built on per-query filters needs a fast scan.

Holden folds the weights into the normalization scale, so any weight change means regenerating the database ([README](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching)). There is an exact alternative, which is my own derivation rather than a published technique. If the block bounds are stored in unweighted normalized space and the query applies non-negative per-dimension weights when it computes its distance to a box, each weighted per-axis box term stays a lower bound on the weighted point term. Runtime weight tuning then costs pruning efficiency but never correctness. The same holds for an axis-aligned KD-tree with backtracking, but not for PCA, VP-trees, or HNSW graphs, whose structure depends on the metric they were built with.

### Memory layout and Rust crates

Every public implementation stores features row by row, one pose's floats together, which suits the per-pose early exit. A blocked layout that interleaves each 16-frame box's dimensions would let a surviving box be scored eight poses at a time with `f32x8`; no source reports this layout, so it is an experiment to benchmark rather than established practice. The Rust crates offer building blocks rather than a fit. Kiddo has SIMD and f16 support but targets fewer than about 10 dimensions, and its distance metrics carry no state, so weights would have to be baked into coordinates ([kiddo](https://docs.rs/kiddo/latest/kiddo/)). `usearch` accepts stateful metrics and filter predicates but is approximate ([usearch](https://docs.rs/usearch/latest/usearch/)), and `vpsearch` requires a true metric rather than squared distance ([vpsearch](https://docs.rs/vpsearch/latest/vpsearch/)). Holden's bounding boxes need no crate at all.

### Crowds survive by searching less often

Crowd-scale motion matching relies on searching every 5–10 frames, staggering when each character searches, restricting the database by context, and updating distant characters less often. *For Honor* ran more than 100 on-screen characters through aggressive LOD ([Game Anim](https://www.gameanim.com/2016/05/03/motion-matching-ubisofts-honor/)). The Unity thesis estimates more than 300 characters within 1 ms when each searches every ten frames ([Ponton thesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05215)). Environment-aware MM found that search-time variance grows with database size and proposed a search capped by a time budget ([arXiv 2510.22632](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22632)). No public source reports thousands of motion-matched characters on the CPU. Traverse's GPU search sidesteps CPU cost, but gameplay can no longer step through the decision on the CPU ([Traverse](https://traverse.nl/blog/motion-matching-on-the-gpu)), and because Byte-Engine hands root motion to gameplay every step, a GPU search would add readback latency.

### Measured against Byte-Engine's own numbers

On an M4, one walking character costs about **1.8 µs per update**, and 1,024 walkers cost **0.57 ms** across the performance-core lanes ([CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md); [develop note](../docs/develop/animation.mdx)). A pruned search at 30–90 µs therefore costs 17–50 times an entire walking character's update; the hardware differs between these figures, so the ratio is an order of magnitude, not a measurement. For the crowd, assume 60 updates per second and staggered searches at 10 Hz. About 171 of the 1,024 characters then search on each update, which at 30–90 µs apiece is 5–15 ms of lane time, or 1.3–3.8 ms across four lanes: 2–7 times today's cost for the whole crowd. A single player character searching every 0.1 s, by contrast, averages 5–15 µs per update.

Memory is the less obvious problem. Byte-Engine stores every key as an uncompressed f32, so a 64-node clip with all channels keyed at 30 fps takes about 77 KB per second of motion ([packed.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/packed.rs)). Ten minutes of database clips would need about 46 MB, more than the clip pool's default 32 MiB budget ([system.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/system.rs)), and Naughty Dog's 2 h 17 min player set would need about 630 MB at the same rate. The features themselves are small: ten minutes at 30 Hz × 27 floats is about 1.9 MB. **Pose storage, not features, dominates**, and because any frame can win a search, the pool's least-recently-sampled eviction conflicts with matching. Database clips must stay resident, and key compression becomes a prerequisite at scale.

## Byte-Engine supplies the back end and lacks every matching piece

### The pipeline today

All skeletal animation lives in about 9,900 lines under `crates/byte-engine/src/animation` and runs on the CPU from import to GPU skinning. FBX and glTF clips bake to rkyv-archived curves of uncompressed f32 keys ([fbx/skeleton.rs](../crates/resource-management/src/asset/handler/implementations/fbx/skeleton.rs)). A load worker packs each clip into a pooled arena ([runtime.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/graph/runtime.rs)). A code-built state machine samples up to three clips per state through 1D and 2D blend spaces, then blends, inertializes, extracts root motion from the root chain, applies the look chain, and posts global matrices for GPU skinning ([graph.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/graph.rs); [docs/develop/animation.mdx](../docs/develop/animation.mdx)). Players advance through `alley.for_each_mut`, 16 to a lane, on performance cores with thread-local scratch. The engine's documented rules forbid allocation after a player's first frame and require results that do not depend on lane count ([player.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/graph/runtime/player.rs); [docs/develop/animation.mdx](../docs/develop/animation.mdx)).

### The sandbox already does pose matching by hand

The third-person sandbox's stop-frame tables were measured by comparing each stop's first pose with every frame of its loop, which cut the gap at entry for sprint stops from up to 63 cm to 9–37 cm ([third_person/src/animation.rs](/Users/facundovilla/development/sandbox/third_person/src/animation.rs)). That is motion matching's pose term computed offline by hand, and the remaining 9–37 cm shows the coverage gaps any database built from these clips would inherit. The sandbox also shows the graph motion matching would replace: 104 distinct clips, a planar blend of an eight-clip walk ring and an eight-clip sprint ring, twin states, fitted stop routes, and, by the changelog's count, more than a thousand routes before `any_state_to` existed ([CHANGELOG](../CHANGELOG.md)). Its `Motion` easing already works as an implicit trajectory model, easing direction at a response of 8 and speed at 3 and turning around past 135° with a 150 ms inertialized jump ([character.rs](/Users/facundovilla/development/sandbox/third_person/src/animation/character.rs)).

### Locomotion is driven by root motion

The engine has no character controller. Kinematic bodies behave like static ones, there are no shape casts, and ray queries answer one step late ([docs/reference/physics.mdx](../docs/reference/physics.mdx)). Characters are static capsules that root motion moves, and gameplay, not the animation system, applies that motion ([system.rs](../crates/byte-engine/src/animation/system.rs)). NPCs replan every 0.1 s, pick a gait from path length alone, turn no faster than the lesser of 10 m/s² divided by speed and 150° per second, and call `Navigation::steer`, while root motion produces their actual displacement ([npc.rs](/Users/facundovilla/development/sandbox/third_person/src/npc.rs); [docs/use/navigation.mdx](../docs/use/navigation.mdx)). Commit `e7e6b05d8` removed the steering response time that had modelled root-motion lag, so the gap between the steered velocity and the velocity root motion achieves now goes unmodelled.

### A layered design

A design that follows AGENTS.md's rules on pure functions, separate layers, and no transient allocation builds up in seven layers ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).

**Bake.** `resource-management` builds a pose database from a list of clips named in a `.bead` sidecar. It resamples on the database's own clock, which also removes the glTF frame-rate problem, and computes Holden's 27 root-relative floats as baseline channels, with room for Naughty Dog's past-trajectory, spine-facing, and turn-speed channels. It stores per-group normalization statistics, per-frame contact bits, tagged frame ranges (exclude, block entry, cost bias), optional mirrored copies, and 16- and 64-frame bounds as an aligned binary payload rather than rkyv metadata.

**Search.** A pure function maps the query, the database view, filters and biases, and the current frame to the best frame and its per-channel costs. It scans by brute force with bounding-box pruning in `f32x8`, with the anti-shredding contract built in from the start: the current frame as the bar, a continuation bias, an exclusion window, a reselect history, unpickable tails, and throttling. Staggering searches by character index keeps results independent of lane count.

**Trajectory.** A gameplay-side spring predicts the path from `CharacterIntent` for the player, and from steering plus the path polyline for NPCs, with separate acceleration and deceleration springs, a turn-rate limit, and parameters fitted to the database's measured speeds. The sandbox's 6.5 m/s NPC sprint is well above the roughly 4 m/s run peak Holden measured in LaFAN1, so speeds must come from whatever data ships. NPCs keep a horizon of 1.2 s or less and slow down before corners. The animation request carries a small fixed-size trajectory, which keeps intact the rule that the animation system never moves the entity.

**Graph integration.** A matched motion variant runs inside a graph state, following Epic's hybrid. Client code already selects states with ordinary Rust control flow, which plays the role of Unreal's Choosers, and jumps, deaths, and other one-shots stay routes.

**Transitions.** The search's switches reuse `PoseInertializer` through `begin_from_display`. The first thing to verify is the untraced question of how it handles jumps to arbitrary mid-clip frames.

**Root reconciliation.** A gameplay-owned simulation point plus pure adjust and clamp functions run in the second task poll, after `animation.step` ([game.rs](/Users/facundovilla/development/sandbox/third_person/src/game.rs)). Because navigation estimates an agent's velocity from its displacement, this puts the steering-versus-root-motion gap, which the removed response time used to model, in one bounded place.

**Feet.** Foot contacts and two-bone IK come later, starting on flat ground.

Following the project's convention, the behaviour should first be specified in `docs/reference`, then given a design note and a Use page ([memory index](/Users/facundovilla/.claude/projects/-Users-facundovilla-development-Byte-Engine/memory/MEMORY.md)). Tests should stay black-box, such as checking that a query built from frame k of a clip finds frame k. Debug views should draw the predicted and matched trajectories as lines, the way Naughty Dog drew the motion model in blue and the animation in red ([Naughty Dog deck](https://s3.amazonaws.com/nd.images/research/tlou2/motion_matching_in_tlou2_nd_final.pptx)).

### Read the fine print before using the best sets

**LaFAN1 has the best quality**, and Holden's demo uses it. Its NoDerivatives term forbids sharing adapted material, yet Holden's MIT repository commits a processed database of about 64 MB built from it ([Motion-Matching README](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching)). Byte-Engine should not copy that pattern, and should instead generate LaFAN1 databases locally from each user's own download for non-commercial benchmarking only. **100STYLE is the strongest permissive set, with two cautions.** A single actor performed it in an IMU suit, and a community authoring guide warns that IMU capture suits motion matching poorly because its global root position is inaccurate ([MxM authoring guide](https://motion-matching-for-unity.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Motion%20Matching%20-%20Guide%20for%20Animation%20Authoring.pdf)); its coverage of starts, stops, and pivots is also undocumented. **O3DE's 22 clips** are the only permissively licensed set captured specifically for motion matching, and the Godot plugin already reuses them ([Godot README](https://github.com/GuilhermeGSousa/godot-motion-matching)), though their total length was not checked. **Mixamo** allows use in games, and an Adobe community FAQ, which is not the binding terms, adds that raw files may not be redistributed and that the content may not train machine-learning models ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/mixamo-discussions/mixamo-faq-licensing-royalties-ownership-eula-and-tos/m-p/13234775)). The licence of the sandbox's MotusMan clips was not examined, and it needs checking before any database built from them enters the repository.

### The Game Animation Sample's license is reported two ways

In **June 2024**, CG Channel reported that the sample's assets were licensed for use only with Unreal Engine, with commercial projects allowed ([CG Channel, June 2024](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/06/get-500-free-game-ready-animations-for-use-in-unreal-engine)). The **October 2026** record reads differently. The current Fab Standard License summary says assets may be used with any compatible tools, not only Unreal, but never redistributed on a standalone basis ([Fab license summary](https://www.fab.com/eula)). An archived snapshot of the Fab listing from 8 October 2026 shows free Personal and Professional licenses and an AI-forbidden flag, with no UE-only marker ([Fab listing](https://www.fab.com/listings/880e319a-a59e-4ed2-b268-b32dac7fa016)). Epic's Content License Agreement (snapshot of 12 October 2026) does define a UE-only content category, and it forbids using NoAI content as training data for generative AI programs ([Epic Content License](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/eula/content)). Whether an LMM-style training run counts as a generative AI program is unclear. Under either reading, the sample's data cannot ship with Byte-Engine.

### A first capture plan

Ubisoft's first test used 10–15 minutes of untouched data ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/ubisoft-introduces-motion-matching-the-first-step-to-next-gen-animation/)). Holden's demo used about 8.3 unique minutes, about 16.5 with mirroring, resampled to 60 Hz and sped up 10% ([generate_database.py in Motion-Matching](https://github.com/orangeduck/Motion-Matching); [Code vs Data Driven Displacement](https://theorangeduck.com/page/code-vs-data-driven-displacement)). That points to a first playable set of roughly 8–15 unique minutes per character, captured as short dance-card phases for each gait, starting and ending each take standing still, and preferably captured optically. Mirroring should be a per-database option, because Zadziuk's team could not make it work in 2016 ([Zadziuk notes](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Zadziuk_Kristjan_MotionMatchingFutureOfGamesAnimation.pdf)).

### Code licenses split the same way as data

Code under MIT (Holden's repositories, tomcatmew, JLPM22, Environment-aware MM, the Godot projects, Esoterica, and dreaw131313) or under Apache-2.0 and dual licenses (O3DE, EraEngine, and the Bevy prototype) can be ported with attribution. Code under the Epic EULA (Unreal Pose Search and GASP), the Unity Companion License (Kinematica), CC BY-NC-SA (MMVR), or AI4Animation's research-and-education terms, and any repository with no license file, can be studied but never copied. **MxM is the case where the sources disagree.** The commercial survey calls it free and open source, and the Frost-Blade community fork's documentation claims MIT. The repository check found that the original Animation-Uprising repository has no LICENSE file: GitHub's license API returns 404, and the README speaks only of price and availability. Under GitHub's own guidance, a repository without a license keeps all rights with its creator, and a fork cannot grant rights the author never granted ([MxM repository](https://github.com/Animation-Uprising/Motion-Matching-for-Unity); [GitHub licensing docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository); [Frost-Blade fork](https://github.com/Frost-Blade-Studios/Motion-Matching-for-Unity)). The repository-verified reading should prevail: treat MxM, and Motion Symphony likewise, as all rights reserved.

### What the Epic EULA allows

The Unreal EULA (snapshot of 5 October 2026) licenses engine code for private use and modification ([Unreal EULA](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/eula/unreal); [Wayback snapshot](https://web.archive.org/web/20261005183952/https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/eula/unreal)). Source code may be distributed only to other licensees of the same engine version, and public distribution must go through Fab or a fork inside Epic's GitHub network. Snippets of up to 30 lines may be posted only to discuss them, never to rebuild a larger part, and the licensed technology may not be combined with copyleft code or used as training input for generative AI. Translating Pose Search into Rust and publishing it in an MIT repository would fall outside every one of those channels. The EULA governs code and content, not the algorithm described in public talks and papers. The Unreal defaults in this report's first table were read privately from the source and are facts, not code; if this report is committed, later notes should stay at that level. Risk management, not legal advice, suggests a clean-room process: write Byte-Engine's implementation from Holden, O3DE, and Epic's public documentation, and have anyone who has read Epic's source avoid transcribing its structure.

### Engineering observations, not freedom-to-operate opinions

**Unity's US 10,888,785 is the first to show an attorney.** Its claim describes a runtime that keeps a weighted stack of active poses and builds the output pose by blending them. A design with a single playing pose, where an inertialized offset hides each switch and decays to nothing, is structurally different from that element, and that is Byte-Engine's existing approach. An Unreal-style blend stack, or a matched state that blends several database poses by weight, would come closer. Two facts may bear on validity, the inventor's public July 2015 presentation of motion matching and the patent's June 2018 priority date, but only a court or the Patent Trial and Appeal Board can weigh them. **EA's US 10,388,053 is the second.** Its March 2015 provisional filing predates the Ubisoft talks, so those talks do not come before it. Its elements resemble the hybrid this report recommends: a gameplay trigger picks rule-eligible start frames filtered by context, and features choose among them, which is the pattern of EA's Match Node and of a graph route gated by matching. The attorney should review that exact design. EA's phase patents matter only if Starke-style phase channels are added, Cygames' pending claim resembles choosing schema channels per state and is worth tracking until the US decides it, and because patents are territorial, US, EP, and JP numbers apply to sales in their own markets.

## Conclusion

For Byte-Engine the question is no longer whether motion matching is feasible but what it costs, and the engine's earlier work moves almost all of that cost into baking, data, and memory. Inertialization from the displayed pose, root-chain sampling, and lane-parallel crowds already exist, and the search is a few hundred lines of bounded SIMD. What the engine lacks is a bake step that turns clips into per-frame features, a clip store that never evicts a candidate frame, and the curated, licensable minutes of data that every production says decide final quality. Three pressures point to the same design: the per-character budget, Unreal 5.8's lesson that filters break trees, and patent claims that hinge on blending several active poses. That design is a single playing pose with inertialized switches, an exact bounding-box scan with per-query tag masks, and throttled, staggered searches inside an ordinary graph state, so here the simplest design is also the lowest-risk one.

Two consequences reach beyond animation. Motion matching breaks the clip pool's assumption that rarely used clips can be evicted, because any frame can win a search; key compression and pinned residency therefore become animation work rather than an optimization. And a gameplay-owned simulation point with a bounded clamp would give navigation back a velocity it can trust, without reintroducing the response-time model the steering rewrite removed. A sensible order is to prove the system on the third-person player first, using the O3DE clips and the existing cut clips, with a `characters_motion_matching` benchmark beside `characters_locomotion`. NPC crowds should follow later behind search LOD, once measured per-search cost confirms this report's estimates.
